Today’s SME cyber look-out: stealth phishing plus Microsoft 365 email/Teams disruption

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Friday 04 September 2026, 17:20 UK time
Today’s look-out: Phishing designed to bypass filters + cloud comms disruption scams

What to look out for today

Two practical risks for SMEs today:

  • High-volume phishing emails designed to evade filters using invisible Unicode characters, often with financial lures.
  • Microsoft 365 disruption: Exchange Online email delays/errors and some users unable to open the Teams desktop client—creating confusion that criminals can exploit with “fix your account” messages.

Also in the background: new reporting highlights that passkeys reduce password attacks but can still be undermined through prompt fatigue, recovery/enrolment tricks and social engineering.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Finance teams are prime targets for phishing that looks like routine payment, invoice, funding or bank admin.
  • When email or Teams is flaky, staff are more likely to accept “helpdesk” instructions, click alternative links, or switch to personal email/WhatsApp—raising fraud and data leakage risk.
  • Passkeys aren’t a magic shield: if staff approve the wrong prompt or fall for a fake recovery/enrolment flow, attackers can still get in—especially to cloud apps.

Warning signs

  • Emails that look normal but have odd spacing, missing/strange words, or inconsistent subject lines (sometimes caused by hidden characters).
  • “Microsoft/IT Support” messages claiming your mailbox is stuck, Teams is being upgraded, or you must re-authenticate due to an outage.
  • Unexpected requests to click Allow, approve a sign-in, or “confirm” a new device/app.
  • Payment change requests arriving during comms disruption: new bank details, urgent same-day transfers, or pressure to bypass normal approval.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Filter evasion phishing: hidden Unicode characters can help emails slip past keyword-based scanning, increasing the chance they land in inboxes.
  • Outage-themed impersonation: attackers send “service incident” emails/SMS/calls pretending to be Microsoft or your IT provider, directing staff to fake sign-in pages or to approve prompts.
  • OAuth / consent tricks: “connect this trusted app to fix mail/Teams” may actually grant an attacker access to mailboxes or files.
  • Passkey prompt abuse: repeated sign-in prompts to wear users down into approving the wrong one, or guiding staff into a fake recovery/enrolment journey.

What to do today

  • Brief staff (2 minutes): if you receive an email about mail/Teams problems, don’t use embedded links—go via your normal Microsoft 365 sign-in/bookmarks or ask IT via a known channel.
  • Finance control: enforce “no bank detail changes by email alone”; use a call-back to a known number and require a second approver for urgent payments.
  • Prompt discipline: instruct staff to deny unexpected sign-in prompts/approvals and report them immediately.
  • Alternative comms plan: if Exchange/Teams is degraded, use a pre-agreed backup channel (and remind staff not to switch to personal accounts for customer or sensitive data).

Ask your IT provider

  • Are we seeing an uptick in phishing getting through today (especially finance-themed), and are our controls flagging Unicode/obfuscated content?
  • Do we have conditional access and alerting for unusual logins, new device enrolments, and new app/OAuth consents?
  • What is our process for Microsoft 365 incidents—how will staff be informed, and what’s the approved workaround?
  • Can you confirm our payment-change verification process is documented and communicated to finance/admin teams?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

There’s reporting of a new CrowdStrike Falcon privilege-escalation zero-day; for most SMEs the key action is operational: ask your IT provider/MSP whether you use CrowdStrike, whether any mitigations or configuration changes are recommended, and how they’re monitoring for suspicious local privilege escalation attempts—avoid ad-hoc changes without guidance.

One action today

Send a same-day staff note: “If you get an email/call about Microsoft 365/Teams issues or asking you to ‘re-authenticate’ or ‘click Allow’, do not use the link—report it to IT and sign in only via your saved Microsoft 365 bookmark.”
