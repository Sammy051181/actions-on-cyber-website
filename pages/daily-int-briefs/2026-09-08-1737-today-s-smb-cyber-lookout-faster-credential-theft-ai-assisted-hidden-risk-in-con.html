Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: faster credential theft (AI‑assisted) + hidden risk in connected apps

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Tuesday 08 September 2026, 17:37 UK time
Today’s look-out: Credential harvesting, shadow AI and SaaS/Workspace connected-app risk

What to look out for today

Be on the lookout for a spike in credential-harvesting attempts (fake Microsoft 365/Google sign-ins, “shared document” lures, “account security alert” messages), with attackers increasingly using AI-driven automation to move faster. Also treat connected third-party apps (especially in Google Workspace) and unapproved “shadow AI” tools as a practical, near-term risk.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Speed: Threat actors can run large-scale credential theft campaigns very quickly, shrinking the time you have to spot and stop it.
  • Knock-on impact: A single mailbox or cloud account takeover can lead to invoice fraud, payroll changes, data leaks, or compromised supplier/customer communications.
  • Hidden access: Old or forgotten third-party integrations can keep access to mail, files, contacts or calendars long after staff stop using them.
  • AI adds new data routes: If staff connect email or documents to AI tools (approved or not), sensitive data may be exposed in ways your normal controls don’t cover.

Warning signs

  • Staff report unexpected sign-in prompts, MFA codes they didn’t request, or repeated “session expired” messages.
  • New mailbox rules or forwarding set up (e.g., copies of invoices sent to an external address).
  • Unfamiliar “connected apps” granted access to Google Workspace or other SaaS tools.
  • Sudden changes to payment details or “urgent” requests arriving via email threads that normally look trustworthy.
  • Employees using new AI tools for “quick help” with documents, HR, contracts, or finance—especially if they’ve linked accounts or uploaded files.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • AI-assisted phishing at scale: More convincing, better-targeted messages, produced faster and sent in higher volumes.
  • Credential harvesting: Fake login pages to capture passwords and MFA tokens, followed by rapid takeover of mailboxes and cloud storage.
  • Abusing OAuth/third-party access: Attackers don’t always need your password if they can trick someone into granting an app access to mail/files.
  • “Shadow AI” leakage: Staff paste sensitive text into unapproved tools, or connect business accounts, creating new exposure and audit gaps.
  • AI tool risk & disruption: If your teams rely on AI services for work, outages or security issues can disrupt operations and increase risky workarounds.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-minute staff note: “Don’t re-enter passwords from links; go to the site/app directly. Report repeated sign-in prompts and unexpected MFA codes immediately.”
  • Check for mailbox tampering: Review forwarding, rules, and suspicious logins on key accounts (finance, payroll, directors, shared mailboxes).
  • Review connected apps: In Google Workspace/M365, list third-party apps with access to mail/files and remove anything not clearly required.
  • Decide your AI stance: What’s approved, what’s banned, what data must never be pasted/uploaded, and whether account connections are allowed.
  • Reinforce MFA: Ensure MFA is on for all staff; pay extra attention to finance/admin accounts and any accounts used for supplier payments.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you show us a report of connected third-party apps with access to our email and files, and confirm what’s been removed?
  • What monitoring is in place for impossible travel, repeated failed logins, new mailbox rules/forwarding, and suspicious OAuth consents?
  • How quickly can you contain an account takeover (disable sessions, reset tokens, remove rules, check for lateral movement)?
  • Do we have an agreed policy for AI tools (approved list, data handling rules, and whether staff can connect corporate accounts)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you run your own identity services (e.g., Linux domain/identity platforms such as FreeIPA), ask your IT team to confirm they’re tracking today’s reporting on an administrator-credential risk chain and that your identity systems are covered by monitoring and rapid change control. For most SMEs using managed cloud identity (Microsoft/Google), today’s bigger practical risk remains credential theft and risky third-party access rather than niche server patching.

One action today

Review and remove unnecessary third‑party apps connected to your email/files (Google Workspace/M365), prioritising finance and shared mailboxes.
