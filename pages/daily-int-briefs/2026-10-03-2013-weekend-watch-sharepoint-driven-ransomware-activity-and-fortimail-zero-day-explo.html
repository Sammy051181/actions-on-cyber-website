Weekend watch: SharePoint-driven ransomware activity and FortiMail zero-day exploitation

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Saturday 03 October 2026, 20:13 UK time
Today’s look-out: Ransomware entry via internet-facing services + follow-on phishing using breach and IT reset themes

What to look out for today

Two themes to brief your team on today:

  • Ransomware and disruption linked to internet-facing Microsoft SharePoint (attackers abusing flaws to get in, disable security tools, and deploy ransomware).
  • Fortinet FortiMail being actively exploited as a zero-day (if you run FortiMail, assume it’s a priority check).

Also note: a university identity/access system breach shows how stolen identity data can fuel phishing, password reset scams, and account takeover attempts against staff and students.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • SharePoint and email security gateways sit on critical paths: if they’re compromised, attackers can reach files, internal sites, and email flows quickly.
  • SMEs are often downstream: you may not run these systems yourself, but your managed IT provider, hosting partner, school trust, or supplier might.
  • Breach “ripple effects”: even if your organisation wasn’t breached, criminals reuse stolen contact details to make phishing more convincing (e.g., “IT support” password resets, “new login portal”, “invoice overdue”).

Warning signs

  • Unexpected account lockouts, password resets, or MFA changes staff didn’t request.
  • New or unfamiliar admin accounts or permissions changes on Microsoft 365/SharePoint-related services.
  • Staff reporting strange SharePoint prompts, missing documents, or new “security update” messages.
  • Email security changes: unusual mail flow behaviour, delivery delays, or a sudden increase in spam/phishing getting through.
  • Security tooling suddenly disabled or reporting gaps (EDR/AV not checking in).

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Internet-facing service compromise: attackers scan for exposed SharePoint / email gateway services, then use weaknesses to gain access.
  • Disable security tools to reduce the chance of detection, then move fast to encrypt systems (ransomware) or steal data for leverage.
  • Follow-on impersonation: using breached identity data to target staff with “IT helpdesk” and “security alert” messages to capture passwords/MFA codes.

What to do today

  • Confirm ownership: do you (or your MSP) run on-prem SharePoint or FortiMail? If you’re not sure, ask.
  • Tell staff one simple rule for the weekend: treat unexpected password reset/MFA prompts and “IT support” calls/emails as suspicious; verify via known internal contact methods.
  • Check your backups: verify you can restore key file shares and critical systems, and that backup access is protected with MFA.
  • Review admin access: ensure only necessary accounts have admin rights; remove stale accounts; enforce MFA for admins.
  • Monitor for unusual logins: especially from unexpected locations or at unusual times.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have any internet-facing SharePoint (including legacy or test instances), and how are we monitoring it?
  • Do we use Fortinet FortiMail anywhere (including at a hosted provider)? If yes, what’s the mitigation/upgrade status and how are we validating it?
  • Are our EDR/AV tools reporting normally across servers, and do we alert on security tools being disabled?
  • What’s our ransomware recovery plan for the top 3 systems we can’t operate without on Monday morning?
  • Have we recently tested restore from backup (not just that backups exist)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you run Fortinet FortiMail, there is reporting of active exploitation of a zero-day (CVE-2026-104286) and it has been highlighted by authorities and vendors. This is the kind of issue where SMEs should push for same-day confirmation from their IT provider on exposure, remediation status, and monitoring—rather than waiting for the next routine maintenance window.

One action today

Send a short internal note today: “Do not approve unexpected password resets/MFA prompts or ‘IT support’ requests—verify via our known contact method first,” and ask your IT/MSP to confirm whether you have any internet-facing SharePoint or FortiMail in use.
