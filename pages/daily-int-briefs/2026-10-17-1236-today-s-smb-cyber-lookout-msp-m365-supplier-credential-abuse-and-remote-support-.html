Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: MSP/M365 supplier credential abuse and remote support tool risk

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Saturday 17 October 2026, 12:36 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier/MSP compromise leading to Microsoft 365 account takeover and business email risk

What to look out for today

Two themes stand out for SMEs today:

  • Supplier/MSP risk affecting Microsoft 365: attackers are reportedly using a compromised credential linked to an MSP-focused cloud backup product to access customer Microsoft 365 tenants.
  • Remote support access risk: a widely used remote support product has an actively exploited authentication bypass issue (mainly a concern where organisations run a self-hosted appliance).

Why this matters to smaller businesses

Many SMEs rely on an MSP for backup, Microsoft 365 administration, and remote support. If a supplier tool, admin credential, or integration is compromised, attackers may not need to phish your staff first. The impact can be fast and practical: mailbox access, invoice fraud, data theft, and business disruption.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected Microsoft 365 sign-in alerts, especially from unusual locations, new devices, or outside normal business hours.
  • New or changed mailbox rules (e.g., forwarding, deleting, or moving emails about invoices/payments).
  • Unrecognised admin actions: new admin accounts, consent granted to unfamiliar apps, or changes to MFA/security settings.
  • Clients/suppliers reporting odd emails from your domain (payment changes, urgent requests, “re-sent invoice” messages).
  • Remote support sessions you can’t account for, or unexpected prompts about remote access tooling.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Tenant access via supplier credentials/integrations to read mail, steal data, and impersonate staff for payment diversion scams.
  • Persistence in email by creating forwarding rules or adding additional sign-in methods, so they keep access even if a password is changed.
  • Abuse of remote support to obtain initial access and then move around systems, potentially leading to ransomware or data theft.
  • Follow-on phishing: once inside one mailbox, attackers often send convincing internal messages to payroll/finance.

What to do today

  • Check your Microsoft 365 sign-in alerts and recent admin activity (or ask your IT provider to do this) for the past 7–14 days.
  • Review mailbox rules and external forwarding, especially for finance and senior leadership accounts.
  • Re-confirm your payment change process: treat all bank detail changes as suspicious and verify using a known, offline method.
  • Ask your MSP what tools they use for M365 backup/management and whether any supplier incidents affect you.
  • Make sure MFA is on for all users, and use stronger MFA for admins (and separate admin accounts where possible).

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have any dependency on ConnectWise Cloud Backup / SkyKick (or related MSP integrations) for Microsoft 365 access or backup?
  • Which credentials, tokens, or app consents exist between supplier tooling and our Microsoft 365 tenant—and how are they monitored and rotated?
  • Can you provide a recent sign-in and admin activity review for our tenant (including impossible travel, new devices, and risky sign-ins)?
  • Do we run BeyondTrust Remote Support as a self-hosted appliance anywhere? If yes, what’s the status of mitigation/updates and how do we confirm it?
  • What is our rapid containment plan if a mailbox/admin account is suspected compromised (who to call, what gets disabled first, how we protect finance)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

CISA is warning of active exploitation affecting BeyondTrust Remote Support (particularly self-hosted appliances). If your organisation (or your IT provider) uses it, ask for a clear confirmation of your exposure and that the vendor-recommended fix has been applied or the system upgraded as required.

One action today

Ask your IT provider today for a 7–14 day Microsoft 365 sign-in/admin activity review and a check for mailbox forwarding rules, focusing on finance and leadership accounts.
