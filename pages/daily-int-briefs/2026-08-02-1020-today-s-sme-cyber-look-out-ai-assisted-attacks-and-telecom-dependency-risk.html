Today’s SME cyber look-out: AI-assisted attacks and telecom dependency risk

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Sunday 02 August 2026, 10:20 UK time
Today’s look-out: AI-assisted attack automation + telecom/MFA dependency disruption

What to look out for today

Two themes to keep on your radar:

  • AI-assisted “hands-off” attacks: reporting suggests criminals are experimenting with AI/agent tooling to scan for internet-facing systems and attempt compromises with minimal ongoing human input.
  • Telecom dependency risk (4G/5G core issues): new research highlights weaknesses that could, in some scenarios, lead to disruption or session hijacking at the network level. For SMEs, the practical concern is reliability of mobile connectivity and phone-based security steps.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Speed and scale: if attackers can automate more of the “find and try” work, exposed services and weak logins get discovered faster.
  • Business disruption: even short mobile issues can impact card payments on mobile terminals, field teams, deliveries, and remote access.
  • MFA exposure: if your sign-ins rely heavily on SMS/phone signals, any telecom disruption increases the likelihood of lockouts and support calls—and can create cover for social engineering (“I can’t receive the code, can you reset me?”).

Warning signs

  • Sudden spikes in blocked login attempts on Microsoft 365/Google/admin portals.
  • Unexpected password reset requests or “can you disable MFA just for today?” type messages.
  • Unplanned mobile connectivity issues reported by multiple staff at once (especially if it coincides with strange account activity).
  • Helpdesk tickets about users being “locked out” combined with pressure to act quickly or bypass normal checks.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Automated reconnaissance: scanning for exposed remote access, admin panels, or poorly secured web services, then trying common paths to entry.
  • Low-friction social engineering: using disruption (real or claimed) as a reason to request MFA resets, number changes, or urgent access.
  • Targeting internet-facing systems: focusing on whatever is reachable from the outside (including third-party hosted tools), because that’s easiest to find at scale.

What to do today

  • Reconfirm your “no bypass” rule: staff (including IT support/admins) should not disable MFA or reset access without strong identity checks.
  • Review external exposure: make sure you know what services are internet-facing (remote access, admin portals, web apps) and who owns them (internal IT vs MSP vs software supplier).
  • Reduce single points of failure: if critical sign-ins rely on SMS, ensure at least one backup MFA method exists for key roles (e.g., authenticator app or hardware key) and that it’s actually enrolled.
  • Log and alert: ensure you receive alerts for risky sign-in activity (impossible travel, repeated failures, new device sign-ins) for priority accounts.

Ask your IT provider

  • Which of our systems are currently accessible from the internet, and how do we routinely check that list hasn’t grown?
  • What’s our process when a user claims they can’t receive MFA codes—what checks are mandatory before any reset?
  • Do we have sign-in alerts turned on for admin and finance accounts, and who responds out of hours?
  • If mobile service is disrupted, what’s our fallback for critical access (banking, payroll, email admin, remote access)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Nothing here is a “go patch this one product today” item for most SMEs. Treat this as a prompt to reduce exposure of internet-facing services and to validate your MFA and account recovery processes, especially where SMS/mobile reliance is high.

One action today

Send a short internal note today: “No MFA bypasses or urgent access resets without identity checks” and confirm at least one non-SMS MFA backup is enrolled for admin and finance accounts.
