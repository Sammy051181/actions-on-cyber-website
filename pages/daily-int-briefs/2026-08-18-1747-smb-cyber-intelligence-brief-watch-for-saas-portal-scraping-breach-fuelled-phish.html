SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief: Watch for SaaS portal scraping, breach‑fuelled phishing, and code supply‑chain traps

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Tuesday 18 August 2026, 17:47 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier/SaaS data exposure scams and portal scraping

What to look out for today

  • SaaS portal data scraping risk: reports of a long-running campaign pulling records from Salesforce and ServiceNow customer portals across organisations.
  • Breach ripple-effects: a disclosed customer data exposure at hardware wallet maker SafePal may lead to targeted phishing and “we need you to confirm” messages.
  • Software supply-chain trap: typosquatted RubyGems packages published to steal browser credentials and crypto wallet data—relevant if you have in-house developers or use agencies that build/maintain your systems.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs rely heavily on cloud services (CRM, ticketing, HR, finance and customer support). If attackers can quietly pull data from portals, the impact is often felt as:

  • Account takeover (email, CRM, support desk) leading to invoice fraud or customer impersonation.
  • Customer and staff phishing that looks believable because it uses real names, ticket references, delivery addresses or purchase details.
  • Supply-chain exposure where a developer dependency or plugin brings malware into a build process or admin workstation.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected data export activity in CRM/helpdesk tools, or users reporting missing/changed records they didn’t touch.
  • Unusual login prompts, repeated MFA requests, or sign-ins from unfamiliar locations for Salesforce/ServiceNow accounts.
  • Staff or customers receiving emails/SMS claiming to be from a supplier asking to “confirm details”, reset passwords, or re-verify orders (especially where the message includes accurate personal details).
  • Developer/IT team mentions “quick gem update” or new dependencies that aren’t in normal change control.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Portal scraping → targeted phishing: attackers harvest names, emails, ticket subjects, and organisational context to craft believable messages (e.g., “ticket update”, “case closed”, “new invoice attached”).
  • Breach data → impersonation: exposed customer contact/shipping details can be used to convince recipients that an email is genuine and push them to click login links or approve payments.
  • Typosquatting → credential theft: a small naming mistake in a software package can lead to malware on a developer machine, then onward access to business systems via saved browser sessions and passwords.

What to do today

  • Lock down SaaS portals: review who has access to Salesforce/ServiceNow portals; remove dormant accounts; ensure MFA is enforced for all users (including admins and contractors).
  • Turn up monitoring: check sign-in logs and alerting for unusual locations, impossible travel, mass record reads/exports, and repeated failed logins.
  • Brief staff (10 minutes): warn that “support ticket updates”, “order issues”, and “account verification” emails may be more convincing right now; reinforce checking requests via a known channel.
  • If you use developers/agency support: ask what controls they have to prevent typosquatting/dependency confusion and how they review new packages before use.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have conditional access/MFA enforced for Salesforce/ServiceNow and admin accounts, including third parties?
  • What alerts do we have for bulk exports, unusual API usage, or atypical portal access patterns?
  • Can you confirm our least-privilege approach for CRM/helpdesk roles and portal users (including old staff and contractors)?
  • For any software development you manage: how do you approve dependencies (e.g., RubyGems) and detect typosquatted packages?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Microsoft is continuing to change Windows built-in tooling (including removing WMIC from newer Windows 11 builds). This isn’t an emergency patch item for most SMEs today, but it’s a prompt to ensure your IT provider isn’t relying on legacy tools for support scripts and that endpoint monitoring remains effective as Windows features evolve.

One action today

Review Salesforce/ServiceNow access today: remove dormant/third‑party accounts you no longer need and confirm MFA is enforced for every user (especially admins).
