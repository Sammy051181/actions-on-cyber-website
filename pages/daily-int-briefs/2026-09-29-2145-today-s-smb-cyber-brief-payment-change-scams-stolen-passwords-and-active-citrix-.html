Today’s SMB Cyber Brief: Payment-change scams, stolen passwords, and active Citrix NetScaler exploitation

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Tuesday 29 September 2026, 21:45 UK time
Today’s look-out: Invoice/payment change scams and supplier edge-system compromise

What to look out for today

  • Business Email Compromise (BEC) / invoice fraud: criminals impersonating suppliers, clients, or senior staff to redirect payments.
  • Stolen staff passwords being used quietly: data can be accessed and taken for weeks before anyone notices.
  • Supplier/edge-system risk: active attacks reported against Citrix NetScaler (often used for remote access / app delivery), which can be a stepping-stone into internal systems.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

Smaller organisations are targeted because processes are busy, payments are frequent, and access controls are often lighter. BEC scams don’t need malware to work—just a convincing email and a rushed approval. Separately, real incidents show that stolen passwords alone can enable large-scale data theft without triggering obvious alarms. And if a key internet-facing system (such as a remote access gateway) is compromised, attackers can move from “one login” to widespread disruption and potential ransomware.

Warning signs

  • Bank detail changes requested by email, especially with urgency (“today only”, “new account due to audit”, “director approved”).
  • Emails that “look right” but come from a slightly altered address (extra character, different domain, reply-to mismatch).
  • New MFA prompts you didn’t trigger, or staff reporting unexpected login codes.
  • Unusual account behaviour: mailbox rules being created, sent items missing, or sudden forwarding to external addresses.
  • Remote access behaving oddly (new prompts, strange redirects) or unexplained admin logins around the time of supplier alerts.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • BEC/payment diversion: compromise or spoof an email account, watch an invoice thread, then insert “updated bank details”.
  • Password reuse / stolen credentials: use leaked passwords to access email, cloud storage, finance systems, or shared drives—then quietly export data.
  • Edge-system compromise: if a remote access gateway is breached, attackers may steal credentials and expand access across internal services.

What to do today

  • Run a 10-minute payment-change drill: remind staff that bank detail changes are never accepted by email alone; require a call-back to a known number (not the email signature).
  • Check the last 7 days of high-value payments: confirm bank details matched the supplier master record and were verified out-of-band.
  • Lock down email: ensure MFA is on for all mailboxes (especially finance and leadership) and disable automatic external forwarding unless there’s a business-approved exception.
  • Credential hygiene: prompt staff to change passwords if they reuse them across services; prioritise finance, payroll, admin and shared accounts.
  • If you use Citrix NetScaler: treat this as urgent operational risk—confirm your IT provider is actively monitoring and responding to vendor/security guidance.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have a written process for supplier bank detail changes and do you help enforce it (e.g., mail filtering, anti-impersonation controls)?
  • Can you show us recent sign-in alerts for Microsoft 365/Google Workspace and confirm MFA coverage for all users?
  • Are there any mailbox forwarding rules to external addresses, or suspicious inbox rules, on finance or director accounts?
  • Do we run Citrix NetScaler (directly or via a hosting/MSP partner)? If yes, what is the status of monitoring, containment measures, and vendor-recommended remediation?
  • If a key remote access system is compromised, what is our containment plan (credential reset, session revocation, log review, isolation)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Active exploitation is being reported against Citrix NetScaler. If your organisation (or your IT/MSP supplier) uses it, treat this as a priority service-protection issue and ensure mitigations/remediation and log review are underway. Don’t wait for “normal patch cycles” for internet-facing access systems.

One action today

Send a same-day reminder to finance/admin: no bank detail changes by email—verify via call-back to a known number and document the check before releasing payments.
