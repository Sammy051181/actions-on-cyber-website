Today’s SME cyber look-out: supplier incident scams + internet-facing gateway risk

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Thursday 01 October 2026, 15:27 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier/infrastructure incident scams and internet-facing remote access gateway compromise

What to look out for today

  • “Security incident” impersonation scams using big-name brands in crypto and payments (e.g., MetaMask / Bitget) as a hook to get staff to click links, approve “urgent” account actions, or change payment details.
  • Remote access / gateway compromise risk where attackers target internet-facing appliances (Citrix NetScaler) to gain a foothold and steal configuration data.
  • Windows device behaviour change: Windows 11 organisations may now have settings backup enabled by default on Entra-joined devices, which can affect what gets synced and restored when devices are rebuilt.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs often rely on a small number of key suppliers (IT providers, cloud identity, remote access gateways, finance tools and wallets). When a supplier reports an incident, criminals commonly follow up with phishing, fake support calls, and “urgent verification” messages. Separately, if your business (or IT provider) runs an internet-facing gateway for remote access, compromise can quickly lead to business disruption, credential theft and ransomware.

Warning signs

  • Email/Teams/WhatsApp messages claiming to be from a supplier “security team” asking you to re-login, reset, verify or import a new wallet / recovery phrase.
  • Requests to change bank details or “secure your funds” due to an incident.
  • Unexpected prompts to approve sign-ins or MFA requests you didn’t initiate.
  • Remote access behaving oddly: sudden lockouts, new admin accounts, unexplained config changes, or new/unknown access rules on gateways.
  • After a laptop rebuild, settings or sign-in behaviour looks different than expected (possible impact of newly enabled settings backup/restore).

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Brand-jacking: criminals reference real news about supplier incidents (e.g., MetaMask/Bitget) to make phishing more believable.
  • Fake support escalation: “We detected suspicious activity—call this number” scams that lead to credential theft or remote access installation.
  • Gateway foothold to internal access: attackers target exposed remote access infrastructure (e.g., Citrix NetScaler) and then move on to email, file shares and backups.
  • Configuration/data theft: stolen configuration details can help attackers understand your environment and target accounts and integrations.

What to do today

  • Brief staff (especially finance and anyone with admin access): no clicking “incident” links; no payment changes based on email alone; use a known-good contact route.
  • Verify your remote access exposure: confirm what gateways are internet-facing (yours or your MSP’s) and who is responsible for monitoring and rapid containment.
  • Lock down helpdesk processes: ensure password resets and MFA changes require strong verification (call-back to a known number, not one supplied in the message).
  • Review Windows settings backup policy for Entra-joined devices to ensure you’re comfortable with what may be backed up/restored in your environment.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we (or you, on our behalf) run Citrix NetScaler or any similar internet-facing remote access gateway? If yes, what monitoring and alerting is in place for suspicious changes and admin activity?
  • What is our containment plan if a gateway is suspected compromised (disable external access, rotate credentials/certs, investigate logs, restore from known-good config)?
  • Are we seeing any phishing themes related to supplier incidents (crypto wallets/exchanges, “account security updates”, fake support)? Can you share examples we should warn staff about?
  • With Windows 11 26H2 upgrades, is settings backup enabled by default for our Entra-joined devices, and do we need policy changes to control it?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If your organisation (or MSP) uses Citrix NetScaler/remote access gateways, treat “internet-facing gateway security” as a priority operational item this week: confirm ownership for updates, monitoring and rapid isolation. This is less about a single patch and more about making sure exposed access points are actively managed and logged.

One action today

Send a same-day message to staff: treat any “supplier security incident” email/text as suspicious—do not click links or change payments; verify via a known contact route.
