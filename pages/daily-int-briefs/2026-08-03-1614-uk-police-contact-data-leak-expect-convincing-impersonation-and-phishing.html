Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

UK police contact-data leak: expect convincing impersonation and phishing

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Monday 03 August 2026, 16:14 UK time
Today’s look-out: Impersonation scams and credential phishing using breached UK contact details

What to look out for today

Be alert for an uptick in targeted emails and calls impersonating the police, government partners, or criminal justice contacts following reports that contact data from the Police National Legal Database (PNLD) has been compromised and published.

In parallel, watch for fake cloud sign-in pages (including AWS-themed login lures) aimed at stealing passwords, and increased risk from mobile malware targeting Android and iOS users.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • More believable social engineering: When attackers have real names, organisations and work email formats, they can craft very convincing “routine” requests.
  • Invoice and payment fraud risk: Impersonation is often used to push urgent “fine/fee/payment” demands or to change bank details.
  • Credential theft leads to business disruption: Stolen Microsoft 365/Google/AWS-style logins can lead to mailbox takeovers, supplier fraud, data theft and (sometimes) ransomware.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected emails claiming to be from police/government/criminal justice contacts, especially requesting quick action or secrecy.
  • Messages referencing an “incident”, “case”, “warrant”, “evidence request”, “urgent compliance check”, or “data access request” with short deadlines.
  • Links that go to a sign-in page you weren’t expecting (particularly “cloud” login prompts) or attachments asking you to enable extra steps to view content.
  • Calls asking your team to “confirm” staff details, phone numbers, system access, MFA codes, or to install remote support tools.
  • Payment change requests that arrive right after a convincing “authority” email or call.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Authority-based impersonation: Using real police/government names and work addresses to pressure staff into sharing information or paying.
  • Credential phishing: Sending links to realistic cloud sign-in pages to capture passwords (and sometimes MFA prompts) for email or admin accounts.
  • Mobile compromise: Encouraging staff to open links on phones, install malicious apps, or respond to “security verification” prompts—risk is higher where mobiles are used for email/MFA.

What to do today

  • Brief staff (10 minutes): warn about police/government impersonation and remind them: no sharing passwords, MFA codes, or bank changes by email.
  • Reinforce payment controls: any change of bank details must be verified via a trusted number (from your own records, not the email).
  • Harden sign-in habits: require staff to access cloud services via bookmarks/known portals rather than email links.
  • Review mailbox rules and forwarding: check for unexpected inbox rules/auto-forwards on finance and senior accounts.
  • Mobile hygiene: ensure phones used for work have screen locks and are kept updated; challenge any request to install “security” or “support” apps.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you increase monitoring for suspicious sign-ins (impossible travel, unusual locations, repeated failures) on our email and key cloud accounts?
  • Do we have protections against lookalike domains and external sender impersonation (mail filtering, DMARC/SPF/DKIM posture, impersonation rules)?
  • How quickly would we know if a mailbox is forwarding externally or if new inbox rules were created?
  • What is our process for responding to suspected business email compromise (who to call, what gets disabled, how payments are checked)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

There’s a vendor patch reported for specialist laboratory human identification software that could affect integrity of certain DNA-related files in controlled environments. For most SMEs this is not directly relevant, but labs and organisations handling forensic or chain-of-custody workflows should confirm their supplier has applied the vendor fix and that file-handling controls are in place.

One action today

Send a same-day staff note: “Expect police/government impersonation and fake sign-in links; never share MFA codes or approve bank-detail changes without a call-back to a trusted number.”
