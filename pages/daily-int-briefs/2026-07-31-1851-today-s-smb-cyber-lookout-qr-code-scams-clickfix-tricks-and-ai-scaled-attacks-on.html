Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: QR-code scams, “ClickFix” tricks, and AI-scaled attacks on exposed systems

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Friday 31 July 2026, 18:51 UK time
Today’s look-out: Quishing and social-engineering leading to account takeover and malware, amplified by AI-assisted attacker tooling

What to look out for today

Be on alert for a rise in QR-code phishing ("quishing") and social-engineering lures that try to get staff to take urgent “fix” actions (often called ClickFix style attacks). Recent reporting also highlights attackers using AI tools to scale opportunistic attacks against internet-exposed servers with less manual effort.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • QR codes bypass suspicion: people treat them as “safe” and scan without checking the destination.
  • “Just do this quick fix” messages work on busy teams (office managers, finance, reception, teaching staff) and can lead to credential theft or malware.
  • AI-assisted attacker tooling increases volume: more automated probing means exposed remote access, old test systems, and misconfigured cloud services get found faster.
  • Ransomware risk increases when attackers can disable security tools or establish persistence after initial access.

Warning signs

  • Emails, letters, posters or invoices containing a QR code claiming to be for “payment”, “parcel redelivery”, “HMRC”, “Microsoft”, “staff login”, or “account verification”.
  • Messages telling users to copy/paste steps, run a “repair”, “security update”, or “verification” to regain access.
  • Any “your account will be locked today” or “payment failed, rescan to retry” urgency.
  • Unexpected multi-factor prompts, password reset emails, or sign-in alerts staff don’t recognise.
  • IT tools suddenly disabled, security alerts suppressed, or endpoints repeatedly “dropping off” management consoles.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Quishing: QR codes send staff to realistic login pages to harvest Microsoft 365/Google/CRM credentials, then attackers access email to intercept invoices or request payment changes.
  • ClickFix-style social engineering: attackers persuade users to take “manual fix” steps that end up granting access, installing unwanted software, or weakening device protections.
  • AI-assisted scanning and attack workflows: exposed admin panels, remote access services, or forgotten servers are identified faster and hit more consistently.
  • Post-break-in behaviour: once inside, attackers commonly try to establish persistence and disable defences, increasing the chance of business disruption.

What to do today

  • Brief staff in 5 minutes: “Don’t scan QR codes from emails/letters for logins or payments. If in doubt, type the official website yourself or call the supplier on a known number.”
  • Add a finance control: no bank detail changes from email/QR links—verify using a trusted, pre-existing contact method.
  • Check external exposure: confirm there are no internet-exposed admin interfaces, remote access services, or old servers you’ve forgotten about (including hosted or cloud systems).
  • Make sure security tooling can’t be easily disabled: confirm tamper protection / admin controls are in place for endpoint security.
  • Review sign-in alerts for your key platforms (email, accounting, payroll, CRM) and investigate anything unusual.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have a list of everything exposed to the internet (remote access, portals, admin pages), including any cloud-hosted services?
  • What controls are in place to prevent or alert on security software being disabled?
  • Do we have phishing-resistant MFA options available for email/admin accounts, and are they enabled for administrators?
  • How quickly would you detect and respond to email account takeover (new inbox rules, suspicious sign-ins, forwarding rules)?
  • Are we logging and alerting on new device enrolments and unexpected remote access?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Browser security fixes are landing frequently, with recent reporting highlighting unusually large volumes of fixes in Chrome releases. For SMEs, the practical takeaway is simple: ensure automatic updates are enabled across staff browsers and that any managed devices aren’t falling behind due to compatibility holds or deferred update policies.

One action today

Send a same-day staff note: “Don’t scan QR codes from emails/letters for logins or payments—use saved bookmarks or type the address; report any message asking you to ‘fix’ access by following steps or copying/pasting instructions.”
