Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: password-spraying surges and CCTV/IoT device takeovers

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Wednesday 19 August 2026, 17:46 UK time
Today’s look-out: Account takeover attempts via password-spraying + insecure internet-connected devices

What to look out for today

Two themes to brief staff and review with IT today:

  • Password-spraying at scale (high-volume login attempts using common passwords) targeting business email, Microsoft 365/Google, VPNs and remote access. Reports highlight attackers finding MFA gaps where some sign-in paths aren’t protected.
  • Internet-exposed CCTV/IoT compromise (notably Dahua devices in recent reporting), often via weak credentials or insecure remote access features. These devices can be used for disruption, spying, or as a foothold into the wider network.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • SMEs are frequently hit by automated attacks that don’t care who you are—just whether your sign-in is easy to crack.
  • Many organisations have MFA “on” but not everywhere (legacy protocols, app passwords, service accounts, old VPNs, shared mailboxes). Attackers actively look for these gaps.
  • CCTV and other “set and forget” devices are often managed outside normal IT processes (installed by facilities, a supplier, or a previous contractor) and can be left with risky remote access settings.

Warning signs

  • Sudden spikes in failed sign-ins (especially from many locations/ISPs) in Microsoft 365/Google/VPN logs.
  • Users receiving repeated MFA prompts they didn’t trigger.
  • Accounts getting temporarily locked (or password reset emails triggered unexpectedly).
  • Mailbox rules created without explanation (e.g., auto-forwarding, “mark as read”, moving invoices to RSS/Archive).
  • CCTV/NVRs behaving oddly: settings changed, new admin users, remote viewing enabled unexpectedly, or devices showing up in inventories you didn’t know existed.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Password spraying to find one weak account, then using that access for invoice fraud, payroll diversion, data theft, or to set up persistent mailbox rules.
  • Bypassing “partial MFA” by targeting older sign-in methods or edge cases not covered by your MFA policy.
  • Compromising CCTV/IoT to gain a foothold, observe premises, pivot into internal systems, or participate in broader criminal activity that can consume bandwidth and cause downtime.

What to do today

  • Check your MFA coverage end-to-end: confirm that every remote sign-in path is protected (email, VPN/remote access, admin portals, shared mailboxes, service accounts).
  • Turn on/confirm sign-in alerts for unusual login activity and impossible travel where available.
  • Enforce strong passwords and remove obvious weak/seasonal patterns; prioritise admin and finance accounts.
  • Review conditional access / login policies: block legacy authentication where possible and limit access by geography if that fits your business.
  • Inventory CCTV/IoT: list models, who manages them, where remote access is enabled, and whether default/admin credentials still exist.
  • Disable unnecessary remote access on CCTV/NVRs; restrict management interfaces to internal networks or trusted IPs only.

Ask your IT provider

  • “Can you show me which sign-in methods are exempt from MFA in our environment (including legacy protocols and service accounts)?”
  • “Do we have alerting for password-spraying and risky sign-ins, and who responds when it triggers?”
  • “Which accounts are most targeted (admin/finance), and do they have stronger controls than standard users?”
  • “Do we have an up-to-date asset list for CCTV/IoT, who owns it, and when credentials/firmware were last reviewed?”
  • “Are any CCTV/NVR devices internet-reachable directly or via vendor P2P/relay features?”

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you run industrial control or building automation that includes PLCs (common in some manufacturing, facilities, and estates teams), note that CISA has warned about active targeting of Siemens S7-series PLCs. The key SME takeaway is operational: make sure PLC/OT systems are not exposed to the internet, access is tightly controlled, and monitoring is in place—this is best handled with your specialist OT/IT provider.

One action today

Ask IT to confirm (in writing) that all your organisation’s sign-in paths are covered by MFA and that legacy/exception login methods are blocked or tightly controlled.
