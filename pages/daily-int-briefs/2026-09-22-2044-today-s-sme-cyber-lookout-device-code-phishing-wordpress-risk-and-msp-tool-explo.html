Today’s SME cyber lookout: device‑code phishing, WordPress risk, and MSP tool exploitation

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Tuesday 22 September 2026, 20:44 UK time
Today’s look-out: Cloud account takeover & supplier/MSP tooling risk

What to look out for today

  • Microsoft 365 / email account takeover attempts via “device-code” sign-in prompts (phishing designed to bypass passwords and sometimes even MFA if users approve it).
  • WordPress sites: a new core security fix is out—unpatched sites could be at higher risk of being taken over.
  • Supplier/MSP risk: reports of active exploitation against network and security management platforms (e.g. SD‑WAN orchestrators and firewall/security management servers). If your IT provider runs these, your business could be impacted indirectly.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs are often targeted for quick wins: email compromise that leads to invoice fraud, payroll diversion, or access to cloud files. WordPress remains a common website platform for smaller organisations. And if an attacker gets into a provider’s management tooling, disruption can cascade to many customers at once (policy changes, connectivity issues, or remote access abuse).

Warning signs

  • Staff report an unexpected sign-in message prompting them to enter a code on a legitimate-looking Microsoft sign-in page, or they receive repeated “sign-in request” notifications.
  • Emails or Teams messages urging “quick verification” or “urgent security check” that results in approving a sign-in.
  • WordPress admin accounts created unexpectedly, plugins/themes changed without approval, or sudden redirects/spam pages appearing on your site.
  • Internet connection instability across sites, sudden firewall rule/policy changes, or your IT provider mentions emergency work on their management platforms.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Device-code phishing: attackers trick users into completing a real sign-in flow by entering a code and approving access, giving attackers access to mailboxes and files.
  • WordPress compromise: attackers look for unpatched sites and attempt to gain control, then use them for defacement, malware distribution, spam/SEO abuse, or as a stepping stone to other accounts.
  • Provider tooling exploitation: attackers target central management servers (SD‑WAN / firewall policy managers) because one breach can affect many downstream customers.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-minute staff alert: “Never enter sign-in codes or approve unexpected login prompts. If you didn’t start it, deny it and report it.”
  • Review Microsoft 365 sign-in activity for key accounts (finance, shared mailboxes, admins) and confirm MFA is enabled.
  • For WordPress owners: ask your web partner to confirm the latest WordPress security release has been applied and that you have a clean, recent backup.
  • Agree an incident contact path with your IT provider for urgent notifications (who calls who, out-of-hours, what “high severity” means).

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have controls to reduce OAuth/app-consent and suspicious sign-in risk in Microsoft 365 (especially for finance and admin accounts)?
  • How quickly can you detect and respond to mailbox takeover and what logs do you review by default?
  • Are any of our sites on WordPress, and can you confirm the core security update is applied (and backups tested)?
  • Do you operate or manage VeloCloud Orchestrator and/or Check Point Security Management Server for us or in your environment? If yes, what mitigations/updates were applied and how are customer environments protected from blast-radius risk?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Today includes a notable WordPress core security release and fixes for security/network management platforms being discussed as exploited in the wild. Even if you don’t run these directly, ask your website provider/MSP to confirm updates are applied and that monitoring is in place for unusual sign-in activity and policy changes.

One action today

Message all staff today: if you receive an unexpected Microsoft sign-in code prompt or approval request, do not proceed—deny it and report it to IT immediately.
