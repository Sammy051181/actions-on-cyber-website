Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Hosting & password-manager incidents: reset access, watch for supplier-themed scams

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Wednesday 04 November 2026, 21:51 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier incident scams + credential/session theft and knock-on account takeover

What to look out for today

Today’s theme is supplier incidents and credential/session theft. Several well-known services have reported security issues that can lead to account takeovers and follow-on scams.

  • DreamHost breach: stolen FTP/SFTP usernames and passwords and webmail sessions.
  • mSecure password manager incident: customers urged to change passwords, reset 2FA and remove passkeys.
  • Supply-chain compromise example: a PC game launcher was tampered with to distribute remote access and data-stealing malware (useful reminder to treat “updates” carefully).
  • Network equipment issue: Zyxel reports a zero-day exploited with nearly 1,000 switches compromised and credentials/configuration targeted.
  • Positive note: Vercel is rolling out protections designed to reduce the impact of stolen session cookies (relevant if you build/host web apps there).

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs and charities often rely on a small number of hosted services (web hosting, email, password managers, and managed IT). When a supplier has an incident, attackers commonly:

  • Try stolen logins against other services (password reuse).
  • Use hijacked webmail sessions to send convincing phishing from a real business mailbox.
  • Target websites via FTP/SFTP to inject malicious redirects, deface pages, or change payment details on invoices/checkout pages.
  • Run follow-up supplier-themed phishing (“reset your password”, “re-verify your domain”, “pay to avoid suspension”).

Warning signs

  • Unexpected “password reset” or “security alert” emails that ask you to log in via a link.
  • FTP/SFTP logins from unusual locations/times, or new FTP users you don’t recognise.
  • Your website content changing unexpectedly (new pages, redirects, strange pop-ups).
  • Unexplained mailbox rules/forwarders added, or sent items you don’t recognise.
  • Staff reporting prompts to re-enter passwords more often than usual (possible session/cookie theft or account hijack attempts).

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Supplier impersonation: sending fake messages claiming to be DreamHost/mSecure/“your hosting provider” to capture credentials.
  • Credential stuffing: trying stolen FTP/email/password-manager credentials against Microsoft 365/Google, payroll, accounting, CRM, and banking portals.
  • Web compromise: using FTP/SFTP access to modify site files (including checkout or contact forms) and collect customer data.
  • Session abuse: using stolen webmail sessions or session cookies to bypass passwords and access accounts until sessions are revoked.

What to do today

  • If you use DreamHost: change FTP/SFTP passwords, disable unused accounts, and review who has access. Rotate any credentials stored in scripts or deployment tools.
  • If you use mSecure (now or previously): follow the vendor guidance—change key passwords and reset 2FA; remove passkeys where instructed.
  • Enforce unique passwords for email/hosting/admin logins and turn on MFA everywhere it’s available.
  • Check for mailbox forwarding rules and suspicious inbox rules (a common persistence trick after a compromise).
  • Brief staff: treat any “security update / re-verify / reset now” message as suspicious—go to the service by typing the address or using a bookmark, not via email links.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you confirm we have MFA on all admin accounts (hosting, domain/DNS, email, password manager, finance systems)?
  • Do we have monitoring/alerts for new mailbox forwarders, suspicious sign-ins, and changes to DNS/website files?
  • Where are our FTP/SFTP and deployment credentials stored, and how quickly can we rotate them?
  • Do any of our sites/apps rely on Vercel (and if so, what’s our plan to reduce session theft impact)?
  • Do we run any Zyxel switches, and are they internet-exposed or remotely managed? What’s the containment plan if credentials are suspected stolen?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Zyxel reported a zero-day being exploited against GS1900 switches with credentials/configs targeted. If you have Zyxel switching in your estate (including at branch sites), treat this as urgent operational hygiene: confirm what you run, ensure remote management isn’t exposed unnecessarily, and follow your provider/vendor’s update and credential-rotation guidance.

One action today

Do a 15-minute “access reset” sweep: change hosting/FTP passwords, revoke active webmail sessions where possible, and verify no unexpected mailbox forwarding rules exist.
