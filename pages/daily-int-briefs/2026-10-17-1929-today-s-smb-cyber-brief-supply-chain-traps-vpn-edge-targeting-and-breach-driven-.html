Today’s SMB Cyber Brief: supply‑chain traps, VPN edge targeting, and breach‑driven scams

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Saturday 17 October 2026, 19:29 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier & SaaS risk plus breach‑driven phishing (dev tooling, VPN edge, customer comms)

What to look out for today

  • Software supply-chain risk (developer tooling): a malicious npm package (“imgdecode”) has been found hiding a Windows native addon loader as part of a wider campaign. This is a reminder that third‑party packages can be an entry point into business systems via internal apps, websites, or build servers.
  • Internet-facing VPN / edge device targeting: researchers report attackers exploiting a Fortinet FortiOS flaw to deliver memory‑resident malware. If you rely on a managed firewall/VPN, this is a “check with your provider” moment.
  • Data breach ripple effects: Burger King Japan disclosed a breach affecting millions of customer records. Even if you’re not directly involved, big consumer breaches often trigger copycat phishing (“refund”, “points”, “account verification”) and credential-stuffing attempts.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs often depend on a small number of suppliers for websites, apps, managed IT, and remote access. A compromised software dependency, a vulnerable edge device, or a breach-driven phishing wave can cause real disruption: account takeovers, payment fraud attempts, ransomware access, or loss of customer trust.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected MFA prompts, password reset emails, or “new device signed in” alerts for Microsoft 365, Google, payroll, finance or CRM accounts.
  • Staff receiving emails/texts about “refunds”, “loyalty points”, “delivery issues”, or “account verification” linked to a well-known brand breach.
  • Unplanned changes in website/app build behaviour (new dependencies, unusual build failures, antivirus alerts on build servers, or unsigned/unknown binaries appearing).
  • Remote access/VPN instability, unexplained admin logins, or configuration changes on firewalls/VPNs (especially if managed by an MSP).

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Trojan packages in development pipelines: attackers publish lookalike or “useful” packages that get pulled into projects, then execute unwanted code during builds or on Windows systems.
  • Edge compromise as a shortcut: targeting VPN/firewall appliances can give attackers a foothold without needing to phish a user first.
  • Breach-themed social engineering: criminals piggyback on public breach news to trick staff/customers into clicking links, entering passwords, or approving payments.

What to do today

  • Tell staff one simple rule: don’t click breach/refund/points links from emails or texts—go to the site/app directly and log in there.
  • Protect admin and finance accounts: confirm MFA is enabled and review sign-in alerts for key accounts (email, accounting, payroll, cloud admin).
  • If you build software or run a web agency workflow: review recent dependency additions/updates and restrict who can approve new packages in production builds.
  • If you use a managed firewall/VPN: ask your provider to confirm whether your edge devices are affected by Fortinet exploitation reporting and what monitoring they have in place.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have any internet-facing Fortinet devices (FortiOS / FortiProxy / FortiPAM / FortiSwitchManager) and are they monitored for suspicious admin sessions and config changes?
  • What’s our process for emergency vendor security advisories on firewalls/VPNs (same day checks, logging, and confirmation back to us)?
  • Do we have a controlled process for third‑party software dependencies (e.g., allow-lists, approvals, build server hardening, and rollback capability)?
  • Can you show us recent sign-in risk events and MFA status for our highest-risk accounts (global admin, finance, payroll)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you run a Drupal website (including charity/school sites), Drupal has issued patches for critical remote code execution flaws and warned exploits may emerge soon. If your site is hosted/maintained by a supplier, ask them today to confirm your Drupal instance is covered and when it was last updated.

One action today

Send a short staff note today: “Ignore refund/points/breach emails & texts—don’t click links; go to the official site/app directly and report anything suspicious to IT/management.”
