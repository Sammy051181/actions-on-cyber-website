Today’s SMB cyber brief: MSP tooling risk, webmail account-takeover tricks, and breach-driven phishing

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Saturday 08 August 2026, 14:11 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier & SaaS risk + email account takeover + breach-driven scams

What to look out for today

  • MSP / IT supplier tooling risk: Reports of ongoing exploitation activity around N-able N-central, with attackers reaching managed systems and maintaining persistence in some cases. This is most relevant if you use an outsourced IT provider or have N-central in your environment.
  • Webmail interface abuse: New research suggests email content (e.g. HTML/CSS) may be able to interfere with webmail interfaces across major providers, with potential knock-on effects like token theft or account takeover in certain chains.
  • Breach ripple effects: A healthcare software company (Unlimited Technology Systems) reported a breach impacting 3.8 million people. Large breach disclosures often trigger waves of convincing phishing, “we’re updating our policy” emails, and identity-fraud attempts.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs often rely on a small number of critical services: an outsourced IT provider/MSP, a single webmail platform, and a handful of SaaS tools. When attackers can abuse a supplier tool or target login tokens, the impact can be disproportionate: mailbox takeover, invoice fraud, payroll diversion, and business disruption can follow quickly. Separately, big breach announcements increase the volume and believability of scams aimed at staff and customers.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected remote support activity (new remote sessions, unusual “maintenance windows”, or admin tools running when nobody requested support).
  • New mailbox rules or forwarding (especially rules that auto-archive, auto-delete, or forward to external addresses).
  • Repeated login prompts or staff reporting they are “randomly logged out” of email/SaaS.
  • Emails that look like normal webmail notifications but push staff to re-authenticate or “confirm” access urgently.
  • Inbound emails referencing recent breach news (medical/benefits/HR/insurance themes) asking for identity documents, payment, or password resets.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Supplier tool compromise: If an MSP’s monitoring/management tooling is exploited, attackers may try to move from the tool into customer systems, then create persistence (so they can come back) and pivot to email or finance workflows.
  • Webmail UI tricks: Attackers may send specially-crafted emails to increase the chance a user clicks something that looks trustworthy, or to influence what a user sees/does in the webmail interface, aiming to capture tokens or trigger unintended actions in connected accounts.
  • Breach-themed phishing: Criminals often use breach publicity to impersonate vendors, “support”, insurers, or compliance teams, pushing targets into handing over credentials, MFA codes, or payments.

What to do today

  • Tell staff one simple rule: if an email prompts you to “log in again” or “verify your account”, don’t use the link—go to the service by typing the known address or using a trusted bookmark.
  • Check for mailbox persistence: review key mailboxes (finance, payroll, shared inboxes) for new forwarding addresses and suspicious rules.
  • Harden finance workflows: require out-of-band verification (phone call to a known number) for new bank details and urgent payment requests—even if the email is from a familiar thread.
  • If you use an MSP: ask whether any RMM changes or emergency mitigations were applied this week, and whether customer environments were reviewed for suspicious remote activity.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we (or our MSP) use N-able N-central anywhere, and if so what additional monitoring/controls are in place right now?
  • What logs/alerts would tell us if a remote management tool is being abused (new agents, unusual remote sessions, new admin accounts)?
  • Can you confirm MFA is enforced for email and admin accounts, and that conditional access/sign-in risk alerts are being monitored?
  • Do you routinely review mailbox rules/forwarding for high-risk users (finance, senior leadership), and can we get a monthly report?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you run self-hosted business apps, be aware of reports of Metabase being exploited in data-theft attacks. This is more relevant to organisations that host analytics/BI tools internally or in their own cloud account—ask IT whether Metabase exists in your estate and, if yes, whether it has been reviewed urgently.

One action today

Review finance and shared mailboxes today for new/invisible inbox rules and any external auto-forwarding—and remove anything you don’t explicitly recognise.
