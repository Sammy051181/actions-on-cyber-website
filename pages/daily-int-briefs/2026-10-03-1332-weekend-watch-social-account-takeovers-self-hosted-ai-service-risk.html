Weekend watch: social account takeovers + self-hosted AI service risk

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Saturday 03 October 2026, 13:32 UK time
Today’s look-out: Account takeover scams and supplier/self-hosted SaaS dependency risk

What to look out for today

1) “Trusted account” posts pushing crypto or investment links. A widely-followed Microsoft social media account was hijacked and used to promote a crypto pump-and-dump. Expect copycat scams impersonating well-known brands, local organisations, and even your own colleagues’ accounts.

2) Increased risk from browser-first attacks that bypass traditional endpoint visibility. Some attacks focus on stealing sessions, abusing browser extensions, or tricking users in ways that don’t look like a classic ‘malware infection’ to endpoint tools.

3) Self-hosted ‘AI gateway’ services as an entry point. GitLab warned about a critical issue in its AI Gateway service used to connect GitLab to AI models. This mainly affects organisations running self-hosted components, which can include some SMEs and many IT providers.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Brand trust gets abused quickly. Staff may trust posts/messages because they appear to come from a reputable account.
  • Fraud risk and reputational risk. If your business social account is taken over, customers may be directed to scams, fake promotions, or bogus support numbers.
  • Outsourced IT and platform dependency. Even if you don’t run GitLab yourself, your developer, MSP, or key supplier might—service disruption or breach impact can ripple to you.

Warning signs

  • Sudden “announcement” posts about tokens, giveaways, urgent promotions, or limited-time investment opportunities from otherwise reputable accounts.
  • Colleagues seeing ‘login prompts’ or ‘security check’ pages after clicking links in social posts or direct messages.
  • Unexpected new browser extensions, changed homepage/search settings, or repeated session logouts.
  • Your own social pages showing new admins, unrecognised scheduled posts, or posts you didn’t approve.
  • Developers/IT mentioning a rushed patch or emergency maintenance relating to self-hosted AI tooling.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Impersonation and momentum. Attackers hijack a trusted account, post a convincing ‘official’ message, then use the credibility to drive victims to scam sites.
  • Session theft over malware. Rather than installing obvious malicious files, attackers may try to steal browser sessions or tokens, letting them access email, social, and SaaS without needing passwords.
  • Supplier-side compromise/disruption. If a self-hosted AI gateway or similar connector is compromised, it can become a stepping stone into internal systems or cause downtime while it’s investigated and rebuilt.

What to do today

  • Send a short staff note: “Don’t trust social posts/DMs about crypto, giveaways, or ‘urgent security updates’. If in doubt, don’t click—report it.”
  • Lock down social accounts: enforce MFA, reduce the number of admins, review connected apps, and remove old agencies/contractor access.
  • Browser hygiene check: remind staff to only use approved extensions; remove anything unneeded; ensure browser updates are on.
  • Supplier check-in: if you rely on a developer/MSP, ask whether any self-hosted GitLab/AI gateway components are in use and whether emergency actions are needed.
  • Prepare comms: have a simple ‘account takeover’ response plan (who resets access, who posts the warning, who handles customer queries).

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we (or any of our suppliers) run self-hosted GitLab AI Gateway or similar ‘AI connector’ services? If yes, what’s the current status and mitigation plan?
  • How are our social media admin accounts protected (MFA type, number of admins, device controls)?
  • Can we get a monthly review of who has admin access to key platforms (Microsoft 365/Google, payroll, website CMS, social accounts)?
  • What controls do we have for browser extensions and session protection on staff devices?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

GitLab has warned about a critical issue affecting its AI Gateway service (primarily relevant to self-hosted deployments). If your business (or your IT provider) runs self-hosted GitLab/AI gateway components, treat this as an urgent maintenance item and confirm a plan to reduce disruption and verify systems after any update.

One action today

Review and reduce admin access on your business social media accounts today (remove old agencies/contractors, enforce MFA, and check for unknown connected apps).
