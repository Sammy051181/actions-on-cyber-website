Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber look-out: firewall management systems under attack, webmail persistence, and SaaS disruption

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Thursday 30 July 2026, 10:40 UK time
Today’s look-out: Perimeter & SaaS dependency risk (firewall management access + webmail persistence + AI service outage)

What to look out for today

Three themes are worth SMEs paying attention to today:

  • Firewall / security management consoles being targeted: Cisco has warned that a Secure Firewall Management Center (FMC) issue involving static credentials has been actively exploited.
  • Webmail “stickiness” after password resets: Reporting suggests attackers are exploiting an Outlook Web Access (OWA) flaw to keep mailbox access even after credential rotation, seen in multiple sectors.
  • SaaS disruption: Anthropic has confirmed a global Claude outage / elevated errors, which can affect any tools that depend on the Claude API.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Perimeter compromise can become total business compromise: If a firewall management system is accessed, attackers may change rules, disable protections, or pivot further into your network.
  • Email is your front door for fraud: If an attacker can maintain access to a mailbox, they can monitor invoices, reset passwords elsewhere, and run convincing “conversation hijack” payment scams.
  • AI tool downtime is an operational risk: If staff rely on AI for customer replies, drafting, analysis, or ticket handling, an outage can create backlogs and rushed workarounds (which often reduce security and quality).

Warning signs

  • Unexpected changes to firewall policies, admin accounts, or remote access settings.
  • New admin logins at odd hours, from unfamiliar locations, or repeated failed login attempts against management portals.
  • Users reporting they are being logged out repeatedly, receiving unexpected MFA prompts, or seeing mailbox rules/forwarding they didn’t set.
  • Suppliers/customers asking to “reconfirm” bank details mid-thread, or invoices that look slightly different from normal.
  • Claude/AI-enabled tools failing with overload-style errors; staff switching to personal accounts or unapproved AI tools to “get the job done”.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Firewall management access: Attackers look for internet-exposed management interfaces or weakly governed admin access and then try to gain unauthorised entry to control security settings.
  • Mailbox persistence: If attackers can hold onto access after a password reset, they can wait for payroll/payment runs, harvest invoices, and trigger resets for other business systems.
  • Outage-driven social engineering: When a service is down, criminals often send “service status” emails or “urgent re-auth” messages to capture credentials, or impersonate support to “help”.

What to do today

  • Confirm ownership of perimeter systems: Identify who manages your firewall and its management console (internal IT or MSP) and ensure there is an accountable owner.
  • Check email abuse controls: Ask for a quick check of mailbox forwarding rules, suspicious inbox rules, and recent sign-in anomalies for key finance/admin users.
  • Reinforce payment-change controls: Remind staff: bank detail changes are confirmed via a known phone number (not email), and second-person approval is required.
  • Prepare for AI tool downtime: Agree a simple fallback process (templates, queue management, human review) so staff don’t create risky workarounds.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we run Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center (FMC) anywhere (including at clients/sites we connect to)? If yes, is the management interface exposed to the internet, and what extra access controls are in place?
  • Have you checked for unusual admin access or configuration changes on perimeter devices in the last 7 days?
  • For Microsoft email: do we have monitoring/alerts for suspicious sign-ins and mailbox rule creation on finance and senior accounts?
  • What is our approved list of AI tools, and how do we prevent staff sharing business data into unapproved services during outages?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Cisco has reported active exploitation of a Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center issue (CVE-2026-20316) and CISA has added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue; if your business (or MSP) uses FMC, treat validation and remediation as urgent and confirm any internet exposure and admin access controls.

One action today

Today, get written confirmation from your IT/MSP whether you use Cisco FMC (and if it is internet-exposed) and ask them to review the last 7 days of admin access/config changes plus sign-in anomalies for finance mailboxes.
