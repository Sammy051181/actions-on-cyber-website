SMB Cyber Brief: Supplier incidents, cloud tooling tampering, and service outages – what to watch today

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Tuesday 20 October 2026, 13:42 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier incident scams, compromised downloads, and service disruption

What to look out for today

Today’s themes are supplier disruption and supply-chain tampering (compromised downloads/updates). These situations often trigger a second wave of risk for smaller organisations: phishing, fake “support” calls, and urgent payment/access change requests.

  • ConnectWise Cloud Backup incident: ConnectWise says it reset credentials and strengthened admin access protections while Cloud Backup remains offline following unauthorised access to application identities.
  • OVHcloud CLI supply-chain compromise: OVHcloud warns that some GitHub releases of its CLI were tampered with to steal developer secrets.
  • WordPress plugin compromise: Some downloads of Rank Math PRO (SEO plugin) from the vendor’s official site carried malware capable of planting hidden backdoors.
  • Service outage ripple effects: Revolut suffered a major outage affecting online banking services worldwide.
  • Large retailer disruption: Ahold Delhaize reports a cyberattack impacting services across the US and Europe—an example of the kind of upstream disruption that can affect suppliers and logistics.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Backups and recovery: If you or your IT provider relies on ConnectWise Cloud Backup, downtime can affect recovery times and business continuity planning.
  • “Trusted tools” risk: Many SMEs (and their outsourced IT/MSPs) use CLIs, plugins, and vendor downloads as part of normal operations. When those are compromised, passwords/tokens can be stolen and websites can be quietly backdoored.
  • Payment and payroll pressure: Banking outages can push teams into risky workarounds (urgent manual transfers, sharing card details, bypassing approvals).
  • Scam wave likelihood: Criminals routinely exploit high-profile incidents/outages by impersonating vendors, banks, or MSPs to harvest logins and MFA codes.

Warning signs

  • Email/calls claiming to be from ConnectWise/your MSP asking you to “re-authenticate”, “reconnect backups”, “reset MFA”, or share a one-time code.
  • Messages urging you to install a “hotfix tool”, “updated CLI”, or “emergency plugin download” outside your normal update process.
  • Unexpected prompts for WordPress admin logins, new admin users appearing, or unfamiliar plugins/themes installed.
  • Developers/IT seeing unusual credential prompts or signs that tokens/keys may have been accessed.
  • Finance receiving “Revolut is down, use this new account today” payment diversion requests.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Vendor impersonation: Sending fake incident notices and directing staff to lookalike login pages to steal credentials/MFA codes.
  • Poisoned updates/downloads: Encouraging IT teams to quickly download and run tools (e.g., a CLI) to “restore service”, capturing secrets and enabling wider access.
  • Website backdoors: Using compromised WordPress plugins to plant hidden access, then redirect traffic, steal form submissions, or pivot to email accounts and invoices.
  • Invoice/payment fraud during outages: When banking is disrupted, attackers try to get businesses to bypass normal approval steps “just this once”.

What to do today

  • Brief staff (10 minutes): No one should share MFA codes or passwords with “support”, even during an incident/outage. Use known contact details only.
  • Confirm your backup position: If you use an MSP, ask whether any of your backups rely on ConnectWise Cloud Backup and what the current recovery options are while services are affected.
  • Freeze “urgent tool installs”: Ask IT to pause any ad-hoc downloads/updates for admin tools and plugins unless verified through your normal process.
  • WordPress check: If you run WordPress and use Rank Math PRO, ask whoever manages your site to validate the plugin source and review admin users and recent changes.
  • Finance controls: During banking disruption, stick to existing approval steps. Treat “new bank details” as high risk and validate via a known phone number.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do any of our backups, DR tests, or restore plans depend on ConnectWise Cloud Backup? What is our workaround while it’s offline?
  • Have you checked whether any of our environments used the OVHcloud CLI releases that were reported as tampered with? If yes, what secrets/keys might need rotating?
  • For our website: do we use Rank Math PRO? If yes, what checks have you done (admin accounts, file integrity, unusual outbound connections, unexpected scheduled tasks)?
  • What’s our process for verifying vendor alerts so staff don’t follow phishing links during incidents?
  • Can you provide a short list of authorised support channels and escalation contacts we should use this week?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Today’s risk is less about routine patching and more about trusting the right update sources. If your business (or MSP) updates developer/admin tooling or WordPress plugins, ensure downloads come from verified channels and that changes are tracked and reviewed before deployment.

One action today

Send a same-day internal note: “No password/MFA code sharing with ‘support’; any vendor/MSP reset request must be verified using our saved contact details,” and remind finance to treat any bank-detail changes as high risk during outages.
