Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: risky browser extensions, AI assistants, and ransomware readiness

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Thursday 17 September 2026, 13:35 UK time
Today’s look-out: Browser extension & AI assistant data exposure, plus ransomware disruption planning

What to look out for today

  • Browser extensions with “too much power” — research suggests a single seemingly ordinary extension could take control of built-in AI assistants in several Chromium-based browsers and AI tools (e.g., Chrome/Edge-style assistants). Treat extensions as high-trust software.
  • Staff connecting business financials to AI tools — a new “connect your bank accounts” style AI feature is being tested in the market. This is a red flag for data governance and supplier risk if used informally inside a business.
  • Ransomware costs are mostly downtime — current coverage again highlights that recovery time, operational disruption, and remediation often outweigh any ransom demand, and that recovery planning (including backup & restore) is what limits damage.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs often rely on browsers, extensions, and SaaS tools to run day-to-day work (email, accounting, HR, client work). If an extension can influence or access an AI assistant, it can increase the chance of data leakage (client info, pricing, contracts, staff data) and account compromise via stolen sessions or tricked users.

Separately, “AI that connects to your bank account” is convenient but raises practical questions: who can link it, what data is shared, where it’s stored, and what happens if the AI account is compromised. And ransomware remains a top driver of business interruption—especially for organisations without tested recovery.

Warning signs

  • Staff installing new extensions to “summarise emails/meetings”, “help with invoices”, “rewrite proposals”, or “talk to the browser”.
  • Extensions asking for broad permissions (e.g., read/change data on websites, access to all sites, manage downloads, read clipboard) without a clear need.
  • Unexpected AI assistant behaviour: sending content you didn’t ask for, responding to prompts you didn’t type, or offering to “connect” to additional services.
  • Finance team members mentioning new AI features that link to bank feeds, cards, or accounting platforms without an approved rollout.
  • Early ransomware indicators: multiple users suddenly locked out, unusual file changes, widespread “file type changed” reports, or backups failing/being disabled.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Malicious or compromised extensions that quietly gain access to sensitive browsing contexts and then interact with built-in AI assistants to extract or summarise data.
  • Social engineering convincing staff that an extension/AI tool is “company approved” or “needed for productivity”.
  • Data harvesting via AI integrations — if a staff member connects bank or accounting data to an AI tool, a compromised AI account (or poor access controls) can expose highly sensitive financial information.
  • Ransomware operators banking on weak recovery: if restores aren’t tested and access is broad, disruption lasts longer and pressure to pay increases.

What to do today

  • Do a quick extension audit: list installed browser extensions across staff devices (especially finance/admin) and remove anything unused or unapproved.
  • Set a simple rule: no-one connects business bank accounts, card feeds, or accounting data to AI tools unless it’s formally approved by finance + IT/data owner.
  • Check AI tool access: ensure MFA is enabled on AI accounts and that staff understand what data they are allowed to paste/upload.
  • Ransomware readiness mini-check: confirm you can restore a key system (file share, line-of-business app, Microsoft 365/Google Workspace data) within an acceptable time, and that the process is documented.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you enforce an extension allow-list (or at least block high-risk categories) in our managed browsers?
  • Do we have visibility of which extensions are installed across endpoints, and can we remove them remotely?
  • How do we protect against session hijacking and token theft for cloud apps and AI/SaaS tools?
  • What’s our current backup and recovery objective (RTO/RPO in plain English), and when was the last successful restore test?
  • If a Google Workspace/Microsoft 365 account is compromised, what’s our first-hour response plan?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

No specific patch-driven issue is the key theme today. The practical focus is controlling high-trust software (browser extensions) and tightening access to AI and finance integrations, alongside tested recovery for ransomware disruption.

One action today

Audit and remove unapproved browser extensions today (start with finance/admin users), and introduce a rule that no-one links business bank/accounting data to AI tools without formal approval.
