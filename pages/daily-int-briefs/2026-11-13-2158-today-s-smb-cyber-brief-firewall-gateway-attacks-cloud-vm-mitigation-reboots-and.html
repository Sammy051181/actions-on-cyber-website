Today’s SMB cyber brief: Firewall gateway attacks, cloud VM mitigation reboots, and tampered hardware wallets

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Friday 13 November 2026, 21:58 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier & platform risk causing business disruption (security gateways, cloud VMs, hardware device supply chain)

What to look out for today

  • Security gateway / firewall compromise and disruption: reporting suggests some Check Point gateway devices have been actively targeted, including cases linked to follow-on malware affecting VMware ESXi environments.
  • Cloud VM maintenance that needs a restart window: Google Cloud is warning some customers on newer AMD “Turin” hosts that mitigations require a guest OS update and a stop/start of VMs.
  • Device supply-chain tampering: Trezor confirmed hardware implants in a small number of Safe 5 wallets sold via a specific reseller, designed to steal recovery phrases.
  • AI agent safety (process issue): reminder that AI agents connected to business tools can be tricked into taking unsafe actions if permissions and approvals aren’t set up carefully.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs often depend on a small number of key systems to stay operational: a security gateway to access the internet and VPN, a virtualised server stack (including ESXi), and cloud-hosted services. If a gateway is compromised, it can become the stepping stone into the business. If cloud mitigations require VM stop/start, even a well-run business can face unplanned downtime. And supply-chain tampering is a reminder that “hardware security” still depends on where you buy from and how you verify devices.

Warning signs

  • VPN users report sudden inability to connect or unusual MFA prompts; new/unknown VPN accounts appear.
  • Unexpected configuration changes on your firewall/security gateway (new admin users, new rules, changed remote access settings).
  • VMware ESXi hosts show unexplained service interruptions, missing VMs, or management access behaving oddly.
  • Cloud workloads show unplanned reboots or your provider flags that instances need stop/start to apply mitigations.
  • For crypto (if your organisation holds any): staff report a “new hardware wallet” arrived from a reseller and asks for recovery phrase in an unusual way; device packaging or provenance seems off.
  • AI/automation: an AI tool suggests exporting data, changing payment details, or sending bulk emails without a clear business reason.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Gateway-to-network pivot: attackers who gain high privileges on an internet-facing gateway may use it to move further into the network, access admin interfaces, or deploy disruptive malware.
  • Disruption as leverage: wiping or knocking out virtual hosts (e.g., ESXi) can create sudden downtime that pressures a business into rushed decisions.
  • Cloud co-tenancy concerns: cloud/CPU issues can matter most when an attacker already has a foothold (e.g., can run code in a VM) and tries to learn more than they should.
  • Supply-chain theft: tampered devices can defeat otherwise good security practices by capturing secrets at setup time.
  • AI agent prompt/permission abuse: attackers may plant instructions inside tickets, emails, or documents that cause an over-permissioned AI agent to take unauthorised actions.

What to do today

  • Confirm ownership and monitoring of your gateway/firewall: who manages it (in-house or MSP), how logs are reviewed, and how quickly emergency fixes can be applied.
  • Check for recent admin changes: review recent administrative logins and configuration changes on security gateways and remote access services.
  • Protect ESXi management paths: ensure ESXi management interfaces aren’t exposed unnecessarily and confirm you have offline/immutable backups and a restore plan you’ve tested.
  • Plan cloud maintenance windows: if you use Google Cloud VMs, ask your provider/team whether any workloads need a stop/start after OS updates, and schedule downtime to avoid surprises.
  • Hardware wallet hygiene (if relevant): only procure via trusted channels, quarantine and verify any new devices, and follow vendor guidance if you bought from the named reseller.
  • AI agent guardrails: ensure any AI agent connected to business systems has least privilege and requires human approval for exports, payment changes, account changes, or bulk communications.

Ask your IT provider

  • Are any of our security gateways affected by the actively targeted Check Point issue, and what evidence do we have (logs/alerts) that we’re clean?
  • Do we have a known-good backup for critical servers and virtual machines that’s protected from deletion/wiping, and when was a restore last tested?
  • If we run on Google Cloud, are any workloads on hosts impacted by the AMD “Turin” mitigations, and what’s our stop/start plan (timing, comms, rollback)?
  • What’s our process for emergency hotfixes to security appliances (change control, out-of-hours response, verification)?
  • If we use AI tools/agents: what permissions do they have, and what actions require human approval?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Today’s patch watch is about emergency fixes and planned restarts: Check Point has issued hotfixes for an issue described as actively exploited, and Google Cloud notes that some mitigations for AMD “Turin” environments require a guest OS update plus a VM stop/start. Ensure you know which systems you run, who applies urgent fixes, and how you schedule downtime safely.

One action today

Today, ask your IT provider to review your security gateway/firewall for recent admin/config changes and confirm whether emergency hotfixes (and any required reboots) are needed this weekend.
