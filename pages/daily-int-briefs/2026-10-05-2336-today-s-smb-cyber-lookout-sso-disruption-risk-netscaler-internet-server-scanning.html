Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: SSO disruption risk (NetScaler), internet server scanning (HFS), and AI-enabled scams

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Monday 05 October 2026, 23:36 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier/SSO disruption + exposed server scanning + AI-powered social engineering

What to look out for today

1) Citrix NetScaler (ADC/Gateway) SAML/SSO disruption risk: Reports indicate a newly addressed NetScaler issue has been exploited in targeted attacks and can knock SAML deployments offline (impacting sign-in and access to business apps).

2) Rejetto HFS (HTTP File Server) systems being actively scanned: If you (or a supplier) still run HFS exposed to the internet, attackers may be probing it for a critical weakness.

3) AI-assisted attack themes in the mainstream: Banking-focused incidents and broader reporting highlight attackers using automation/AI to scale convincing lures and speed up account takeover attempts.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • SSO downtime becomes business downtime: If SAML/SSO breaks, staff may be locked out of Microsoft 365, email, CRM, payroll, finance or line-of-business systems.
  • Knock-on effects for outsourced IT and hosted services: If your MSP, hosting provider, or a key supplier relies on NetScaler or HFS, your operations can be impacted even if you don’t run these tools directly.
  • Disruption creates “helpdesk” scam opportunities: When sign-in issues hit, attackers often exploit the confusion with fake IT support calls/emails, password reset links, or MFA push fatigue.

Warning signs

  • Sudden spikes in staff reporting they “can’t log in” to multiple services at once (especially via SSO).
  • Unexpected SAML/MFA prompts, repeated approval requests, or “session expired” loops.
  • Inbound emails/chats claiming to be IT/MSP asking users to re-authenticate, install a remote tool, or share MFA codes.
  • Unexpected server/service instability on a file-sharing or legacy web server (particularly any internet-facing file server).

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • SSO disruption as cover: Attackers may trigger outages or sign-in failures and then follow up with believable “fix your access” phishing.
  • Account takeover attempts: Password spraying, session hijacking attempts, and MFA fatigue tactics become more effective when users are already struggling to sign in.
  • Opportunistic scanning: Internet-exposed servers (like HFS) can be scanned at scale; once found, attackers try to gain a foothold and pivot to file access, ransomware, or data theft.

What to do today

  • Brief staff (5 minutes): “If you get a login problem, don’t trust random ‘IT fix’ links. Use the normal bookmarked sign-in pages and report anything odd.”
  • Check your exposure: Confirm whether you or your suppliers use Citrix NetScaler for remote access/SSO, and whether any internet-facing file servers (including legacy tools like HFS) exist.
  • Harden sign-in support: Make sure staff know the official way to contact IT/MSP (a known phone number / portal), not via links in emails.
  • Increase monitoring today: Pay extra attention to sign-in anomalies, repeated MFA prompts, and new remote access tool installs.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we (or any key supplier/MSP platform we depend on) use Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway for SSO or remote access?
  • Have you checked whether we’re exposed to the NetScaler issue affecting SAML/SSO availability, and what mitigations/updates have been applied?
  • What’s our fallback plan if SSO is down (break-glass admin accounts, alternate access to email/critical apps, comms plan)?
  • Do we have any internet-facing file servers or legacy web/file services (including Rejetto HFS)? If yes, who owns them and how are they monitored?
  • What alerts do we have in place for MFA fatigue and unusual sign-in patterns today?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Citrix has issued emergency updates relating to an exploited NetScaler issue reported to disrupt SAML/SSO. Even if you don’t manage patches yourself, treat this as a priority supplier question today: confirm whether NetScaler is in your environment (or your MSP’s) and whether updates/mitigations have been applied, plus what user-visible disruption to expect during remediation.

One action today

Send a same-day internal note: “If you have login/SSO problems, do not approve unexpected MFA prompts and don’t use ‘IT reset’ links from emails—contact IT/MSP via the usual number/portal.”
