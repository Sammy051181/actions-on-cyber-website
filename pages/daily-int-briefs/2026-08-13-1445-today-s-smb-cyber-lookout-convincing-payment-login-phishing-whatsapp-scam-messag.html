Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: convincing payment/login phishing, WhatsApp scam messages, and mobile card theft

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Thursday 13 August 2026, 14:45 UK time
Today’s look-out: Payment and login phishing + messaging scams + mobile payment theft

What to look out for today

Three themes to brief staff on today:

  • Highly convincing “checkout” and “account login” phishing pages that imitate well-known shopping and payment brands.
  • Scam messages arriving via WhatsApp (including business conversations), with new in-app scam warnings beginning to roll out.
  • Android phone malware used to steal payment card details, including scenarios where criminals relay card data in real time.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Card and bank fraud hits cashflow fast and often starts with one member of staff entering credentials or approving a payment.
  • WhatsApp is widely used for quick operational decisions (shift swaps, supplier comms, customer queries), making it an easy route for impersonation and urgent-payment pressure.
  • Mobile compromise is a business compromise if staff use phones for email, MFA codes, banking, card payments, or approving invoices.

Warning signs

  • “Session expired” / “Confirm your payment” / “Unusual activity” messages pushing you to log in or re-enter card details.
  • Checkout or login pages that look perfect but arrive via a link in an email, text, QR code, social message, or WhatsApp.
  • WhatsApp messages that change the normal process: new bank details, new payee, “pay this now”, “can you buy gift cards”, or “I’m in a meeting”.
  • On Android phones: unexpected accessibility permissions prompts, requests to install an app outside the Play Store, battery drain, or new apps you don’t recognise.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Phishing frameworks can generate realistic, brand-matching pages to harvest credentials and payment details at scale.
  • Follow-on account takeover: stolen logins used to access email, Microsoft 365/Google Workspace, shopping accounts, payment processors, or saved cards.
  • Messaging-based impersonation: criminals use WhatsApp to imitate directors, finance staff, suppliers, or customers and push for urgent transfers.
  • Mobile theft of card data: Android malware may help criminals capture or relay card data to make fraudulent purchases.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-minute staff note: “No logins or card details via links. Use bookmarks/app. Verify payment changes by phone using a known number.”
  • Enable/encourage WhatsApp’s scam warning feature where available, and remind staff it’s a hint—not a guarantee.
  • Re-check payment controls: dual approval for new payees and bank detail changes; call-backs for first-time or changed details.
  • Mobile hygiene: only install apps from official stores; remove unknown apps; ensure screen locks are on; keep devices updated.
  • Report and contain: if anyone entered credentials, reset passwords immediately and review recent sign-ins and payment activity.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have phishing-resistant MFA (or at least MFA everywhere) for email, cloud apps, and finance tools?
  • Can we detect and block suspicious sign-ins (impossible travel, risky IPs) and alert the right person quickly?
  • Do we have domain and brand impersonation monitoring or anti-phishing protections for common “checkout/login” lures?
  • What is our mobile device policy for staff who access email/MFA on personal Android phones?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

The UK NCSC is highlighting the value of adding a BitLocker PIN on Windows devices to better protect data if a laptop is lost or stolen. If you use BitLocker, ask IT whether a startup PIN is appropriate for your organisation and how it would be rolled out without disrupting staff.

One action today

Send a short internal message today: “No payments or password resets from links/messages (including WhatsApp). Use saved bookmarks/apps, and verify any payment or bank-detail change by calling a known number.”
