Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Supplier disruption + data breach: expect impersonation and downtime knock-ons

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Monday 17 August 2026, 08:59 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier outage scams and data-breach follow-on phishing

What to look out for today

Three themes to keep an eye on:

  • Supplier outages: Anthropic Claude is experiencing a major outage, and Threema has suffered disruption from DDoS attacks.
  • Data-breach follow-ons: SafePal has reported a customer order-data breach, with stolen information reportedly being offered for sale.
  • Impersonation risk: When a well-known service is down or in the news, criminals often use it as a pretext for “support” scams and phishing.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Business disruption: If staff rely on AI assistants or secure messaging to draft emails, handle customer queries, or coordinate work, outages can delay service and create pressure to take shortcuts.
  • Higher scam success rate: Breached customer data (names, contact details, order information) can make phishing more convincing—especially for finance/admin staff.
  • Shadow IT risk: If a tool is down, teams may switch to unapproved apps or personal accounts, increasing data leakage and compliance issues.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected emails/texts/calls claiming to be from “Claude/Anthropic support”, “Threema support”, or “SafePal” asking you to log in, reset, or confirm details.
  • Messages urging urgent action due to an “outage”, “account lock”, “security check”, or “compensation/refund”.
  • Payment or bank-detail change requests that reference a supplier incident (“we can’t access our usual system, use this account instead”).
  • Staff moving work conversations to personal WhatsApp/Signal/email “just for today”.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Outage-themed phishing: Fake status updates and fake support tickets that capture passwords or MFA codes.
  • Helpdesk/social engineering: Attackers call pretending to be support to “verify your account” while the service is degraded.
  • Targeted follow-on scams: If customer/order data is leaked, criminals can tailor messages that reference real purchases or delivery addresses to build trust.
  • Invoice and payment diversion: Disruption and confusion are used to bypass normal approval steps.

What to do today

  • Brief staff (2 minutes): “Expect fake support messages related to outages/breaches. Don’t click login links from emails. Use bookmarks or your normal portal.”
  • Lock payment changes: No bank-detail changes or urgent payments without a call-back to a known number and a second approver.
  • Plan for disruption: Identify one approved fallback for team communications and one for drafting/customer responses if AI tools are unavailable.
  • Reduce data leakage: Remind teams not to paste sensitive customer or case details into ad-hoc tools while normal services are unstable.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have conditional access/MFA policies to reduce harm if a password is phished?
  • Can we block or flag lookalike domains and known phishing patterns for “support” scams quickly?
  • What’s our approved contingency for secure messaging and for AI/tool outages (and how do we prevent staff using unapproved apps)?
  • Are we logging and alerting on unusual sign-ins and mass mailbox forwarding-rule changes (common after phishing)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

No specific patch issue is highlighted in today’s items. The priority today is operational: treat supplier disruption and breach news as a trigger for impersonation/phishing, and reinforce payment verification and account-sign-in hygiene.

One action today

Send a short internal note today: “Outage/breach-themed ‘support’ messages are likely—don’t use emailed login links, and verify any payment detail changes by calling a known number.”
