Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: browser attacks, WordPress scanning, and a software supply‑chain compromise

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Friday 04 September 2026, 13:07 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier compromise + common-business software being actively targeted (browser and WordPress)

What to look out for today

  • Browser-based attacks hitting unpatched devices: Google has warned of a new Chrome zero-day being exploited in the wild.
  • WordPress sites being hammered: Reports of large-scale exploit attempts against popular WordPress plugins (including Elementor Pro and Super Forms).
  • Software supply-chain risk: A registry/infrastructure compromise at Coder reportedly led to malicious Terraform modules designed to steal credentials.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs don’t need to be “special targets” to be affected. These stories are about high-volume, opportunistic attacks and supplier dependency risk:

  • A single staff laptop with an out-of-date browser can become the entry point for account takeover or further malware.
  • WordPress is common for local businesses, charities and schools. Automated scanning can hit any site on the internet, not just big brands.
  • If your IT provider, web agency, or internal dev team uses infrastructure-as-code tools (e.g., Terraform modules), a compromised upstream source can quietly leak credentials and expose cloud systems.

Warning signs

  • Staff report Chrome behaving oddly, repeated crashes, or unexpected sign-ins/prompts after browsing.
  • Your website suddenly redirects, shows new admin users, unknown plugins, or unexplained changes to pages/forms.
  • Unusual login activity in cloud/admin consoles (Microsoft 365/Google, AWS/Azure/GCP), especially from new countries or impossible travel.
  • Build/automation jobs start failing, new outbound connections, or alerts about secrets/keys being used unexpectedly.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Chrome exploitation: attackers use real-world browsing lures (ads, compromised sites, SEO pages) to compromise devices that haven’t updated yet, then pivot to email accounts and files.
  • WordPress plugin scanning: mass probing for sites running vulnerable plugins, then attempting to gain access or plant backdoors and spam/redirects.
  • Compromised Terraform modules: malicious code in modules can siphon credentials (e.g., API tokens, cloud keys), enabling follow-on access to cloud resources and business data.
  • Access-broker ecosystem: sophisticated malware frameworks are being used to turn compromised Windows machines into resale “access”, which can later lead to ransomware or data theft.

What to do today

  • Force browser updates: ensure all staff devices running Chrome are updated today (including shared/front-desk PCs).
  • Quick WordPress check: confirm who manages your site, which plugins are installed, and whether you have recent backups and admin MFA enabled.
  • Reduce blast radius: review who has admin access to your website, hosting, domain registrar, and cloud dashboards; remove dormant accounts.
  • Supply-chain hygiene: if you (or your supplier) use Terraform/modules, ask whether they’ve checked for suspicious registry/module sources and rotated any exposed credentials.
  • Staff reminder: be cautious of “routine” prompts to sign in, approve access, or install updates from pop-ups—use company-approved update routes.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you confirm all managed endpoints have applied the latest Chrome update, and that reporting shows compliance?
  • Do we have a monitored inventory of our public-facing web assets (WordPress, plugins, admin accounts), and do we get alerts for unauthorised changes?
  • What is our restore plan for the website (backup frequency, retention, and a tested restore time)?
  • If you build/deploy infrastructure for us: how do you validate third-party modules/registries, and how quickly can you rotate credentials if an upstream source is compromised?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Today’s patch watch is focused on Chrome: Google has warned of an actively exploited zero-day, so prioritise updating Chrome across all business devices (including any personal/BYOD devices used for work) and confirming the update actually completed.

One action today

Get confirmation (from your IT provider or internally) that Chrome has updated successfully on every staff device today, not just “scheduled to update”.
