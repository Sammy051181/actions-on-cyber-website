Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber look-out: supplier-linked data leaks and AI-assisted attacks targeting Microsoft 365 and MSP chains

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Sunday 15 November 2026, 21:01 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier incident scams, cloud/M365 takeovers, and breach follow-on phishing

What to look out for today

  • Supplier-linked data exposure: a major organisation has linked an employee data leak to a cloud provider, reinforcing that third-party systems can be the weak link.
  • AI-assisted attack automation: law enforcement is warning that an AI-driven “pentest-style” tool is being used to scale attacks globally, including activity connected to a supply-chain breach affecting Microsoft 365 customer environments.
  • Follow-on scams: when personal or business contact details leak, expect a surge in targeted phishing, fake IT/support calls, and invoice/payment-change fraud.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

Even if the incident isn’t “about you”, SMEs often feel the knock-on effects: criminals reuse leaked contact details, imitate suppliers, and target smaller firms because approvals are faster and controls are lighter. If your business relies on Microsoft 365, an outsourced IT provider/MSP, or cloud services, supply-chain issues can turn into account takeovers, email fraud, and disruption quickly.

Warning signs

  • Emails or calls claiming to be your IT provider, Microsoft support, or a security team urging urgent login, “token refresh”, or password reset.
  • Unexpected MFA prompts (approval requests you didn’t initiate) or users reporting they’ve been “logged out everywhere”.
  • Supplier emails asking to change bank details, resend invoices to a new address, or “confirm payment” via a new link.
  • New inbox rules/forwarding set up in mailboxes; sent items missing; unusual sign-in alerts.
  • Staff receiving more tailored messages that reference real names, job titles, or internal terms (a clue data may be circulating).

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Breach data → targeted phishing: using leaked employee details to craft believable password reset, HR, payroll, or IT security messages.
  • Supply-chain trust abuse: impersonating your MSP/IT provider, or using compromised supplier accounts to email you from “real” addresses.
  • M365 takeover → payment fraud: once an email account is accessed, attackers watch invoices, then intercept and change payment instructions at the last moment.
  • Automation at scale: AI-assisted tooling can increase the volume and speed of attacks, meaning smaller organisations may be hit “in the same wave” as larger targets.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-minute staff nudge: “Expect fake IT/support messages and supplier bank-change requests; verify via a known number; don’t approve unexpected MFA prompts.”
  • Reconfirm payment controls: no bank detail changes by email alone; always call a trusted number already on file and have a second-person approval for first payments or changes.
  • Check M365 basics: ensure MFA is on for all users (especially finance/admin), and confirm you have sign-in alerts and mailbox auditing/logs available via your plan/provider.
  • Review supplier contacts: make sure you have verified phone numbers for key suppliers and your IT provider (not numbers included in an email).

Ask your IT provider

  • Are we affected by any Microsoft 365 / MSP supply-chain activity you’re tracking, and what extra monitoring is in place this week?
  • Do we have conditional access / risky sign-in monitoring (or equivalent) and a clear process for responding to suspicious logins?
  • Can you confirm MFA coverage for all accounts, including legacy/admin/service accounts?
  • What’s our rapid containment plan if an inbox is compromised (disable sign-in, revoke sessions, remove rules/forwarding, reset credentials, notify affected parties)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

CISA has flagged active exploitation of a high-severity flaw in UnrealIRCd (an IRC server). Most SMEs won’t run this, but if your organisation (or a supplier) hosts IRC for community/support, treat it as urgent operational risk and have your IT provider confirm whether it exists anywhere in your environment.

One action today

Send a short internal message today: ‘Do not approve unexpected MFA prompts and do not accept supplier bank detail changes by email—verify using a known phone number.’
