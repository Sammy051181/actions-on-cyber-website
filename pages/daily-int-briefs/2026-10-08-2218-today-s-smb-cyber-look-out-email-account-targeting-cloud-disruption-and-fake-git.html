Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber look-out: email account targeting, cloud disruption, and “fake GitHub” malware

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Thursday 08 October 2026, 22:18 UK time
Today’s look-out: Email account takeover + supplier cloud outage scams + poisoned developer downloads

What to look out for today

Three themes SMEs should actively watch today:

  • Targeting of email systems and credentials (including password spraying and email/credential theft) highlighted in a UK and international partner advisory.
  • Cloud/service disruption risk after a ransomware incident caused a data-centre cluster outage for a major cloud provider (reminder: your suppliers can be the single point of failure).
  • Malicious “lookalike” code and downloads, with a large wave of fake GitHub repositories pushing malware.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Email compromise is still the fastest route to fraud: if attackers get into Microsoft 365/Exchange mailboxes they can read invoices, reset passwords elsewhere, and impersonate staff.
  • Supplier outages quickly become business outages: even if you’re not the target, ransomware at a cloud provider can stop access to services, customer portals, or line-of-business apps.
  • One bad download can ripple across the business: infected developer tools or scripts can lead to credential theft and follow-on account takeovers.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected sign-in alerts for email accounts, repeated login failures, or logins from unusual locations/devices.
  • New or unfamiliar inbox rules (e.g., auto-forwarding, moving finance emails to RSS/Archive, marking invoice emails as read).
  • Sudden “supplier outage” messages that ask you to “reconfirm” credentials, reset MFA, or change bank details.
  • Developers/staff reporting odd GitHub repos (recently created, minimal history, copied README files, lots of stars that look artificial) or new “install” steps that feel off.
  • Service instability from a cloud provider (intermittent portal access, degraded performance) followed by increased phishing pretending to be that provider.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Password spraying against business email to find weak/reused passwords, then using the mailbox to pivot to other systems.
  • Business Email Compromise (BEC): replying inside real email threads to request urgent payments, invoice reissues, or bank-detail changes.
  • “Outage support” social engineering: using a real supplier incident as cover to push fake login pages or fake helpdesk calls.
  • Poisoned code supply chain: luring staff to download “tools”, “templates”, or “fixes” from fake repos that install malware or steal credentials.

What to do today

  • Lock down business email: ensure MFA is enabled for all accounts (especially admins and finance), and block legacy/basic authentication where possible.
  • Check for mailbox forwarding and suspicious rules on finance and senior leadership accounts.
  • Reinforce payment-change controls: never accept bank detail changes by email alone; verify using a known phone number or a separate trusted channel.
  • Brief developers and IT: don’t run “install scripts” or downloads from unknown GitHub repos; prefer trusted publishers and internal-approved tool lists.
  • Review your top 5 suppliers (cloud, payroll, email, MSP, payments): confirm you have an outage workaround and the right contact routes.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you show us recent sign-in risk and failed login trends for Microsoft 365/Exchange and key accounts (finance/admin)?
  • Do we have alerting for new mailbox forwarding rules and suspicious inbox rules?
  • What’s our ransomware-ready supplier plan: backups, recovery time expectations, and what services fail “closed” if a cloud provider is down?
  • Do we have controls for developer tooling (approved repositories, application allow-listing, endpoint protection coverage on dev machines)?
  • If credentials are stolen, what is the first-hour playbook (disable sessions, reset tokens, review MFA methods, check rules/forwards)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Today’s main risk is not “a single new patch” but credential and email-system exposure. If you still run on-prem email or internet-facing services, confirm your IT provider is monitoring vendor security advisories and that remote access (including VPN) and email systems are maintained and reviewed for suspicious access.

One action today

Run a 15-minute check today: review MFA coverage and scan finance/admin mailboxes for suspicious inbox rules or auto-forwarding, then remind staff to verify any payment/bank-detail changes via a known phone number.
