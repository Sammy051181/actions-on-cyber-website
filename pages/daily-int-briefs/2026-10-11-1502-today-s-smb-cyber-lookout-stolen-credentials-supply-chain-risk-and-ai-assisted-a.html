Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: stolen credentials, supply‑chain risk and AI‑assisted attacks

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Sunday 11 October 2026, 15:02 UK time
Today’s look-out: Credential theft and software supply‑chain exposure (GitHub Actions), plus AI‑assisted intrusion tactics

What to look out for today

Three themes to brief staff and suppliers on today:

  • Developer and IT account credential theft, including malicious automation in GitHub repositories (GitHub Actions workflows) designed to steal secrets and access tokens.
  • AI-assisted attacks being used to speed up reconnaissance, phishing, and intrusion activity (reported in attacks against banks).
  • Mobile device compromise risk (iOS exploit kit reporting) that may lead to account takeover and sensitive data theft, particularly where phones are used for MFA, email and finance approvals.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Supply-chain ripple effects: even if you don’t use GitHub directly, you may rely on software, plugins, websites, or MSP tools built from open-source code and automated pipelines.
  • Business email compromise: stolen credentials can lead to invoice fraud, payroll diversion, or customer data exposure.
  • Phones are now a “master key”: if a phone is compromised, attackers may hijack email sessions, reset passwords, and intercept MFA prompts.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected password reset emails, MFA prompts, or sign-in alerts for Microsoft 365/Google, payroll, banking, accounting, or CRM tools.
  • Unexplained changes to payment details, supplier bank details, or “new beneficiary” setups.
  • Developer/IT notifications about new or changed CI/CD workflows, new repository “Actions”, or secrets being accessed unexpectedly.
  • Users reporting their phone behaving oddly (new profiles/apps, frequent pop-ups, battery drain) alongside account lockouts or new logins.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Malicious automation in code repos: attackers add a workflow that quietly harvests credentials/tokens and uses them to access other services.
  • AI to scale social engineering: faster, more convincing messages to finance teams and busy managers (payment changes, “urgent approval”, account recovery).
  • Mobile-to-cloud pivot: compromise a phone, then take over email and use it to reset passwords for payroll, accounting, and file storage.

What to do today

  • Run a quick “credential check”: confirm MFA is on for email, finance, payroll, and admin accounts; remove any unused admin accounts.
  • Finance control reminder: re-issue your call-back process for bank detail changes and first-time payments (no exceptions, even for “CEO urgent”).
  • If you use GitHub (or your MSP/dev supplier does): review recent workflow changes, restrict who can modify workflows, and rotate any secrets/tokens if anything looks unfamiliar.
  • Mobile hygiene: ensure staff phones used for work are up to date, use screen lock, and avoid approving unexpected MFA prompts.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have alerts for unusual logins and impossible travel on Microsoft 365/Google and key SaaS tools?
  • Which accounts are “high impact” (finance, payroll, global admin) and are they protected with phishing-resistant MFA where possible?
  • If we (or our suppliers) use GitHub: what controls are in place to prevent/flag unauthorised GitHub Actions workflow changes and secrets access?
  • Do we have an agreed process to rapidly rotate credentials/tokens if a developer or repo is suspected compromised?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If staff use iPhones for work (email, MFA, approvals), ensure iOS and key apps are kept up to date and that devices are managed where possible (basic MDM, enforced screen lock). Today’s iOS exploit-kit reporting is a reminder that mobile devices are part of your attack surface.

One action today

Send a short internal note today: “Do not approve unexpected MFA prompts or payment detail changes; report any sign-in alerts immediately,” and re-confirm your finance call-back process for bank detail changes.
