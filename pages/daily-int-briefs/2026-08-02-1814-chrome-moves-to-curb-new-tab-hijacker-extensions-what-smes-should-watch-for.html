Chrome moves to curb “New Tab” hijacker extensions — what SMEs should watch for

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Sunday 02 August 2026, 18:14 UK time
Today’s look-out: Browser extension hijacks and managed device policy risk

What to look out for today

Google Chrome is preparing a security feature that may block policy-installed extensions from hijacking the New Tab page or changing the default search engine. This is a useful reminder to treat sudden browser changes as a potential security issue, not just an annoyance.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

Many SMEs rely on browsers as their “front door” to email, accounting, HR, payments and customer data. If a browser is quietly redirected (new tab/search), staff can be steered to lookalike pages, malicious adverts, or tracking that increases the chance of credential theft and fraud.

If you use an IT provider or MDM to manage devices, extensions may be installed centrally. That’s good when controlled properly, but it also means a single mistake (or abuse of an admin account) can affect lots of users quickly.

Warning signs

  • Your New Tab page suddenly changes (different layout, “enterprise search”, unfamiliar branding, unexpected widgets).
  • Default search engine changes without a clear business reason.
  • More pop-ups/ads, unusual “coupon” prompts, or a noticeable performance slowdown in the browser.
  • Staff report being repeatedly logged out of Microsoft 365/Google/other web apps, or seeing extra sign-in prompts.
  • The same change appears on multiple company devices at roughly the same time.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Credential harvesting: redirecting search/new tabs to pages that push lookalike login flows.
  • Traffic interception: steering users to ad-heavy or malicious results and increasing exposure to scams.
  • Data collection: tracking browsing and search terms that reveal suppliers, bank names, staff roles, or ongoing projects.
  • “Managed” persistence: if an extension is installed by policy, users may be unable to remove it, so issues can spread and persist until IT changes the policy.

What to do today

  • Tell staff what to report: any sudden New Tab/search changes, new “company search” tools, or repeated sign-in prompts.
  • Check your managed Chrome policies: confirm which extensions are approved and why they’re needed.
  • Reduce extension sprawl: remove anything unused, duplicated, or “nice to have”.
  • Protect admin access: ensure accounts that can push browser policies/extensions use MFA and are tightly limited.

Ask your IT provider

  • Which Chrome extensions are policy-installed across our business today, and what business purpose does each serve?
  • Do we run an allow-list approach for extensions, or can users install anything?
  • How would we detect and respond if a browser policy was changed unexpectedly across devices?
  • Who has permission to push browser policies, and is MFA enforced for those admin accounts?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

No specific patch action from today’s item. This is primarily about browser configuration and extension governance: keep Chrome auto-updates enabled and focus on controlling which extensions can be installed (especially via policy) and monitoring for unexpected changes.

One action today

Send a quick internal note: “If your Chrome New Tab page or default search changes unexpectedly, report it to IT immediately,” and ask your IT provider for the current list of policy-installed extensions.
