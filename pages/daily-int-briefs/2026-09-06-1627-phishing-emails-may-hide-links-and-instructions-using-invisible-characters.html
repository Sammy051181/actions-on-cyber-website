Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Phishing emails may hide links and instructions using invisible characters

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Sunday 06 September 2026, 16:27 UK time
Today’s look-out: Phishing evasion tricks (hidden text, disguised links) bypassing email filters

What to look out for today

Phishing campaigns are using invisible Unicode characters to conceal malicious lures in emails and other messages. The text can look harmless to a person, while the hidden characters help it slip past some email security checks.

  • Emails that look “blank” in places, have odd spacing, or contain strangely formatted text
  • Links that appear normal but behave differently when clicked or copied
  • “View document / voice note / shared file” prompts that don’t match your normal tools

Why this matters to smaller businesses

Many SMEs rely on standard email protections and staff judgment. If attackers can make phishing content harder for filters to detect, more of it will land in inboxes, increasing the chance someone clicks a link, signs in to a fake page, or approves a payment request.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected urgency: “account locked”, “invoice overdue”, “payment failed”, “new voicemail”
  • Unusual formatting: odd line breaks, inconsistent fonts, strange gaps, or text that doesn’t highlight normally
  • Link mismatch: hover preview doesn’t match the visible link text, or the link looks normal but the destination is unfamiliar
  • Login prompts for Microsoft 365/Google/DocuSign/Dropbox that arrive out of the blue

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Use invisible characters to disguise keywords that filters look for (e.g., “password”, “verify”, brand names)
  • Hide or distort parts of a URL so the message looks legitimate at a glance
  • Increase deliverability of common lures (invoice, shared file, HR document), leading to credential theft and mailbox takeover

What to do today

  • Tell staff what to watch for: odd spacing/formatting, “view document” prompts, unexpected login pages
  • Use a verification step for payment or bank detail changes (call back using a known number, not the email)
  • Encourage reporting: forward suspicious emails to your IT/helpdesk or use the built-in “Report phishing” button
  • Reduce impact: ensure MFA is on for email and key SaaS accounts, and that finance users have extra checks

Ask your IT provider

  • Are our email security tools set to detect or flag Unicode/invisible character obfuscation patterns?
  • Do we have a simple, working one-click phishing reporting process and do you review trends weekly?
  • What controls do we have for business email compromise (MFA coverage, risky sign-in alerts, mailbox forwarding rules monitoring)?
  • Can you run a quick check for suspicious mailbox rules and unusual sign-in activity in the last 7–14 days?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

No specific patch action is required for this phishing technique. Treat it as a reminder to keep email clients, browsers, and security tools up to date and to focus on verification steps and MFA so a single click doesn’t become a business-wide incident.

One action today

Send a 2-minute staff warning today: be cautious of emails with odd spacing/formatting and never sign in via unexpected “view document/voicemail” links—verify via a known channel instead.
