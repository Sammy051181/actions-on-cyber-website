Inbox-led payment fraud and Microsoft 365 account persistence: today’s SME look-out

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Friday 07 August 2026, 18:06 UK time
Today’s look-out: Invoice/payment diversion using real email threads, plus longer-term cloud access after a single infected device

What to look out for today

Today’s theme is “real-looking” business email being used to hijack payments, alongside a reminder that if malware runs on a signed-in Windows PC it may be able to turn that foothold into longer-term Microsoft 365 (Entra ID) access.

  • Payment diversion from genuine email chains (compromised inboxes / browser manipulation) rather than obvious spoof emails.
  • Social engineering of staff leading to direct access to corporate data on their machines.
  • Cloud account persistence risk if a device is already infected while a user is logged in.
  • Crypto payment redirection via clipboard hijacking (where relevant).

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs often rely on a small number of people to approve invoices, manage supplier changes, and administer Microsoft 365. Attackers take advantage of that reality: a single compromised mailbox or infected laptop can be enough to (1) redirect a payment, (2) quietly collect sensitive files, and (3) create longer-term access to your cloud tenant that survives password changes.

Warning signs

  • Supplier/customer emails that look normal but introduce urgency ("payment needed today", "banking details updated", "new beneficiary").
  • A request to change bank details arriving mid-thread in an existing conversation.
  • Emails that push you to open the bank in a browser or follow a different process “just this once”.
  • Unexpected MFA prompts, sign-in alerts, or users reporting they were logged out of Microsoft 365 or asked to re-authenticate repeatedly.
  • Staff reporting unusual copy/paste behaviour (mainly relevant if you make cryptocurrency transfers): pasted wallet addresses change or don’t match what was copied.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Compromised business inboxes are used to monitor real invoices and then insert believable “bank detail change” instructions at the perfect time.
  • Social engineering targets specific employees to gain access to data stored on their machines.
  • If malware is already running in a signed-in Windows session, it may be able to use existing authentication keys to access Microsoft Entra ID and establish longer-term cloud access (e.g. registering a device or adding additional sign-in methods), depending on your tenant policies.
  • Clipboard hijacking can silently swap payment details during a transaction workflow (most commonly discussed for crypto transfers, but the lesson is to verify critical payment details out-of-band).

What to do today

  • Reconfirm your payment change process: no bank detail changes by email alone. Require a call-back to a known number (from your records, not the email) and a second-person approval.
  • Brief finance/admin staff: “If it’s urgent, that’s the point.” Encourage pause-and-verify on any change request.
  • Check Microsoft 365 sign-in alerts (especially for finance, directors, and admins) and ensure you know who receives and reviews those alerts.
  • Review device hygiene: ensure endpoint protection is running and that users aren’t routinely working as local admins.
  • If you handle crypto payments: independently verify the destination address (e.g. compare multiple times, use known-good address books, and confirm via a second channel).

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have conditional access or equivalent controls to reduce the impact of a stolen/abused session (especially for Microsoft 365 admins and finance users)?
  • How quickly would you detect and respond to a compromised mailbox (rules created, forwarding set up, unusual sign-ins)? What’s the containment playbook?
  • Are new device registrations and additional authentication method changes monitored and alerted on for our tenant?
  • What is our business process control for payment changes (two-person approval, call-back, verification logs) and can you help us test it?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

No specific patch rush from today’s items for most SMEs. The practical priority is reducing the impact of “already-on-the-PC” malware by keeping managed devices updated, ensuring endpoint protection is active, and using strong Microsoft 365 tenant policies and monitoring for suspicious sign-in and registration activity.

One action today

Send a 5-minute note to finance/admin staff today: “No bank detail changes by email alone—call back using a trusted number and get a second approval before any payment details update.”
