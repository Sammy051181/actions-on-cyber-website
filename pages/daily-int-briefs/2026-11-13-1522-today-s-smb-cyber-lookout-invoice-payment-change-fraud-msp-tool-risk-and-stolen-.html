Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: invoice/payment change fraud, MSP tool risk, and stolen cloud credentials

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Friday 13 November 2026, 15:22 UK time
Today’s look-out: Fake payment changes + supplier/MSP compromise leading to account takeover

What to look out for today

Three themes to brief staff on today:

  • Invoice and bank-detail change scams made more convincing (including where AI or automation is involved in invoice processing).
  • Managed service provider (MSP) / remote support tool risk: malware designed to spread from a customer device to MSP technician machines and then onward to other client networks.
  • Cloud and email account takeover via stolen credentials and secrets (e.g., keys and tokens found in code repositories), leading to fraud and disruption.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs are attractive targets because a single compromised mailbox, remote support session, or finance workflow can quickly lead to:

  • Misrouted payments (supplier bank details quietly changed).
  • Wider compromise via trusted IT access if an MSP or remote tool is abused.
  • Cloud disruption and data exposure if attackers obtain API keys, service credentials, or admin sessions.

These attacks often look like normal business activity (a supplier email, a helpdesk session, a “routine” invoice), which is why simple verification steps make a big difference.

Warning signs

  • Any request to change bank details, especially if it adds urgency or asks to bypass “normal process”.
  • Invoices that look normal but have new bank details or subtle changes to beneficiary name/sort code/account number.
  • Emails that appear to come from a colleague/supplier but contain odd phrasing, unusual timing, or pressure to act quickly.
  • Unexpected remote support prompts, new remote agents installed, or IT asking for access in a way that’s not your usual process.
  • Unexplained logins, MFA prompts you didn’t initiate, or new “apps/integrations” added to Microsoft 365/Google/finance platforms.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Payment diversion via email manipulation: criminals send a malicious or carefully crafted email that leads staff (or an automation/AI agent) to update supplier bank details without the right checks.
  • MSP pivoting: attackers compromise one organisation, then use remote management/support pathways to reach an MSP technician environment and potentially other customers.
  • Secrets harvesting: criminals abuse compromised developer accounts and repositories to find credentials (tokens/keys), then use them to access cloud services, data, or automate fraud.
  • Ransomware enablement: stolen credentials and remote tool access are commonly used as stepping stones to deploy ransomware or wipe/disable systems.

What to do today

  • Re-confirm your payment change process: bank-detail changes must be verified via a known, independent channel (e.g., call a verified number from your records, not the email).
  • Add a “2-person rule” for first-time or changed bank details (maker/checker approval in finance).
  • Lock down who can change supplier bank details in your finance/payroll systems and review recent changes.
  • Ask your MSP how they are monitoring remote tool abuse and how they separate technician access between customers.
  • Check cloud/email security basics: ensure MFA is on, review recent sign-ins, and remove unknown app integrations.
  • If you build software or use GitHub: confirm secrets scanning is enabled and that no keys/tokens are stored in repos; rotate any credentials you suspect may have been exposed.

Ask your IT provider

  • “If a customer device is compromised, what stops it spreading to technician machines and then to other clients?”
  • “How do you secure and monitor remote support tooling (including alerts for unusual remote sessions and deployments)?”
  • “Do you enforce separate privileged accounts per customer, and do you use conditional access / device checks for admin logins?”
  • “Can you show us how we’re alerted to suspicious sign-ins and new app integrations in Microsoft 365/Google?”
  • “Do you help us implement (or audit) maker/checker controls for supplier bank changes in our finance platform?”

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you use a managed security gateway or rely on an MSP to run one, confirm they have a clear process to rapidly address actively exploited perimeter issues and to validate monitoring afterwards. Even when a supplier says mitigations are in place, you want written confirmation of what was done, when, and what extra monitoring is enabled for follow-on activity.

One action today

Send a same-day note to finance: “No bank detail changes are actioned from email alone—verify using a known phone number and require a second approver.”
