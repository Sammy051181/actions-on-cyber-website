Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Teams ‘IT support’ vishing driving ransomware + fake macOS update scams

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Friday 31 July 2026, 10:51 UK time
Today’s look-out: Impersonation scams via Teams/remote support leading to ransomware

What to look out for today

Be on alert for impersonation attempts where someone claims to be IT support and asks staff to approve a call, install a tool, or allow remote access—particularly via Microsoft Teams. Reporting shows these social-engineering approaches are being used as the first step towards Chaos ransomware deployment.

Also watch for fake macOS update pages (often reached via adverts/redirects) that try to trick people into installing “updates” that are actually malware.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs and charities are attractive targets because a single successful remote-access trick can give attackers the same outcomes as a full technical breach: stolen credentials, disrupted operations, and ransomware downtime. Teams and remote-support workflows are also normal day-to-day tools—making it easier for a convincing caller to blend in.

Warning signs

  • An unexpected Teams chat/call from “IT”, “Helpdesk”, “Microsoft support”, or a manager asking for urgent action.
  • Pressure tactics: “we need to fix this now”, “your account is being attacked”, “approve this access request”.
  • Requests to install remote access tools, browser add-ons, “security updates”, or to sign in to a link while on the call.
  • On Macs: a full-screen “update” experience that doesn’t look quite right, appears after clicking an ad, or asks to download and run a file.
  • Unusual MFA prompts or login notifications shortly after a call or “support” message.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Teams vishing: attackers impersonate internal IT to persuade a user to grant remote access or run software, then move quickly to business disruption and ransomware.
  • Credential theft: “support” processes are used to capture passwords/MFA approvals, enabling mailbox or SaaS takeover.
  • macOS malvertising: adverts/redirects lead to fake update pages designed to install credential-stealing malware.

What to do today

  • Set a simple rule for staff: IT will never ask for passwords, and any unexpected “support” contact must be verified via a known internal number or ticketing portal.
  • Turn on (or reinforce) “report phishing/suspicious” guidance for Teams messages and emails—make reporting easy and non-punitive.
  • Remind Mac users: only update via System Settings (or your managed update tool), not via pop-ups/ads.
  • Ensure you can quickly disable accounts and revoke sessions if someone suspects they approved access or entered credentials.
  • Do a quick check that backups are recent and recoverable (ransomware readiness).

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have controls to limit or monitor remote access tools and unattended access on endpoints?
  • What’s our process to rapidly contain an account takeover (disable account, revoke tokens/sessions, reset MFA)?
  • Do we have Teams security settings reviewed (external access, file sharing, safe links, reporting)?
  • Can we detect and respond quickly to ransomware behaviours on endpoints and servers?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If your organisation (or your IT provider) runs VMware platforms, today’s reporting highlights new VMware security fixes. You don’t need to act alone, but you should ask your provider to confirm whether your environment is affected and when updates will be applied, as compromise of core virtualisation infrastructure can lead to wide business disruption.

One action today

Send a same-day staff note: “If anyone contacts you on Teams claiming to be IT and asks you to install software, approve access, or sign in—stop and verify via our known helpdesk route before doing anything.”
