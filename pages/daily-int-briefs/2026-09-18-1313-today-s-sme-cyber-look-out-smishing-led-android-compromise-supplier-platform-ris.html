Today’s SME cyber look-out: smishing-led Android compromise, supplier platform risk, and firewall management exposure

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Friday 18 September 2026, 13:13 UK time
Today’s look-out: Smishing and third‑party supplier compromise (mobile + IT/security tooling)

What to look out for today

Today’s main theme is attackers getting in via “everyday” channels: SMS messages to staff mobiles, and third-party platforms your business (or your IT supplier) depends on.

  • Smishing leading to Android malware via links to fake download pages and deceptive ads (malvertising).
  • Security/IT management server exposure that could impact organisations running their own firewall management, or those relying on an MSP/IT provider to run it.
  • Supply-chain risk via npm packages (developer dependencies) delivering information stealers that can harvest browser data, including extension storage.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs don’t need to be “targeted” to be hit. If a staff mobile is compromised, it can lead to mailbox access, payment fraud, or account takeovers. If a supplier’s security management environment is exposed, it can become a fast route to broad business disruption. And if your website/marketing agency or internal app uses compromised third-party code, credentials and customer data can be put at risk.

Warning signs

  • Text messages claiming missed deliveries, invoices, account “verification”, HMRC/benefits, or IT support, pushing you to install an app or “update” a service.
  • Android devices suddenly asking for unusual permissions, accessibility access, or trying to get you to enable developer-type settings.
  • Unexpected re-auth prompts for Microsoft 365/Google/work apps after clicking a text link.
  • New browser extensions, changes to existing extensions, or staff reporting “odd” pop-ups and logouts in Chrome.
  • From your IT provider: urgent messages about firewall policy changes, management server issues, or emergency maintenance that affects connectivity.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Smishing + fake download portals: attackers use SMS links and ads to drive installs outside official app stores, then use remote control and data theft to pivot into business accounts.
  • Abuse of trusted identities: stolen passwords/session data and “valid logins” can make attacks look like normal staff activity.
  • Third-party code pull-in: compromised npm packages can be introduced by developers, agencies, or tools used to build websites and internal services, leading to credential/data theft.
  • Security tooling compromise: if a firewall/security management server is exposed, attackers may attempt to gain high privileges and impact multiple systems quickly (including remote access services and site-to-site connectivity).

What to do today

  • Run a 5-minute staff reminder: don’t install apps from text links; use official app stores; if in doubt, forward the message to IT/office manager and delete.
  • Check mobile basics: ensure work Android devices are enrolled in MDM (if you have it), have screen lock, and can be remotely wiped; remove unknown apps and extensions.
  • Protect key accounts: confirm MFA is enabled for email, payroll, banking, and any remote access tools; ensure finance users have stronger controls.
  • Website/app owners: ask whoever maintains your site/apps to confirm they are monitoring third-party dependency risk and have a process to quickly roll back suspicious packages.
  • Log and alert: watch for unusual logins, new device registrations, mass mailbox rules, and unexpected forwarding setup.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we run (or do you run for us) a firewall/security management server that could be exposed to the internet? If yes, what’s the current risk status and what controls isolate it?
  • What is your emergency change process if security tooling needs urgent action (and how will you confirm communications are genuine)?
  • Do you enforce MFA and conditional access for Microsoft 365/Google and admin accounts, including your own support/admin access?
  • For any development/website work: what is your third-party dependency policy (npm/package approval, monitoring, and rollback)?
  • How quickly can you contain a suspected mobile compromise (device isolation, token revocation, password reset, mailbox rule review)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

There is reporting of a critical issue affecting Check Point Security Management / Log Servers. Even if you don’t manage these in-house, it’s worth confirming with your IT provider today whether any of your environments (or theirs, if they manage multiple customers) rely on these systems and whether mitigations or updates have been applied.

One action today

Send a same-day internal note: “Do not install apps from text-message links or ads; if you clicked, report it immediately,” and make sure your IT provider can rapidly revoke sessions/reset passwords for anyone who reports a click.
