Today’s SMB Cyber Brief: VPN gateway attacks + retail incident scam risk

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Tuesday 13 October 2026, 22:13 UK time
Today’s look-out: Remote access gateway compromise and supplier-impersonation scams

What to look out for today

1) Ivanti Connect Secure (ICS) VPN devices under active attack. If your business (or your outsourced IT provider) uses Ivanti Connect Secure for remote access, treat this as urgent. Reporting indicates attackers can gain high-level access without a normal login, and not all fixes are available yet.

2) UK retailer cyber disruption may drive “supplier update” scams. A cyber incident affecting TFG London (Phase Eight, Hobbs, Whistles) has disrupted IT systems. Whenever a well-known brand reports disruption, criminals often exploit the news with fake emails about order issues, payment changes, or “updated bank details”.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Remote access is a high-impact single point of failure. If a VPN/gateway is compromised, attackers may be able to reach file shares, email, finance systems and backups—leading to data theft and ransomware disruption.
  • Brand-related disruption creates cover for fraud. Even if you don’t buy from these retailers, staff may not verify unexpected “account updates” during noisy news cycles, and finance teams are prime targets.

Warning signs

  • Unusual remote access behaviour: logins at odd hours, new admin accounts, unexpected configuration changes, or remote access being “reset”.
  • Staff receiving emails claiming: delayed deliveries/returns, “re-issue payment”, “bank details changed”, “new remittance address”, or urgent requests to confirm account details.
  • Unexpected MFA prompts or password reset emails for remote access, Microsoft 365, finance or payroll systems.
  • IT provider messages that are vague or push you to “approve” emergency access quickly without context.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Gateway compromise → internal access. Attackers target internet-facing remote access appliances because one foothold can expose many internal systems.
  • Follow-on activity. After access, criminals typically try to expand permissions, locate sensitive data, and disable or bypass security controls before disruption.
  • Invoice and payment diversion. Using current disruption headlines, criminals may impersonate suppliers/retailers to redirect payments, capture card details, or harvest logins through lookalike portals.

What to do today

  • Confirm whether you (or your MSP) run Ivanti Connect Secure. If yes, ask for a written status update today: exposure, mitigations applied, and a plan for the remaining fix.
  • Brief finance and front-desk teams. For the next 7 days, treat any “changed bank details” or urgent payment requests as suspicious—verify using known phone numbers (not numbers in the email).
  • Check your emergency access list. Ensure you can reach your IT provider out-of-band (phone), and know who can approve emergency security actions internally.
  • Increase monitoring focus. Ask for heightened alerting on new admin accounts, remote access changes, and unusual outbound connections from remote access infrastructure.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we use Ivanti Connect Secure anywhere (including for third-party/support access)? If yes, which instance(s) and who owns them?
  • What immediate mitigations have been applied, and how are we monitoring for signs of compromise on the gateway?
  • If a fix is pending for part of the issue, what compensating controls are in place until it’s available?
  • What is our rapid response plan if the remote access gateway is suspected compromised (containment steps, password/MFA resets, log review, customer notification decision path)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you rely on Ivanti Connect Secure, treat vendor guidance as time-sensitive: some fixes are available while at least one related issue is still awaiting a fix in reporting. The key SME action is confirming whether you’re exposed and ensuring mitigations and monitoring are in place while patching progresses.

One action today

Today, confirm (in writing) with your IT provider whether your business uses Ivanti Connect Secure and what mitigations/monitoring are in place right now.
