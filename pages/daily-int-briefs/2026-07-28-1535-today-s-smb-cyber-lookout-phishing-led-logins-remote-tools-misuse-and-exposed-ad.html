Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: phishing-led logins, remote tools misuse, and exposed admin interfaces

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Tuesday 28 July 2026, 15:35 UK time
Today’s look-out: Credential theft and remote access abuse (SSO/MFA, remote management tools, exposed admin interfaces)

What to look out for today

Keep a close eye on phishing-led account takeovers (especially where staff use Single Sign-On) and unexpected use of remote management tools on laptops and servers. Also review any internet-exposed management interfaces (server/hosting admin panels and similar) which can leak credentials or become a quick route into your systems.

  • Phishing is still the front door: incident responders are seeing more attacks starting with convincing emails and messages that capture passwords or MFA codes.
  • Legitimate remote tools are being weaponised: attackers often prefer “normal” admin software because it blends in and can evade basic alerting.
  • SSO blast radius: one compromised SSO account can unlock multiple business apps (email, files, HR, finance, CRM).
  • Internet-exposed device management: some server management controllers have been found leaking password hashes when exposed online.
  • Some on-prem IT tooling may be at elevated risk: if you run JetBrains TeamCity on-prem, treat it as high priority to review with your provider.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs and charities often rely on a small number of admin accounts, a handful of key cloud services, and an MSP/IT provider. That means:

  • One login can equal full business access (email + files + finance workflows).
  • Remote access misuse can lead to rapid ransomware or payroll/payment fraud.
  • Disruption risk: if build/IT automation tools are compromised (e.g., CI/CD systems), it can cascade into outages, delayed projects, and supplier impacts.

Warning signs

  • MFA prompts staff didn’t initiate (“Approve sign-in?” fatigue prompts).
  • New email inbox rules, forwarding set up, or “sent items” that staff don’t recognise.
  • Fresh remote access software appearing on endpoints (or remote tools running at odd hours).
  • Admin logins from unusual locations/devices, or multiple failed logins followed by a success.
  • Unexpected changes to payment details, supplier bank changes, or urgent invoice chasing tied to email threads.
  • Servers/devices that should never be reachable from the internet appearing in external scans or alerts.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Phishing for credentials, then using SSO to pivot into multiple SaaS platforms.
  • MFA bypass by social engineering (tricking users into approving prompts) or capturing one-time codes.
  • Abusing legitimate remote management tools to move around the network quietly, deploy scripts, or exfiltrate data.
  • Targeting exposed management interfaces to obtain credential material and attempt password cracking or reuse elsewhere.
  • Compromising on-prem automation/admin platforms to run unauthorised actions and spread further.

What to do today

  • Send a short staff reminder: don’t approve unexpected sign-in prompts; report them immediately.
  • Prioritise identity hardening: ensure phishing-resistant MFA is enabled where available for admin accounts and finance/payroll users.
  • Audit remote access tools: confirm which tools are approved, who can use them, and ensure logging is enabled and reviewed.
  • Check for exposed management interfaces: ask IT to confirm no server/device management consoles are reachable from the internet unless explicitly required and tightly restricted.
  • Review high-value SaaS accounts: finance, payroll, M365/Google, file storage, CRM—confirm recovery emails, MFA methods, and admin roles are correct.

Ask your IT provider

  • Which accounts are protected with phishing-resistant MFA (not just SMS/OTP) and what’s the rollout plan?
  • Do we have a list of approved remote management tools, and alerts for new/unknown ones appearing?
  • Can you show us sign-in anomaly reporting for our email/SSO (impossible travel, unfamiliar devices, repeated MFA prompts)?
  • Are any of our servers/devices exposing management interfaces to the internet? If yes, what is the business justification and how is access restricted?
  • Do we run JetBrains TeamCity on-prem anywhere (including at a supplier/MSP)? If yes, what’s the risk assessment and mitigation plan?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you (or a supplier) run JetBrains TeamCity on-prem, treat today as a prompt to confirm it’s being actively managed and updated. Even if you don’t use it directly, it can sit inside agencies, software suppliers, or IT providers—so it’s worth asking the question as part of supplier assurance.

One action today

Message staff today: “If you get an unexpected sign-in/MFA prompt, do not approve it—report it to IT immediately,” and ensure finance/payroll/admin accounts are prioritised for phishing-resistant MFA.
