Weekend SMB Cyber Brief: breach-driven scams, software supply-chain risk, and VPN exploitation warnings

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Saturday 12 September 2026, 16:30 UK time
Today’s look-out: Breach-driven impersonation scams + supplier/VPN compromise risk

What to look out for today

  • Breach-driven scams: A US state driver database breach has been confirmed and involved stolen police credentials. Expect follow-on impersonation attempts and identity-themed scams.
  • Supplier/supply-chain risk (developer tools): New reporting revisits a malicious campaign involving the Ruby ecosystem (RubyGems/RubyDoc). If you run apps built by third parties, this is a reminder that “software supply chain” issues can land in small businesses via websites and business systems.
  • Remote access/VPN risk: A national cyber authority is warning exploitation of critical Check Point VPN issues is imminent. This is mainly relevant if you (or your IT provider) run Check Point remote access for staff.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Scam volume rises after big breaches: Criminals use breach news to make messages feel legitimate (e.g., “licence check”, “fine”, “ID verification”, “address change”).
  • One compromised login can expose a lot: The driver database incident highlights how stolen credentials can unlock sensitive systems without “hacking” in the traditional sense.
  • VPN compromise can be business-stopping: If an attacker gets into a remote access gateway, they may pivot into email, files, finance systems and backups—leading to ransomware risk and downtime.
  • Supply chain risk isn’t just for big tech: If your website, booking system, or internal tools are maintained by an agency/MSP, issues in common packages can still affect you.

Warning signs

  • Emails/SMS/calls claiming to be from a DMV/licensing/police/council asking for payment, ID documents, or “verification”.
  • Messages pressuring staff to click to view a document, “confirm details”, or “avoid a penalty” outside normal processes.
  • Unexpected MFA prompts or account recovery notifications for Microsoft 365/Google/work email.
  • Remote workers reporting VPN prompts at odd times, repeated disconnects, or being asked to “re-enrol” or install an update via a link.
  • From your IT provider: sudden urgent requests to disable security, add new remote tools quickly, or change firewall/VPN rules without a clear explanation.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Impersonation and data-harvesting: Using breach headlines to trick staff into sharing personal data, payroll details, or payment card info.
  • Account takeover: Reusing stolen passwords, pushing MFA fatigue prompts, or persuading users to approve a sign-in.
  • VPN edge compromise: Targeting internet-facing remote access devices to gain an internal foothold and then moving to file shares and backups.
  • Supply-chain “second hand” compromise: Attacks that start upstream (libraries, plugins, build pipelines) and end up in customer environments through normal updates or deployments.

What to do today

  • Brief staff (5 minutes): This weekend, be extra cautious with any “licence/ID/fine/verification” message and do not click through—verify via known official numbers or your usual internal process.
  • Lock down sign-ins: Ensure MFA is on for email and admin accounts, and review recent sign-in alerts for unusual locations/devices.
  • Check your remote access dependency: Confirm what VPN/remote access platform you use (and whether it’s Check Point). Make sure you know who monitors it out of hours.
  • Make payment changes harder: Reconfirm a “call-back” rule for supplier bank detail changes and urgent invoices.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we use Check Point VPN anywhere (including for third-party support access)? If yes, what is the status of mitigations/updates and what monitoring is in place?
  • What’s our process for detecting and responding to suspicious sign-ins (impossible travel, repeated failures, MFA fatigue) on email and key SaaS?
  • If our website or internal apps are built/maintained by a third party, how do they manage software supply-chain risk (dependency review, code signing, change control)?
  • Do we have an up-to-date offline or immutable backup and when was the last restore test?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

A national cyber authority is warning that exploitation of critical Check Point VPN issues is imminent. If you (or your MSP) run Check Point remote access, treat this as urgent: confirm ownership, confirm the remediation plan, and ensure monitoring is in place for unusual VPN/admin activity.

One action today

Send a short internal note today: “Do not click ‘licence/ID verification/fine’ links or share ID details—verify via known numbers; report any unexpected MFA prompts immediately.”
