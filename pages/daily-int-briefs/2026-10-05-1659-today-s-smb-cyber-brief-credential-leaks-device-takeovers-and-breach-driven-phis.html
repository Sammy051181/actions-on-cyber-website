Today’s SMB Cyber Brief: credential leaks, device takeovers, and breach-driven phishing

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Monday 05 October 2026, 16:59 UK time
Today’s look-out: Credential/identity abuse and breach-driven phishing (plus supplier/device compromise)

What to look out for today

1) Passwords, API keys and tokens leaking from everyday work tools (including code repos, shared docs, scripts and AI-assisted workflows). Stolen credentials remain the quickest route into Microsoft 365, email, finance systems and cloud apps.

2) Internet-connected “edge” and embedded devices being pulled into botnets. Attackers are targeting widely deployed software components used in devices and gateways, aiming to turn them into remotely controlled nodes.

3) Breach ripple effects: a large population-registry breach in Denmark is likely to lead to more convincing phishing, identity checks, and fraud attempts that reference personal data.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Most SME incidents start with account access: one exposed mailbox or cloud login can lead to invoice fraud, payroll changes, data theft or ransomware.
  • SMEs rely on third parties (MSPs, hosted phone systems, routers, cameras, building systems, payment providers). If a device or supplier is compromised, your business can be impacted even if your PCs are fine.
  • Breached personal data makes scams “feel real”. Attackers can tailor messages to staff using correct names, addresses or other details to bypass suspicion.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected multi-factor authentication prompts or “approve sign-in” requests people didn’t initiate.
  • New email forwarding rules, “inbox rules”, or unusual sent items (often used in payment diversion).
  • Suppliers/customers requesting urgent bank detail changes or “new payment portals”, especially via email-only.
  • Unusual logins: odd locations, new devices, sign-ins at strange hours, or repeated failed login attempts.
  • Network oddities from edge devices: internet connection slowing, devices rebooting, or admin credentials no longer working.
  • Staff receiving messages referencing personal information (address/DOB/customer number) to “verify identity” or “reconfirm details”.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Credential harvesting and reuse: leaked passwords/tokens from repositories, shared folders, or copied into tickets/chat can be used to access email, SaaS and cloud consoles.
  • Business email compromise (BEC): once inside a mailbox, attackers monitor invoices and then time a believable “bank details changed” email.
  • Device-to-network stepping stone: compromised edge/IoT devices (routers, gateways, NAS, cameras) can be used as a foothold for scanning, disruption or persistence.
  • Identity and impersonation scams fuelled by breached registry data: fraudsters may pass basic “security questions” or craft highly personalised phishing aimed at finance/HR.

What to do today

  • Run a quick credential hygiene sweep: ensure no passwords/API keys are stored in shared docs, email threads, support tickets, or public/private code repos without protection.
  • Reinforce payment-change controls: require an out-of-band verification step (phone call to a known number, not the email) for any bank detail change.
  • Check email account safeguards: confirm MFA is on, and review recent sign-ins and forwarding/inbox rules for finance and senior accounts.
  • Inventory your edge devices (routers, firewalls, NAS, CCTV/NVR, access control, VoIP, building systems): confirm who manages them, where they are exposed, and that remote admin access is locked down.
  • Brief staff (especially finance/HR/front desk): expect more convincing “verification” or “update your details” messages using personal data.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you show us which accounts are most targeted (failed logins, risky sign-ins) and what’s being monitored?
  • Do we have alerting for new mailbox forwarding rules and suspicious OAuth/app consents?
  • How do we manage secrets (passwords, API keys, service account creds)? Do we have a policy and scanning to detect leaks?
  • Which internet-facing devices do we have and who patches/owns them (including third-party managed equipment)?
  • What is our invoice fraud playbook (verification steps, approvals, and what happens if a mailbox is suspected compromised)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Dell has warned about a serious issue in its System Update (DSU) command-line tool used for deployments. If your IT provider uses DSU in imaging/automation, ask them to confirm they’ve assessed the impact and updated safely, and that admin tooling is restricted to trusted machines and accounts.

One action today

Today, have finance confirm (and document) an out-of-band verification step for any supplier bank-detail change before the next payment run.
