Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: modern phishing for account takeover + risky browser extensions

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Wednesday 16 September 2026, 20:33 UK time
Today’s look-out: Identity theft and session hijacking (phishing kits + malicious browser extensions)

What to look out for today

Two themes are especially relevant to UK SMEs today:

  • More convincing phishing that steals real logins by impersonating trusted services and abusing legitimate sign-in flows (so it can look “normal” after the fact).
  • Browser extension-based theft where a malicious extension in Chrome/Edge can capture credentials, session tokens and sensitive data.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

Most smaller organisations run on cloud identity (Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, line-of-business SaaS). If an attacker gets one working account—or a valid session token—they can often:

  • Read and search email for invoices, payroll, client data and password resets.
  • Set up mailbox forwarding rules to quietly monitor finance conversations.
  • Access cloud files and shared drives, then use that access to pressure payment or deploy ransomware later.
  • Impersonate staff to suppliers/customers, leading to payment diversion scams.

Warning signs

  • Staff report “odd” sign-in prompts, MFA fatigue/push spam, or unexpected verification codes.
  • New browser extensions appear in Chrome/Edge (especially ones staff don’t recall installing), or the browser starts behaving differently.
  • Unusual logins (new country/city, new device) or logins at strange times for that user.
  • New email forwarding rules, suspicious “inbox rules”, or sent items the user doesn’t recognise.
  • Finance team receives a sudden request to “reconfirm” logins, “view shared documents”, or approve a payment change urgently.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Phishing kits can convincingly mimic trusted services and capture credentials and/or tokens, resulting in a real account takeover without obvious malware.
  • Malicious browser extensions can harvest saved passwords, session cookies/tokens and page data—turning a single infected laptop into access across multiple services.
  • Once inside a mailbox, attackers can target invoice and bank detail changes, monitor live deals, and trigger password resets on other systems.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-minute staff warning: don’t approve unexpected sign-in prompts; report them. Don’t install new browser extensions unless approved.
  • Check your admin consoles (Microsoft/Google): review recent sign-in alerts and high-risk logins for key roles (finance, senior leadership, IT, payroll).
  • Audit browser extensions on company machines (and especially finance/admin users). Remove anything unapproved.
  • Confirm your “payment change” process: require an out-of-band call-back to a known number for any bank detail change.
  • Enable/confirm MFA for all accounts, prioritising admin and finance. Where possible, prefer phishing-resistant methods over SMS.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have alerts for impossible travel / risky sign-ins and do you actively monitor them?
  • Can you restrict or centrally manage browser extensions on managed devices (especially for finance and leadership)?
  • How quickly can you lock an account, revoke sessions, and remove malicious inbox rules if we suspect a takeover?
  • Do we have a tested process to reset compromised credentials and validate no persistence remains (e.g., forwarding rules, OAuth/app access)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

No specific patch event is the key issue today. The main risk is credential/session theft and unauthorised browser extensions, so focus on identity controls, device management, and rapid account containment rather than a rush patch cycle.

One action today

Today, audit and remove any unapproved Chrome/Edge extensions on finance and admin users’ devices, and remind staff not to approve unexpected sign-in prompts.
