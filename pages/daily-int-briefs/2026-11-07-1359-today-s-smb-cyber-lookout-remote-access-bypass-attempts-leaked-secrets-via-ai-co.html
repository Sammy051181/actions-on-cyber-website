Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: remote access bypass attempts, leaked secrets via AI coding tools, and backup disruption risk

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Saturday 07 November 2026, 13:59 UK time
Today’s look-out: Remote access & supplier/tooling risk (VPN, AI dev tools, backups)

What to look out for today

  • SonicWall firewall SSL-VPN/MFA bypass being exploited – if your business (or your IT provider) uses SonicWall SonicOS with SSL-VPN enabled, treat this as an urgent exposure.
  • Passwords/API keys accidentally published to GitHub – developer teams using AI coding assistants may have saved “approved commands” (including secrets) into project settings files that then got committed to public repos.
  • Backup platform disruption risk – Veeam has warned that its Software Appliance VMs may fail to boot on hosts with AMD EPYC processors, which could affect recovery when you need it most.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs are frequently hit through remote access (VPN/SSL-VPN), supplier and toolchain mistakes (secrets leaking from repos), and backup availability issues. Any one of these can quickly turn into invoice fraud, data theft, ransomware downtime, or a failed recovery attempt.

Warning signs

  • Remote access/VPN: unexpected login alerts, new admin users, changes to VPN settings, spikes in authentication attempts, or staff reporting account lockouts.
  • Secrets leakage: developers mentioning “don’t ask again” approvals in AI coding tools; new repos created quickly; unusual API usage bills; third-party services flagging exposed tokens.
  • Backup/recovery: failed backup jobs, appliance restart loops, recovery tests failing, or your IT provider postponing routine restore tests due to “appliance issues”.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • VPN bypass/remote access: attackers look for exposed SSL-VPN endpoints and attempt to get in without valid MFA, then move to email, file shares, and finance systems.
  • Exposed secrets in public repos: attackers trawl GitHub for credentials/keys, then log into cloud services, automation tools, payment/CRM platforms, or data stores to steal data or set up persistence.
  • Backup disruption: even without an attacker, a non-booting backup appliance can mean longer outages. Attackers also benefit when recovery is slow or uncertain.

What to do today

  • Check whether you use SonicWall SSL-VPN (including at branch sites or via an outsourced IT provider). If yes, treat as a priority to reduce exposure and confirm monitoring is in place.
  • Do a quick “secrets hygiene” sweep: confirm no passwords/API keys are stored in code repositories; rotate any credentials that might have been copied into scripts/commands and committed.
  • Confirm backup recoverability: if you use Veeam Software Appliance on AMD EPYC hosts, ensure the appliance is healthy and schedule a restore test (even a small one) to prove recovery works.
  • Remind staff: unexpected MFA prompts and login codes are a red flag—report them immediately, don’t approve.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have any SonicWall SSL-VPN exposed to the internet? What has been done this week to reduce risk and monitor for suspicious logins?
  • What alerts are in place for VPN/admin changes and unusual authentication patterns?
  • Do any of our teams (or suppliers) use AI coding assistants in a way that could store commands/settings inside repos? What’s our policy on secrets and repo scanning?
  • Are any of our backups relying on Veeam Software Appliance VMs on AMD EPYC hosts? Have you validated they boot and that restores succeed?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

SonicWall has released fixes for multiple issues in SonicOS, including an SSL-VPN authentication/MFA bypass that is reported as being exploited. If you run SonicWall, prioritise remediation and confirm compensating controls/monitoring until you’re certain exposure is reduced.

One action today

Today, confirm whether your business (or IT provider) has any internet-exposed SonicWall SSL-VPN and, if yes, get written confirmation of the mitigation/updates applied and the monitoring in place.
