SMB Cyber Brief: Portal data exposure, fake VPN browser add‑ons, and SharePoint exploitation

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Thursday 13 August 2026, 09:42 UK time
Today’s look-out: SaaS/customer portal data exposure + browser extension scams + internet-facing server exploitation

What to look out for today

  • Customer portal data theft: A campaign is targeting data exposed to anonymous users via Salesforce Experience Cloud and ServiceNow customer portals. If your customer portal allows guest/anonymous access, review what’s actually visible.
  • Fake Chrome “VPN” extensions: Hundreds of Chrome Web Store extensions have impersonated well-known VPN/proxy brands and routed traffic through third-party proxy infrastructure.
  • SharePoint exploitation activity: Attackers are exploiting a Microsoft SharePoint authentication bypass after public proof-of-concept code was released.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs often rely on customer portals and browser-based tools to serve clients and run operations. If a portal exposes data to unauthenticated visitors, it can quickly become a quiet data breach (customer records, case/ticket info, documents). Meanwhile, a rogue “VPN” extension can intercept browsing and business logins, especially on shared office devices. If you run on-prem or hosted SharePoint, active exploitation can lead to service disruption, data access, and potentially follow-on ransomware risk.

Warning signs

  • Unusual portal activity: spikes in anonymous/guest page views, downloads, or searches; unexpected scraping-like patterns; increased failed login attempts alongside heavy guest browsing.
  • Customer reports that information they shouldn’t see is visible in the portal (other customers’ cases, attachments, knowledge base items, invoices, contact details).
  • Staff browsers showing new VPN/proxy extensions installed “overnight”, or staff reporting pop-ups encouraging them to install a VPN to “access a site”.
  • SharePoint: unexpected sign-in prompts, unusual admin changes, new service accounts, unexplained outbound connections, or sudden performance issues.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Portal exposure abuse: Attackers look for misconfigured portal permissions and enumerate what anonymous/guest users can access, then extract data at scale.
  • Extension-driven interception: A fake VPN/proxy extension can route traffic via attacker-controlled infrastructure, increasing the chance of credential theft, session hijacking, or data leakage.
  • Server foothold: If SharePoint is internet-facing and behind on security updates, attackers may use authentication-bypass techniques to gain access and expand within the environment.

What to do today

  • Salesforce/ServiceNow portal check: Review guest/anonymous access settings and verify what pages, objects, attachments, cases/tickets, and knowledge articles are visible without logging in.
  • Audit Chrome extensions: On business devices, remove unapproved VPN/proxy extensions. Prefer an allow-list approach for extensions where feasible.
  • SharePoint review: Confirm who owns it, whether it’s internet-facing, and whether monitoring is in place for unusual authentication and admin activity.
  • Staff reminder: “Do not install browser VPNs/extensions to ‘fix access’ or ‘improve security’ unless IT has approved it.”

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have a documented list of all customer/community portals (Salesforce/ServiceNow or others) and who reviews their guest access?
  • How do we detect and alert on bulk data extraction from portals (guest browsing spikes, abnormal downloads, unusual queries)?
  • Can we enforce an extension policy for Chrome/Edge (block unknown VPN/proxy add-ons, allow-list only)?
  • Do we run SharePoint anywhere? If yes, is it internet-facing, who patches it, and what’s our plan if indicators of compromise appear?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you run Microsoft SharePoint, treat this week as a priority verification window: exploitation is reported after public PoC release, so confirm your SharePoint security updates are applied and that logging/alerting for suspicious authentication and admin actions is working.

One action today

Audit and lock down guest/anonymous access on your Salesforce Experience Cloud and/or ServiceNow customer portals today, confirming exactly what an unauthenticated visitor can view or download.
