Today’s SME cyber look-out: risky “cheap smart” devices on your network, plus supplier patch urgency

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Saturday 01 August 2026, 18:13 UK time
Today’s look-out: Rogue devices and supplier/platform compromise risk

What to look out for today

1) “Cheap smart” Android TV boxes and similar devices on business Wi‑Fi. Reporting suggests some low-cost Android TV boxes may come with apps that can turn the device into a proxy node, effectively using your internet connection as part of someone else’s network.

2) Supplier/platform compromise risk from widely used tools. If your organisation (or your website/app supplier) uses platforms like Adobe Campaign Classic, Ruby on Rails apps, or JetBrains TeamCity, today’s newsflow includes items that can translate into quick real-world compromise and business disruption if left unmanaged.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Business disruption: If your broadband gets abused as a proxy, you can see slow internet, unstable video calls, and knock-on issues for cloud accounting, VoIP and card terminals.
  • Reputational/operational fallout: If your connection is used for abuse, you may face complaints, account restrictions, or extra scrutiny from providers—even if you’re a victim.
  • Supply-chain exposure: Even if you don’t run these tools directly, your web developer, marketing agency, or MSP might. Their compromise can become your incident (website defacement, data exposure, email/account takeover, downtime).

Warning signs

  • Unknown set-top boxes, “streaming sticks”, or Android TV devices plugged into office TVs or meeting rooms.
  • Unexplained spikes in internet usage, persistent slowness, or broadband/router logs showing unusual outbound connections.
  • Users reporting that “everything is slow” at the same time each day, or video calls failing more than usual.
  • Sudden account lockouts or warnings from your ISP, email provider, or security tools about suspicious traffic.
  • For supplier-managed websites/apps: unexpected site changes, new admin users, odd scheduled jobs, or unexplained outages.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Pre-installed or bundled apps on cheap devices can run in the background and enrol the device into a proxy network.
  • Opportunistic scanning and fast follow-on attacks often happen after high-profile security update news—attackers target exposed services and poorly managed third-party deployments.
  • Abuse of “edge” devices (TV boxes, meeting-room kit) because they’re rarely monitored, rarely updated, and often share the same network as business PCs.

What to do today

  • Do a 15-minute device sweep: check meeting rooms and TVs for unknown Android TV boxes/streaming devices; remove anything not approved.
  • Check your router/Wi‑Fi admin list: identify unfamiliar devices; block them and change Wi‑Fi passwords if you find anything suspicious.
  • Separate networks: ensure “guest/IoT” Wi‑Fi is separate from business laptops, finance machines and servers.
  • Supplier check-in: ask your web/app/marketing providers to confirm whether they use Rails, Adobe Campaign Classic, or TeamCity and what their update/monitoring plan is.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you provide an up-to-date inventory of devices on our network (including IoT/meeting-room kit)?
  • Do we have separate VLANs/SSIDs for business devices vs guest/IoT? If not, what’s the quickest safe approach?
  • How do you detect unusual outbound traffic that might indicate proxying or bot activity?
  • Do any of our systems—or our suppliers’ systems you manage—use Adobe Campaign Classic, Rails apps, or JetBrains TeamCity? Who owns patching and how quickly do you act on urgent vendor alerts?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

There is active reporting around urgent security updates for commonly used platforms (including JetBrains TeamCity, Adobe Campaign Classic, and Rails applications). This is most relevant if you run these tools directly or rely on an agency/MSP that does—today is a good day to confirm ownership, timelines, and evidence of updates rather than assuming it’s handled.

One action today

Check your router/Wi‑Fi device list today and remove/block any unknown Android TV/streaming boxes or other unapproved devices.
