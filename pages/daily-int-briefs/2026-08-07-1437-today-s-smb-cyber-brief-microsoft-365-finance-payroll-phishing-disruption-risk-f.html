Today’s SMB cyber brief: Microsoft 365 finance/payroll phishing + disruption risk from port cyberattack

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Friday 07 August 2026, 14:37 UK time
Today’s look-out: Microsoft 365 account takeover aimed at payroll/finance, plus supplier/logistics disruption scams

What to look out for today

1) Microsoft 365 account-takeover phishing aimed at finance and payroll. Researchers are reporting a widespread email-driven campaign using “adversary-in-the-middle” (AitM) techniques to hijack Microsoft 365 sign-ins, then hunt for payroll and finance email conversations and key approvers.

2) Supplier/logistics disruption ripple effects. North Carolina Ports has confirmed a cyberattack disrupting operations. Even if you’re not directly connected, incidents like this often trigger delivery delays and a wave of “we’ve changed our bank details / reroute this shipment” scams.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Business email compromise risk: If an attacker gets into a Microsoft 365 mailbox used for invoices, payroll, or supplier payments, they can quietly watch threads and time a believable payment diversion.
  • Payroll impact: Access to payroll-related emails can help criminals target salary payment changes, starter/leaver details, or HR/finance approval chains.
  • Operational disruption: Logistics disruption can create genuine confusion, making it easier for criminals to introduce “urgent changes” to delivery or payment instructions.

Warning signs

  • Staff report a Microsoft 365 sign-in page that looks normal but arrives via an unexpected link in an email.
  • Unusual login prompts or repeated sign-in requests, especially when the user wasn’t trying to sign in.
  • New or unexpected inbox rules, mail forwarding, or “read” status changes on finance/payroll emails.
  • Suppliers or freight/logistics contacts suddenly asking for bank-detail changes, rerouting, or “pay to release goods” charges.
  • Pressure tactics: “must be today”, “director approved”, “confidential”, or requests to bypass normal checks.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • AitM phishing for Microsoft 365: Attackers trick users into signing in via a page that relays the session, letting criminals take over the account even when the user enters correct credentials.
  • Finance workflow surveillance: Once inside, criminals look for invoice approvals, payroll runs, and regular payment patterns, then impersonate a supplier/colleague or reply within an existing email thread.
  • Disruption-themed social engineering: During real port/logistics issues, criminals send plausible emails about delayed containers, alternative delivery routes, and urgent fees—often coupled with new payment instructions.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-minute staff alert to finance, payroll, HR and reception/admin: do not sign in to Microsoft 365 from email links; use a bookmarked link or company portal.
  • Verify payment changes out-of-band: any new bank details or “urgent” payment request must be confirmed by calling a known number from your address book (not the email).
  • Check for mailbox tampering on finance/payroll accounts: unexpected forwarding, inbox rules, or newly-added delegates.
  • Prepare for delivery delays: if you rely on ports/logistics, confirm expected shipments using known contacts and be cautious of “fee to release” or last-minute change requests.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have conditional access / sign-in risk alerts enabled for Microsoft 365, and who monitors them?
  • Can you quickly report recent sign-ins, new inbox rules, and forwarding for our finance/payroll mailboxes?
  • Do we enforce strong phishing-resistant authentication for high-risk users (finance, payroll, admins), and can we tighten it without breaking workflows?
  • What is our process to contain a suspected mailbox takeover (session revoke, password reset, MFA reset, rule cleanup, customer/supplier notification)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

No broad SME patch action is the main story today. The priority is reducing account takeover and payment-diversion risk in Microsoft 365 (process controls, sign-in monitoring, and rapid response for suspicious mailbox activity).

One action today

Send a targeted internal alert to finance/payroll/HR: ‘No Microsoft 365 sign-ins from email links; verify any bank-detail or payment change by calling a known number.’
