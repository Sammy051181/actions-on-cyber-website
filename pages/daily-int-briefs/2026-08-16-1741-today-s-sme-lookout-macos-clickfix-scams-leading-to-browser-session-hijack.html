Today’s SME lookout: macOS ‘ClickFix’ scams leading to browser session hijack

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Sunday 16 August 2026, 17:41 UK time
Today’s look-out: Fake fix prompts (ClickFix) and macOS browser session theft

What to look out for today

Be alert for Mac users being tricked by “ClickFix” style prompts (fake error or verification messages) that lead to installing or running something to “fix” a problem. Reporting today highlights a new macOS information-stealer (AmnesiaStealer) that can take over a victim’s browser session via remote interaction.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

Many SMEs rely on browser sessions for everything: Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace, bookkeeping, payroll, CRMs, online banking, card payments dashboards and social media. If an attacker can hijack a live browser session, they may be able to access accounts even without knowing the password, and quickly attempt:

  • Payment diversion (changing payee details, adding new beneficiaries, or initiating transfers)
  • Invoice fraud using real email threads from inside your mailbox
  • Taking over SaaS admin consoles to add new users or reset MFA
  • Data theft from shared drives and customer systems

Warning signs

  • A pop-up or web page insisting you must “verify”, “fix”, or “update” immediately to continue
  • Instructions telling staff to copy/paste steps, run a command, install a “support tool”, or allow unusual permissions
  • Browser behaviour that feels “possessed”: tabs opening/closing, unexpected clicks, or settings changing
  • Unexpected logins, new devices, or MFA prompts that nobody initiated
  • New payees/beneficiaries added in banking or payment platforms, or rules/forwarders added in email

How attackers may exploit the situation

Attackers use convincing web prompts and “helpful” instructions to get a Mac user to run something that installs an information-stealer. Once in, the attacker’s goal is to take over the browser session to access business accounts and move quickly before anyone notices—often targeting finance, inboxes, and admin portals.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-minute staff warning: “Never follow ‘fix’ prompts from websites. If you’re told to install/run anything to continue, stop and contact IT.”
  • Prioritise finance and admin users on Macs: remind them to treat unexpected MFA prompts as a red flag and report immediately.
  • Check key accounts for tampering: email forwarding rules, new inbox rules, new users in admin consoles, and new payees in banking/payment tools.
  • Reinforce payment controls: call-back verification for any bank detail change or new beneficiary, using a known number (not the email signature).

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have Mac endpoint security in place, and are alerts monitored (including for browser credential/session theft behaviours)?
  • Can we enforce least privilege on Macs (staff not running with admin rights day-to-day)?
  • Do we have conditional access / sign-in alerts for our main SaaS (M365/Google, finance, CRM)?
  • What’s our fast response step if someone reports a suspicious “fix prompt” or unexpected MFA (isolation, password resets, session revocation)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

No specific patch issue is the focus here. This is mainly a social engineering + malware risk: make sure macOS and browsers are set to update automatically, and prioritise user training and sign-in monitoring.

One action today

Message staff today: “If a website tells you to ‘fix/verify’ by installing or running anything, stop—close the tab and report it to IT.”
