Today’s SMB Cyber Brief: social engineering breaches, FortiGate lockouts, and software supply-chain risk

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Thursday 08 October 2026, 15:34 UK time
Today’s look-out: Credential theft & supplier/SaaS trust (social engineering, VPN access, and software supply chain)

What to look out for today

  • Social engineering + credential theft: ASOS has linked a recent breach to social engineering and stolen credentials. Expect copycat phishing that claims to be “from ASOS” (or other retailers) and tries to harvest logins or payment details.
  • Remote access disruption: The FBI is warning that FortiGate / SSL VPN attacks are still ongoing and can lock out legitimate administrators. If your business relies on Fortinet VPN for remote work, this is a business continuity risk.
  • Software supply-chain exposure: A compromised npm package (“tensorlake”) was reported to have been tampered with to steal credentials and secrets. This matters if you build software in-house, use web agencies, or have suppliers shipping code into your environment.
  • “Ransomware recovery” trust risk: A ransomware recovery company CEO has been charged over alleged secret ransom payments while claiming proprietary recovery. This is a caution for any SME shopping for emergency incident support.
  • Phishing kits getting smarter: Reporting on the “Wazza” phishkit highlights increasingly sophisticated phishing infrastructure designed to evade simple blocking and filtering.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs tend to be hit hardest by credential theft (email takeover, payroll diversion, invoice fraud) and by remote access outages (VPN disruption stops staff working). Supply-chain issues (like a compromised code package) can also arrive via web developers, MSPs, and SaaS integrations without warning.

Warning signs

  • Staff receiving “account security”, “password reset”, “suspicious activity” or “refund” emails/SMS that create urgency and push a login link.
  • Unexpected MFA prompts or users reporting they are suddenly “locked out”.
  • VPN/admin consoles showing new admin users, changed permissions, or unexplained configuration changes.
  • Developers or suppliers reporting a “dependency update” that must be applied immediately, especially if details are vague.
  • New OAuth/app connections appearing in Microsoft 365/Google/SaaS that nobody recognises.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Brand-based phishing: using high-profile incidents (like ASOS) as a believable story to steal passwords and payment details.
  • Credential replay: using stolen passwords against business email, payroll, accounting, and file-sharing tools.
  • Remote access takeover or disruption: targeting exposed VPN gateways to gain access or deny access (locking out admins) and then attempting follow-on fraud or ransomware.
  • Supply-chain entry: compromised packages or “helpful updates” used to harvest credentials, tokens, API keys, and secrets that unlock wider access.
  • Dubious incident support: during ransomware events, stressed businesses may engage “recovery” firms without clear terms—creating financial, legal, and reputational risk.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-minute staff warning: don’t click “account security” links from retailers/banks; use bookmarks/known URLs; report anything that asks for urgent login/payment details.
  • Protect business email: ensure MFA is enabled and that finance/payroll mailboxes have extra scrutiny (rules/forwarding checks).
  • Confirm remote access ownership: if you use Fortinet VPN, ask your IT provider to confirm monitoring for admin lockout attempts and that recovery access is in place.
  • Check supplier/dev pathways: if you have developers (internal or external), ask what they do to prevent malicious dependency updates and how secrets are stored/rotated.
  • Review third-party app access (OAuth/app grants): remove anything unused or unknown, especially anything with mailbox/file access.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have any FortiGate / SSL VPN exposure, and what are we doing to prevent admin lockouts and detect suspicious admin activity?
  • Do we have break-glass admin access for Microsoft 365/Google and for VPN/firewall management if normal admins are locked out?
  • How quickly would you spot and respond to new email forwarding rules, suspicious login locations, or MFA fatigue attempts?
  • Which suppliers can push code or integrations into our environment, and how do we control/approve dependency updates?
  • If we suffer ransomware, what is our agreed approach to third-party “recovery” services—who we would use, what we will/won’t pay for, and what approvals are required?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If your business uses Fortinet FortiGate/SSL VPN, treat today’s FBI warning as a prompt to confirm your provider has applied relevant vendor guidance and that internet-facing remote access is tightly controlled and monitored. Avoid DIY changes if you don’t manage the firewall day-to-day—get confirmation in writing from your IT partner.

One action today

Send a same-day staff note: “Ignore urgent ‘account security/refund’ messages—don’t use embedded links; go via saved bookmarks; report anything asking for logins or payment changes.”
