Today’s SMB Cyber Brief: Deepfake payment calls, Android banking app swapping, and an Ivanti remote access risk

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Wednesday 14 October 2026, 13:52 UK time
Today’s look-out: Impersonation-led fraud and remote access compromise

What to look out for today

  • Deepfake / voice-clone impersonation aimed at rushing payments, changing supplier bank details, or triggering helpdesk password resets (e.g., “CFO needs it done before close of play”).
  • Android banking malware that replaces apps with modified lookalikes to steal credentials and bypass in-app checks.
  • Remote access/VPN supplier risk: if you (or your IT provider) use Ivanti Connect Secure, there is a newly exploited issue that attackers may use for credential theft and data access.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

These threats don’t rely on sophisticated hacking of your systems first. They exploit people, process gaps, and dependency on devices and suppliers (banks, payroll, MSPs, remote access tools). SMEs are particularly exposed because finance approvals and IT support are often handled by a small number of busy staff, and mobile devices are commonly used for banking and MFA.

Warning signs

  • Urgency + secrecy: “Do it now”, “don’t loop anyone else in”, “I’m in a meeting”.
  • Channel shift: requests arriving via WhatsApp/Teams/voice note instead of normal email/ticket routes.
  • Payment or bank-detail changes close to weekends, month-end, or just before a ‘deal’ supposedly completes.
  • Helpdesk reset pressure: a “senior” caller pushing for an immediate password/MFA reset or new device enrolment.
  • On Android: banking app icon/name changes, unexpected prompts to “update” outside the Play Store, accessibility/permissions prompts that don’t make sense.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Voice-clone social engineering to authorise a fraudulent supplier payment or to change bank details, using realistic audio/video to bypass “it sounded like them”.
  • Helpdesk compromise by impersonating staff to reset credentials, then pivoting into email, file storage, finance systems, and payroll.
  • Mobile banking compromise through Android malware that replaces legitimate banking apps with modified versions to capture credentials.
  • Remote access compromise where vulnerable edge appliances are targeted for credential theft and data exfiltration, potentially impacting multiple customers if an MSP hosts or manages the service.

What to do today

  • Set a “no voice-only approvals” rule for payments, supplier bank changes, and helpdesk resets. Require a second, independent check-back using a known number from your address book (not the caller’s).
  • Run a quick 15-minute drill: can your finance team and IT support explain exactly how they would verify a request if the caller “sounds like” the CEO/CFO?
  • Mobile hygiene: remind staff to only install/update apps via the official app store, and to report any banking app that looks different or asks for odd permissions.
  • Dependency check: confirm whether your business (or your IT provider) uses Ivanti Connect Secure anywhere (office, hosted environment, or third-party access).

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we use Ivanti Connect Secure directly, or do you use it to manage our access? If yes, what’s the current status (patched/mitigated/monitored)?
  • What logs and alerts would show suspicious authentication, unusual data access, or mass credential resets?
  • What is our helpdesk identity verification process for password/MFA resets (especially for directors and finance staff)?
  • Do we have payment change controls (dual approval, supplier call-back, verification notes) that can’t be bypassed by urgency?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

CISA has warned of a newly exploited issue affecting Ivanti Connect Secure (CVE-2026-104935). SMEs should treat this as a dependency risk: if you or your MSP use Ivanti, ask for confirmation of update actions and what monitoring is in place for credential theft or suspicious access.

One action today

Send a same-day memo to finance and admin staff: “No payment approvals or supplier bank changes based on a voice call alone—must be verified via a call-back to a known number plus second-person approval.”
