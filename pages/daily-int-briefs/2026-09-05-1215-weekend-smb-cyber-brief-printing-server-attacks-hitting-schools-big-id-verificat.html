Weekend SMB Cyber Brief: printing server attacks hitting schools + big ID verification supplier breach claims

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Saturday 05 September 2026, 12:15 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier breach ripple effects + credential theft via printing/IT systems

What to look out for today

1) Schools/universities: printing software (PaperCut) being actively exploited to steal credentials. If you run a school, college, or support education customers, treat unexpected account lockouts, new admin users, or unusual print server activity as urgent.

2) Supplier breach ripple effects: an identity verification provider (IDScan) is facing lawsuits over an alleged breach affecting a very large volume of driving licence data. Regardless of jurisdiction, this kind of incident typically triggers follow-on scams aimed at customers and the public.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Credential theft leads to business disruption fast: once attackers get staff passwords, they often move into email, file storage and finance systems to run invoice fraud or deploy ransomware.
  • Schools and charities are common “low-friction” targets: smaller IT teams and shared systems (print/identity/HR/payroll) can become the single point of failure.
  • Supplier incidents create believable lures: criminals recycle breach headlines into “verify your identity”, “download your report”, or “confirm your payroll/bank details” messages.

Warning signs

  • Sudden password reset emails, MFA prompts, or account lockouts that users did not initiate.
  • New or changed admin accounts, especially on print servers or shared service accounts.
  • Staff reporting odd pop-ups, unexpected logins, or “weird” behaviour when printing.
  • Incoming emails/texts claiming to be from an identity-check provider, DVLA-style services, or “document verification” firms asking you to click a link or upload ID.
  • Customers/parents/service users saying they received messages referencing a “licence leak” or “IDScan breach” and asking if it’s real.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Print/IT foothold to steal usernames and passwords: attackers target a central service (like a print management server) to capture credentials and then pivot into email and cloud services.
  • Follow-on phishing and social engineering: breach news is used to pressure people into “urgent verification” or “secure your account” actions.
  • Finance-targeting: once in email, criminals monitor invoice traffic and attempt payment diversion (“new bank details”) or request gift cards/urgent transfers.

What to do today

  • Tell staff what to ignore: no one should click identity-verification links from unsolicited emails/SMS. Use known, bookmarked portals or call using known numbers.
  • Prioritise admin account safety: ensure MFA is on for admin accounts and that admin access is limited to named users (no shared admin logins).
  • Check your email rules: spot-check for suspicious forwarding rules or mailbox delegates (a common sign of compromise).
  • For schools/education IT: review print server and PaperCut administrative access logs and recent changes; escalate anything unusual immediately.
  • Supplier due diligence: if you use any ID verification services, confirm what data they hold about your organisation/customers, and what their breach notification process is.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we run PaperCut (or support clients who do)? If yes, what monitoring is in place for unusual admin activity and sign-ins?
  • Are our admin accounts protected with MFA and separate “day-to-day” accounts (no browsing/email on admin accounts)?
  • Can you check for suspicious mailbox forwarding rules and unusual sign-in locations in Microsoft 365/Google Workspace?
  • What is our process for supplier breach alerts (ID verification, payroll, payments, CRM)? Who decides when we warn staff/customers?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

There are reports of active exploitation against PaperCut using newly disclosed flaws. If your organisation (or a school you support) uses PaperCut, treat this as urgent: confirm who owns the patching/mitigation decision and ensure the system is being monitored for suspicious access while updates are applied.

One action today

Send a short internal alert: “Do not click identity-verification links from unsolicited emails/SMS; report any unexpected password/MFA prompts immediately.”
