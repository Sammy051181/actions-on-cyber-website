SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief: Microsoft 365 update disruption, GitHub Actions supply-chain risk, and AI agent data leakage

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Saturday 26 September 2026, 17:25 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier/SaaS disruption and software supply-chain risk (plus AI tool data leakage)

What to look out for today

  • Microsoft 365 update disruption: Microsoft has paused a Microsoft 365 update (KB5002907) after reports it deactivated or removed perpetual Office 2016/2019 installations. Expect possible licensing/app availability issues on affected PCs.
  • Software supply-chain risk (GitHub Actions): Two third-party GitHub Actions previously compromised were re-enabled while still pointing to malicious code, highlighting ongoing risk from CI/CD dependencies.
  • AI agent data leakage: OpenAI says its AI agents accidentally uploaded user-provided images to third-party image-hosting services during tasks. Treat AI “agent” features as a data-sharing channel unless proven otherwise.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs depend heavily on a small number of suppliers (Microsoft 365, cloud services, MSPs, and developer tooling). When an update breaks licensing, a third-party component is compromised, or an AI tool shares files externally, the impact is immediate: staff downtime, missed deadlines, invoicing delays, and potential data protection headaches.

Warning signs

  • Office suddenly deactivated/unlicensed, prompts to re-activate, or Office apps missing on machines that previously had Office 2016/2019 installed.
  • Unexpected build/deployment behaviour (new network connections, new packages, altered artefacts) after no intentional change to your code.
  • Developers reporting “pipeline weirdness”: failed builds, new steps appearing, or secrets/variables behaving oddly.
  • Staff using AI agents for “helpful tasks” (research, drafting, form filling) and attaching screenshots/images containing customer data, ID documents, invoices, medical/safeguarding info, or internal system screens.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Opportunistic phishing during disruption: If users see Office licensing problems, attackers may send fake “Microsoft reactivation” emails/messages to steal passwords and MFA codes.
  • Compromised automation components: Malicious GitHub Actions can be used to access build secrets, publish tampered releases, or move into connected cloud environments.
  • Data exposure via AI workflows: If AI agents upload files/images to third-party services, sensitive data can leak outside your organisation’s control and retention policies.

What to do today

  • Microsoft 365/Office check: Ask whoever manages IT to confirm whether your estate received the paused update and whether any devices show Office deactivation/removal symptoms. Tell staff to report Office “activation” pop-ups rather than trying random fixes.
  • Phishing heads-up: Warn staff: do not follow “reactivate Office” links from email/Teams/SMS. Use your normal internal IT route.
  • GitHub Actions hygiene (if you develop or use CI/CD): Inventory third-party Actions in use and temporarily block/limit unpinned or newly re-enabled dependencies until reviewed. Ensure pipelines use least privilege and that secrets are tightly scoped.
  • AI agent guardrails: Remind teams: do not upload customer/staff documents or screenshots into AI agent tools unless explicitly approved. Prefer redacted samples. Confirm where files are stored and shared.

Ask your IT provider

  • Have any of our PCs been impacted by the paused Microsoft 365 update, and what’s the recovery plan if Office was removed/deactivated?
  • Do we have monitoring for sudden spikes in Microsoft sign-in failures or suspicious “licence/activation” themed phishing attempts?
  • If we use GitHub (or any CI/CD), are third-party Actions pinned/approved, and do we have a process to rapidly disable risky pipeline dependencies?
  • Do we have a written policy on AI tools/agents covering what data is allowed, and do we have an approved list of tools for staff?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

CISA has flagged active exploitation of flaws affecting Microsoft SharePoint and MikroTik RouterOS. Even if you don’t manage these directly, ask your IT provider/MSP to confirm whether you have any SharePoint servers or MikroTik routers in your environment (including at branch sites) and whether mitigation is in place.

One action today

Send a same-day internal note: “If Office asks you to re-activate or looks unlicensed, don’t click links or enter passwords—report it to IT immediately,” and have IT confirm whether KB5002907 reached your devices.
