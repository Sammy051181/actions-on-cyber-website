SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief: Hosting and cloud account takeover risks to watch today

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Wednesday 23 September 2026, 13:45 UK time
Today’s look-out: Account takeover via hosting panels, cloud logins and third‑party MFA/OAuth tricks

What to look out for today

Today’s themes are about account takeover and supplier/managed service risk across common SME platforms:

  • Web hosting control panels (cPanel): issues reported that could allow a hosting account to escalate control on a server or interfere with other sites on shared hosting.
  • WordPress and network devices: a threat actor has been reported exploiting WordPress and Zyxel switch weaknesses to access data.
  • Cloud identity (Google Workspace): real-world breach patterns include social engineering and malicious OAuth apps ("Sign in with Google" style approvals) to gain access without “breaking in”.
  • Login/MFA supply chain abuse: researchers describe how a rogue external MFA provider, if registered by someone with privileged access, could capture passwords during legitimate logins.
  • Remote/network management platforms: an actively exploited issue is being patched for Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (on-prem deployments).

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs often rely on outsourced IT, shared hosting, and cloud logins for day-to-day operations (email, website, booking systems, payments, and file storage). If a single admin account or supplier platform is compromised, attackers can:

  • take over email and use it to send believable invoice/payment-change fraud,
  • swap website content or add hidden redirect/malware,
  • steal contact lists and sensitive documents,
  • disrupt operations (lockouts, deletions, business email compromise),
  • use your brand/website to scam customers.

Warning signs

  • Google/Microsoft login prompts that feel “normal” but unusual timing (e.g., after an email urging you to “re-authenticate” or “approve access”).
  • Unexpected new app connections to your Google Workspace (new OAuth app approvals), or admin consent prompts you didn’t request.
  • New MFA/SSO settings or changes to authentication providers that your team didn’t plan.
  • Website changes: unexplained redirects, new admin users in WordPress, or plugin/theme changes you didn’t authorise.
  • Hosting anomalies: sudden password resets, unfamiliar control panel logins, or support tickets you didn’t raise.
  • Network management alerts you can’t explain (especially if you use VeloCloud/SD-WAN tooling via an IT provider).

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Social engineering + OAuth: trick staff into approving a malicious app so the attacker can read mail/files without needing the password again.
  • Admin pathway abuse: if an attacker gains privileged/admin access (via phishing, password reuse, or supplier compromise), they may change MFA/SSO settings to intercept or retain access.
  • Shared hosting blast radius: weaknesses in hosting platforms can turn “one compromised account” into broader server control, affecting multiple sites.
  • Known exploited pathways on edge/management systems: attackers target internet-exposed management consoles because they can lead to wide access quickly.

What to do today

  • Review cloud app access: check your Google Workspace (and other SaaS) for newly authorised third-party apps and remove anything not recognised.
  • Protect admin accounts: ensure admin logins use strong MFA and are limited to the minimum number of people; avoid using day-to-day accounts as admins.
  • Website/hosting quick audit: confirm who has cPanel/hosting logins, remove unused accounts, and verify WordPress admin users and plugins are expected.
  • Confirm change control: require a second person check for changes to MFA/SSO settings, payment details, and DNS/hosting settings.
  • Tell staff what to report: any unexpected “approve access” prompts, MFA changes, or login alerts should be reported immediately.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do any of our sites or client systems sit on cPanel or use WP Toolkit, and what’s your process to apply urgent fixes on shared hosting?
  • How do you monitor for and respond to new OAuth app consents or suspicious mailbox access in Google Workspace?
  • Who can change our MFA/SSO configuration, and what approval/audit trail exists for those changes?
  • Do we use (directly or via you) VeloCloud Orchestrator (on-prem) or similar network management tooling, and is it internet-exposed?
  • If our email/admin account is taken over, what is the first-hour plan (lockout steps, session revocation, sign-in log review, customer/supplier comms)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you use a hosting provider running cPanel (or you self-host), ask for confirmation that fixes for the reported cPanel issues have been applied. Separately, if your IT stack includes Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (on-prem), treat this as urgent because it has been reported as actively exploited—your provider should confirm patching and that management interfaces are not openly exposed.

One action today

Review and remove any unfamiliar third‑party app/OAuth access in your Google Workspace (and other key SaaS) today, and confirm only approved admins can grant new app consents.
