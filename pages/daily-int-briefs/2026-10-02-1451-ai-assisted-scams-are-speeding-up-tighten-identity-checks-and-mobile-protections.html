AI-assisted scams are speeding up: tighten identity checks and mobile protections

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Friday 02 October 2026, 14:51 UK time
Today’s look-out: AI-boosted phishing and faster attacker follow-through (especially around identity and mobile devices)

What to look out for today

  • More polished phishing and invoice/payment-change scams (better wording, fewer mistakes, more believable context).
  • Faster attacker “follow-through” after a click: quicker attempts to access mailboxes, reset passwords, and set up forwarding rules.
  • Mobile devices as a weak link, especially where staff install apps that request powerful permissions (including Accessibility-style controls).

Why this matters to smaller businesses

Recent reporting from major vendors suggests attackers are currently getting value from AI faster than defenders, meaning scams and compromise activity can move from “first message” to “account takeover” more quickly. For SMEs, that tends to show up as: compromised email leading to fraudulent payment requests, unauthorised changes to supplier bank details, and staff being manipulated into sharing one-time passcodes or approving sign-ins. Separately, Google is tightening protections on Android to reduce abuse of high-privilege phone features often used in fraud.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected urgency: “pay today”, “account will be closed”, “director needs this now”, or “we’ve changed our bank”.
  • Unusual sign-in activity: prompts for MFA approvals you didn’t initiate, or messages like “here’s your login code” you weren’t expecting.
  • Supplier/customer email tone suddenly changes (even if grammar is perfect): new payment instructions, new contact details, or requests to move conversations off email.
  • Mailbox behaviour changes: missing emails, new rules/forwarding, or colleagues receiving odd replies “from you”.
  • Mobile red flags: staff phones running new “helper/utility” apps that ask for extensive permissions, or users reporting pop-ups asking to enable special access.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • High-volume, high-quality social engineering: AI helps criminals tailor messages to your role (finance, reception, HR, ops) and mimic common business language.
  • Rapid iteration: criminals test what works (subject lines, lures, fake “secure document” prompts) and quickly refine campaigns.
  • Speed after initial access: once an email or device is compromised, attackers may quickly try to change payment details, add inbox rules, or use the account to target customers/suppliers.
  • Abuse of powerful phone features: malicious apps have historically used high-privilege services to enable fraud; Android’s Advanced Protection aims to reduce this pathway for people who enable it.

What to do today

  • Reinforce a “call-back” rule for money and bank changes: if a bank detail or payment instruction changes, staff must verify using a known phone number (not one in the email).
  • Remind staff: never share one-time codes and don’t approve sign-in prompts they didn’t start.
  • Do a quick mailbox hygiene check for key roles (finance, payroll, info@, bookings@): review forwarding and inbox rules.
  • Mobile step: for staff who handle payments/admin accounts on Android, consider enabling/encouraging Advanced Protection and reducing unnecessary app permissions.
  • Update your “reporting path”: make sure everyone knows exactly how to report a suspicious email or MFA prompt (and that reporting is praised, not punished).

Ask your IT provider

  • What monitoring/alerts do we have for new mailbox forwarding rules and suspicious sign-ins on business email?
  • Do we have a tested process to rapidly contain an email takeover (reset sessions, remove rules, check sent items, warn contacts)?
  • Which staff should be placed into a higher protection tier (finance, directors, payroll) and what extra controls apply?
  • What is our current position on mobile device protection for staff accessing email/accounts (recommended settings, app control, guidance for Android Advanced Protection)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

No specific SME software patch focus from today’s items. The practical takeaway is operational: tighten identity checks (especially for payment changes) and ensure email/mobile account protections are switched on and monitored, because attacker speed and message quality are increasing.

One action today

Today, remind all staff: any request to change bank details or make an urgent payment must be verified via a call-back to a known number (not the email) before paying.
