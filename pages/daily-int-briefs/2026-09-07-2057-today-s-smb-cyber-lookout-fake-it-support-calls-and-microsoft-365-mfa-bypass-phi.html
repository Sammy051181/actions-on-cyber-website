Today’s SMB cyber lookout: fake IT support calls and Microsoft 365 “MFA bypass” phishing

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Monday 07 September 2026, 20:57 UK time
Today’s look-out: Microsoft 365 account takeover, data theft and extortion via vishing and AitM phishing

What to look out for today

Be on alert for fake IT/helpdesk phone calls and realistic Microsoft 365 login prompts designed to capture sign-ins and session tokens (so attackers can get into email and files even when MFA is enabled).

  • Phone-based “IT support” or “Microsoft” calls pressuring staff to approve sign-ins, read out codes, or visit a link.
  • Phishing pages that appear to “pass” MFA by intercepting tokens during sign-in (adversary-in-the-middle style attacks).
  • Follow-on behaviour: data theft from Microsoft 365/other SaaS, then extortion (threatening to leak data).

Why this matters to smaller businesses

Most SMEs depend on Microsoft 365 for email, Teams, SharePoint/OneDrive and invoices/quotes. A single compromised mailbox can lead to:

  • Invoice fraud (attackers watching email threads and changing bank details).
  • Payroll/HR data exposure if files are stored in OneDrive/SharePoint.
  • Business disruption if accounts are locked, rules are changed, or files are exfiltrated.
  • Reputational damage if client communications are accessed.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected calls claiming to be “IT”, your MSP, or Microsoft urging immediate action.
  • Requests to approve an MFA prompt you didn’t initiate (“Just accept it so we can fix your account”).
  • Pressure to install remote support tools or to visit a “secure” login page sent by SMS/email.
  • Unusual Microsoft 365 sign-in alerts (new location/device) or repeated MFA prompts.
  • New or changed inbox rules (e.g., auto-forwarding, moving emails to RSS/Archive, marking as read).
  • Colleagues or customers reporting odd emails “from you” requesting payment changes or gift cards.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Vishing (voice phishing) to build trust and steer users to a login link or to approve MFA.
  • AitM phishing to capture credentials and session tokens during a legitimate-looking sign-in flow.
  • Residential proxy sign-ins to make access look more “normal” and bypass simple geo-blocking.
  • Targeting executives/finance because their mailboxes contain sensitive conversations and payment authority.
  • Data theft + extortion rather than (or as well as) encrypting systems.

What to do today

  • Brief staff (especially directors, finance, payroll, and reception): IT will never ask you to approve an MFA prompt you didn’t start, or to read out codes.
  • Set a “call-back” rule: if anyone gets an IT/security call, hang up and call your MSP/IT back using a known number from your internal contact list (not one provided in the call/email).
  • Check Microsoft 365 for auto-forwarding and suspicious inbox rules on key accounts (finance/shared mailboxes/executives).
  • Review conditional access / sign-in policies to reduce risky sign-ins (where appropriate for your organisation).
  • Reconfirm payment-change process: no bank detail changes without an out-of-band verification (known phone number, second approver).
  • Make sure you can rapidly disable a user session if compromise is suspected (and know who to contact out of hours).

Ask your IT provider

  • How quickly can you detect and respond to suspicious Microsoft 365 sign-ins and mailbox rule changes?
  • Do we have alerts for new inbox rules, auto-forwarding, and impossible travel/unusual sign-ins?
  • What is our standard playbook to contain a compromised account (disable user, revoke sessions/tokens, reset MFA, review mail rules, review OAuth/app consents)?
  • Which accounts are treated as high risk (finance, payroll, directors), and what extra protections are in place?
  • Do we have a tested process to handle data theft + extortion scenarios (not just ransomware)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If your business runs an online shop on Magento / Adobe Commerce, there is reporting of active exploitation of a newly described issue to deploy a Linux backdoor. This is primarily relevant to organisations that host and manage their own eCommerce stack (or have a developer/agency doing so). If that’s you, escalate to your web supplier today to confirm what monitoring and hardening is in place and whether they’re seeing suspicious activity.

One action today

Send a same-day internal note: “If ‘IT’ calls you unexpectedly, do not approve MFA or click login links—hang up and call back via our known IT/MSP number.”
