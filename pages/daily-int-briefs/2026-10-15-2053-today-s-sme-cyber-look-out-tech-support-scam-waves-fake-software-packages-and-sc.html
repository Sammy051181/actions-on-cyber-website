Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SME cyber look-out: tech support scam waves, fake software packages, and scam call tactics

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Thursday 15 October 2026, 20:53 UK time
Today’s look-out: Scam-driven business disruption and software supply-chain risk

What to look out for today

  • Tech support scam pages at scale: reporting suggests 20,000+ domains hosted on Cloudflare Pages have been used to push tech support scam journeys (fake alerts and pressure to call “support”).
  • Software supply-chain risk: malicious lookalike packages uploaded to the Arch User Repository (AUR), impersonating popular helper tools.
  • Scam calls and messages evolving: Android is rolling out AI-based scam protections, and India is mandating registered caller name display from 1 November 2026 (useful context if you have staff/operations in India).
  • Ransomware payment ecosystem pressure: the UK has sanctioned a cryptocurrency platform alleged to be a conduit for ransomware-related payments and other cybercrime proceeds.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Staff time and disruption: tech support scams can lock up browsers/devices, trigger panic, and waste hours—sometimes leading to remote access being granted to criminals.
  • Real financial risk: once attackers get remote access, they may target email, finance systems, and online banking, and attempt payment diversion.
  • IT toolchain risk: fake or trojanised packages can introduce malware and privileged access, which is especially damaging if used on admin or server machines.
  • Ripple effects: enforcement actions against crypto services can change how cybercriminals request payment (e.g., different wallets/platforms), influencing extortion and scam narratives.

Warning signs

  • Pop-ups claiming your device is “infected”, “blocked”, or “at risk”, telling you to call a phone number for urgent help.
  • A colleague says a web page suddenly became full-screen, loud, persistent, or is preventing normal browsing.
  • Unexpected requests to install remote support tools or allow screen sharing from someone claiming to be Microsoft, your ISP, your bank, or “IT support”.
  • On Linux/tech teams: installing or updating tools from community repositories and noticing unusual package names, new/unfamiliar maintainers, or prompts for elevated privileges that don’t match expectations.
  • Increase in plausible scam calls/SMS that reference “account verification”, “call forwarding”, “missed delivery”, or “invoice/payment issues”.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Tech support scam funnels drive users from compromised sites or adverts to convincing pages, then into a call centre script that pressures the victim into granting remote access or paying fees.
  • Supply-chain poisoning uses trusted developer/admin workflows (e.g., installing common helper tools) to run malicious code with high privileges.
  • Social engineering upgrades: scammers blend phone + SMS + email, using caller-name displays (where present) and scripted language to appear legitimate, pushing victims toward urgent actions.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-minute staff note: “If you see a scary pop-up telling you to call support, don’t call, don’t install anything—take a photo/screenshot and contact our IT/helpdesk.”
  • Set a clear internal rule: only your named IT provider/helpdesk can request remote access; staff should verify via a known internal number or ticketing system.
  • Check your web filtering / DNS protection: ensure categories for known scam/malvertising/tech support scams are enabled (where your service supports it).
  • If you use Arch/AUR anywhere: review recent installs/updates of helper tools and confirm they came from approved sources; avoid installing unvetted packages on admin machines.
  • Mobile hygiene: encourage staff to keep Android devices updated and enable built-in anti-scam protections where available.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you block known tech support scam patterns at DNS/web gateway level, and do we get alerts when users hit these pages?
  • Do we have a standard remote support process (named tools, approvals, logging) to reduce the chance staff hand access to a scammer?
  • For any Linux/dev environments: what’s our policy on community repositories (like AUR), and how do we review/approve packages used on business devices?
  • Do we have admin separation (separate admin accounts, least privilege) so one bad install doesn’t compromise everything?
  • What’s our plan if a user granted remote access: immediate containment steps, password resets, device checks, and banking/payment safeguards?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Keep an eye on updates from major infrastructure software you rely on (e.g., web servers/proxies) and ensure your IT provider has a routine to apply vendor security releases promptly, especially where the service is internet-facing.

One action today

Send a short staff reminder today: never call numbers from pop-up “virus” warnings and never allow remote access unless it’s via your known IT/helpdesk process.
