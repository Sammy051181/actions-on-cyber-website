Microsoft 365 disruption & cloud-app abuse: what SMEs should watch today

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Tuesday 18 August 2026, 14:06 UK time
Today’s look-out: Cloud productivity disruption + SharePoint/Teams credential theft + ransomware follow-on risk

What to look out for today

Two separate Microsoft 365 themes are in the news today: (1) a Microsoft-confirmed outage affecting search across Microsoft 365 apps, and (2) reports of attackers abusing trusted Microsoft services like SharePoint Online and Teams to blend in while stealing credentials and moving through environments. In parallel, CISA has warned a Windows Task Host flaw is now being exploited by ransomware gangs.

  • Microsoft 365 search issues (Outlook, SharePoint Online, OneDrive): expect users to report they “can’t find emails/files”.
  • SharePoint/Teams used as an attack path: look out for unusual SharePoint/Teams activity that doesn’t fit normal work patterns.
  • Ransomware gangs exploiting a Windows issue: treat unusual endpoint behaviour and login anomalies as urgent, especially if your estate is mixed and patching is uneven.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs rely heavily on Microsoft 365 for day-to-day operations. When search breaks, staff often improvise (forwarding documents to personal email, downloading extra copies, using unofficial storage) which can increase data leakage risk. Separately, attackers know that Microsoft 365 traffic is “normal”, so abusing SharePoint/Teams can help them hide in plain sight while they harvest credentials, access files, and potentially set up a ransomware disruption later.

Warning signs

  • Microsoft 365 disruption signals: sudden spike in helpdesk tickets about missing emails/files, search returning no results, staff repeatedly re-saving or re-uploading documents.
  • Account and access anomalies: unexpected MFA prompts, users reporting they were “logged out”, or password resets they didn’t initiate.
  • SharePoint/Teams oddities: new/unfamiliar SharePoint sites, unusual file uploads/downloads at odd hours, unexpected sharing links, or Teams messages inviting staff to open files “to fix access”.
  • Endpoint red flags: machines running slowly after login, security tools being disabled, unexpected scheduled tasks, or repeated failed logins (often a precursor to ransomware operations).

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • “Fake support” and phishing during an outage: attackers may send emails/Teams chats claiming to be Microsoft/your IT provider and asking users to “re-authenticate” or “apply a fix”.
  • Abusing trusted Microsoft services: using SharePoint/Teams activity to look like normal collaboration while they test access, steal credentials, and move between accounts.
  • Ransomware follow-on: once credentials or admin access is gained, attackers typically target backups, file servers, and cloud storage to maximise business disruption.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-minute staff note: “Microsoft 365 search may be unreliable today. Don’t click ‘fix login’ links, don’t approve unexpected MFA prompts, and report any Microsoft/IT ‘re-auth’ requests.”
  • Lock down sharing: review external sharing settings for SharePoint/OneDrive and check for any sudden changes.
  • Check sign-in risk quickly: review Microsoft 365 sign-in logs for impossible travel, unfamiliar locations, and repeated failures on key accounts (finance, admins).
  • Prioritise resilience: confirm you can still access key documents without relying on search (known folder locations, Teams channel files, critical procedures).
  • Be ready for disruption: ensure you have an offline way to contact staff and your IT provider if Teams/Outlook is degraded.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you confirm whether our tenant shows any unusual SharePoint/Teams activity (new sites, unusual sharing links, large downloads, suspicious logins)?
  • Do we have Conditional Access/MFA protections appropriately enforced for admins and finance users, and are we monitoring MFA fatigue/number-matching bypass attempts?
  • What alerts would we expect to see if a cloud account is being used to access lots of files quickly?
  • If Microsoft 365 is partially down, what’s our agreed business continuity workaround (customer comms, document access, approvals/payments)?
  • Are we protected against ransomware outcomes (tested backups, restore time expectations, and a plan for isolating compromised endpoints)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

CISA reports ransomware gangs are exploiting a Windows Task Host flaw. For SMEs this is a reminder to confirm with your IT provider that Windows updates are being applied promptly across all PCs and servers (including rarely used machines), and that patching exceptions are tracked and time-limited.

One action today

Send a brief internal message today warning staff not to click “re-authenticate/fix your Microsoft 365” links or approve unexpected MFA prompts during the Microsoft 365 search disruption.
