Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Sunday brief: payment card breach risk + router cloud management compromise

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Sunday 22 November 2026, 21:44 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier breach scams and remote access device compromise

What to look out for today

  • Supplier breach fallout: Cleverbridge says attackers stole payment card data including card numbers and CVVs. Expect follow-on fraud attempts and convincing “account/payment security” messages.
  • Remote access device compromise: GL.iNet says attackers exploited a flaw in its cloud remote management service to install malware on some customers’ routers and KVM devices, with reports of backdoors providing remote access.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Card fraud and chargebacks: If your business or customers used Cleverbridge for purchases, you may see disputed transactions, replacement cards, or “verification” calls/emails that look legitimate.
  • Business disruption risk: A compromised router can be a stepping-stone into email, file shares, cloud apps, and payment workflows—often without obvious signs until something breaks or money moves.
  • Supplier dependency: These issues sit outside your office—inside a supplier platform or cloud management layer—so normal endpoint antivirus may not help.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected “Cleverbridge” emails or calls asking you to confirm card details, reset passwords, or “verify” invoices/subscriptions.
  • Customers reporting fraud shortly after purchases; unusual spikes in refunds/chargebacks.
  • Routers/KVMs behaving oddly: slow internet, random reboots, settings changed, new admin accounts, or remote access enabled when you didn’t enable it.
  • New outbound connections from network devices you don’t recognise, or alerts from your firewall/MSP about unusual traffic.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • “Breach-confirmation” phishing: criminals use the news as cover to send fake security notices that harvest passwords or card details.
  • Payment diversion: finance teams may be targeted with “urgent card re-entry” or “update billing” requests that route payments to criminals.
  • Network foothold: if a router or KVM device is backdoored, attackers can monitor traffic, attempt logins to cloud services, and move towards email takeover and payment fraud.

What to do today

  • Run a quick supplier exposure check: confirm whether your business uses Cleverbridge for any purchases/subscriptions (or used it recently) and note which mailbox receives purchase receipts.
  • Brief finance/admin staff (5 minutes): do not act on “payment security” emails; verify by going to the supplier via bookmarks/known portals or calling known numbers (not numbers in emails).
  • Review card activity: check business card statements for small “test” charges and unexpected transactions; tighten approval thresholds for refunds/credits today.
  • Check remote management exposure: if you use GL.iNet cloud remote management, confirm which devices are enrolled, who has access, and whether any devices show unexpected configuration changes.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have any GL.iNet routers or KVM/remote access devices in use, and are any managed via the vendor’s cloud remote management?
  • Can you review logs/alerts for unusual outbound connections or configuration changes from network devices over the last 7–14 days?
  • Do we enforce MFA on cloud admin accounts and vendor portals, and do we have an up-to-date list of who can access them?
  • What’s our process for handling supplier breach follow-on phishing (mail filtering, banner warnings, and a clear “verify out-of-band” procedure for finance)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you use GL.iNet’s cloud-managed devices, treat this as an urgent maintenance check: confirm what mitigations or updates GL.iNet has provided and ensure your devices and their cloud management accounts are secured. The main immediate risk for SMEs is compromised remote access infrastructure rather than a single workstation infection.

One action today

Send a short internal note to finance/admin staff today: “If you receive any Cleverbridge/payment-security message or any request to change payment details, do not reply—verify using our saved supplier portal/bookmarked site or a known phone number.”
