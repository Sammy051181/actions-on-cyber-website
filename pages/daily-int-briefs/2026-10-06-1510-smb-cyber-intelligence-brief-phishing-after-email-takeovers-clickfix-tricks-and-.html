SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief: Phishing after email takeovers, ‘ClickFix’ tricks, and MSP/RMM access risk

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Tuesday 06 October 2026, 15:10 UK time
Today’s look-out: Phishing waves and business email compromise, plus MSP/RMM supplier risk

What to look out for today

1) Phishing waves sent from real, compromised business email accounts. A recent incident reported by a major publisher describes attackers taking over staff email accounts (Microsoft/Google) and then using one account to send thousands of phishing emails.

2) “ClickFix” style scams that trick staff into running something on their PC. These typically present as a fake “fix” message on a website or pop-up (e.g., “copy/paste this to resolve an issue”), but the goal is to get a user to execute a malicious action.

3) MSP/RMM supplier risk. Remote Monitoring & Management (RMM) tools are powerful “keys to the kingdom” for IT providers; weak controls can turn one supplier issue into multi-customer compromise.

4) AI/bot activity causing platform disruption and unwanted changes. Wikimedia reports “rogue agents” making unauthorised edits and generating heavy traffic; this is a reminder that automated tooling can create real-world service and integrity issues.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Email takeover is a fast route to invoice fraud and payroll diversion. If attackers can send from a real mailbox, your staff and customers are more likely to trust it.
  • One successful “do this quick fix” interaction can lead to device compromise and then wider access to files, finance systems, or passwords saved in browsers.
  • MSPs/RMM platforms concentrate risk. If your IT provider’s remote tooling is misconfigured, your business can be impacted even if you did “everything right” internally.
  • Automated bot/agent activity increases background noise (more convincing “support” lures, more spam, more accidental disruption), making it harder for teams to spot the real threats.

Warning signs

  • Customers/suppliers report receiving strange links or attachments “from you”.
  • Staff see unexpected Microsoft/Google sign-in prompts, MFA prompts they didn’t initiate, or new device login alerts.
  • Sudden bursts of outbound email, unusual “sent items”, new inbox rules/forwarding, or auto-replies you didn’t set.
  • Webpages or emails telling staff to “fix” an issue by copying commands, running scripts, or changing security settings.
  • Unexplained remote access sessions, new admin accounts, or RMM agent installs/updates you didn’t authorise.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Compromised mailbox → believable phishing. Attackers use a genuine account to target your customers, partners, or internal finance team with “please review”, “payment overdue”, “new banking details”, or “shared document” lures.
  • ClickFix-style social engineering. The attacker’s goal is to get a user to do the dangerous step for them (run something, bypass protections, or approve a prompt).
  • Supplier/MSP leverage. If an attacker obtains access to an MSP tool (or exploits weak controls), they may push actions across multiple client environments quickly.
  • High-volume automation. AI-generated spam and automated scanning can increase the volume of low-quality but persistent attempts, raising the odds of one staff member slipping up.

What to do today

  • Brief staff (2 minutes): “Do not follow ‘fix your PC’ steps from pop-ups or random sites. If in doubt, screenshot and report.”
  • Finance check: Reconfirm your payment-change process (call-back to a known number, not an email reply). Re-tell the team: no bank detail changes without verification.
  • Email hygiene: Ask IT to quickly check for suspicious inbox rules, forwarding, unusual sign-ins, and mass-sending activity.
  • Limit blast radius: Ensure day-to-day accounts are not local admins; confirm MFA is enforced for email and key SaaS tools.
  • MSP customers: Ask your provider what controls protect their RMM access to your tenant and endpoints.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you confirm whether any of our mailboxes have unexpected forwarding or new inbox rules?
  • Do we have monitoring/alerts for impossible travel, unusual sign-ins, or mass outbound email?
  • What protections are in place for your RMM tooling (separate admin accounts, MFA, least privilege, approvals for remote actions, and tenant isolation)?
  • If our email account is suspected compromised, what is the containment playbook (disable sign-in, revoke sessions, reset MFA, check rules, notify contacts)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Nothing in today’s items requires a specific emergency patch action for most SMEs; today’s risk is primarily about account security, phishing resilience, and supplier (MSP/RMM) controls. Use this as a prompt to validate monitoring and response readiness rather than chasing a patch list.

One action today

Send a same-day staff note: “If a website/email tells you to ‘fix’ something by copying/pasting or running steps, stop and report it—do not proceed.”
