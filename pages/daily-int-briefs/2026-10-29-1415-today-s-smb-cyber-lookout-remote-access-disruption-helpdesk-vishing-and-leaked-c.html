Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: remote-access disruption, helpdesk vishing, and leaked cloud keys

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Thursday 29 October 2026, 14:15 UK time
Today’s look-out: Remote-access and firewall disruption + helpdesk impersonation scams

What to look out for today

Three themes worth acting on today:

  • Remote-access disruption risk: attackers are actively exploiting an issue that can crash Cisco ASA/FTD SSL VPN devices, potentially taking remote working and site-to-site access offline.
  • Internet-exposed admin portals being targeted: reports of active exploitation against Palo Alto Networks management interfaces that are exposed to the internet.
  • Social engineering pressure: incident responders are seeing IT helpdesk impersonation (vishing) and rising ransomware/pre-ransomware activity.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Downtime hits hardest: if your firewall/VPN crashes, you may lose remote access, VoIP, shared drives, cloud access paths, or connections between sites—stopping work and delaying customer service.
  • SMEs often rely on one provider: if your MSP hosts or manages the firewall/VPN, their response speed determines your outage duration.
  • Helpdesk scams bypass “good” tech: a convincing phone call can trick staff into handing over MFA codes, resetting passwords, or installing remote tools—leading to account takeover and ransomware.

Warning signs

  • Sudden VPN instability: frequent disconnects, repeated reboots, or remote users unable to connect (especially if it starts without any internal change).
  • Unexpected IT calls: callers claiming to be “your IT team/MSP/Microsoft” urging urgent action, password resets, or requesting one-time codes.
  • Admin access anomalies: firewall or network admin portals being accessed at unusual times, from unfamiliar locations, or repeated failed logins.
  • Ransomware “warm-up” behaviour: new admin accounts, remote tools appearing, or unusual file activity before any ransom note.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Business disruption: crashing edge devices to force outages, distract IT, or create pressure to accept risky “support” help.
  • Abusing exposed management interfaces: targeting internet-accessible admin pages on network devices that were never meant to be public-facing.
  • Helpdesk vishing: impersonating IT to persuade staff to approve MFA prompts, share codes, or “verify” login details.
  • Credential and key reuse: stolen or exposed cloud/API credentials (e.g. from public code repositories) can enable access to SaaS, backups, or AI/cloud tools—sometimes without triggering obvious alarms.

What to do today

  • Confirm your exposure: list what you use for firewall/VPN (Cisco ASA/FTD? Palo Alto?), who manages it (MSP or in-house), and whether any management interface is exposed to the internet.
  • Set a “no codes on calls” rule: tell staff never to share MFA codes or approve MFA prompts because of a phone call. Use a call-back procedure to a known number.
  • Prepare for an outage: ensure you have a documented fallback for critical work (alternate remote access method, local contact list, offline invoicing/process notes, and how to reach your MSP).
  • Check logging/alerts: make sure someone will notice repeated VPN crashes, firewall reboots, or unusual admin logins today (even a simple email/SMS alert helps).

Ask your IT provider

  • Are any of our firewall/VPN management interfaces exposed to the internet? If yes, what’s the business reason and what extra controls are in place?
  • Do we use Cisco ASA/FTD SSL VPN anywhere, and are we affected by the actively exploited crash issue? What is your mitigation/upgrade plan and timeline?
  • Do we have monitoring for device reboots/crashes and repeated remote-access failures, and who is on-call if it happens?
  • What is our ransomware readiness status (backup isolation, restore testing, privileged access controls), given the reported increase in cases?
  • Do you have a process to detect leaked credentials (especially cloud/API keys) and rotate them quickly if found?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If your business (or MSP) runs Cisco ASA/FTD SSL VPN or has Palo Alto management interfaces, treat today as a prompt to confirm whether you are exposed and whether fixes/mitigations have been applied. This is less about routine patching and more about avoiding sudden remote-access outages and reducing the chance of internet-exposed admin surfaces being targeted.

One action today

Send a same-day note to staff: “IT will never ask for your MFA code or to approve a login over the phone”—and require a call-back to a known internal/MSP number for any urgent ‘helpdesk’ request.
