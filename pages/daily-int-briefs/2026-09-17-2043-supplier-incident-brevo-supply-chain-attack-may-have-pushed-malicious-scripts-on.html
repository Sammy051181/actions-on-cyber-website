Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Supplier incident: Brevo supply-chain attack may have pushed malicious scripts onto customer sites

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Thursday 17 September 2026, 20:43 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier incident scams and website script tampering (third‑party JavaScript risk)

What to look out for today

A reported supply-chain incident at Brevo (email/SMS marketing platform) involved attackers stealing a Cloudflare API key and using it to inject malicious “ClickFix” scripts into Brevo websites and JavaScript files that can be embedded on customer sites. If your organisation uses Brevo tags/scripts on your website, treat this as a priority check today.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Your website can become the delivery mechanism: if a third-party script you embed is altered, visitors (including customers and staff) can be exposed without you changing anything.
  • Brand and trust impact: customers associate the problem with your site, even if the root cause is a supplier.
  • Follow-on scams: incidents like this are often used to launch convincing “Brevo security update” emails, fake login pages, or “urgent domain verification” requests to finance/marketing teams.

Warning signs

  • Unusual pop-ups or “verification/fix” prompts appearing on your website (especially after submitting a form or opening a marketing sign-up modal).
  • Customers reporting being redirected, seeing strange instructions, or being asked to run steps to “confirm you’re human”.
  • Marketing staff receiving unexpected Brevo-related emails asking to re-authenticate, reconnect domains, add DNS records, or download “security tools”.
  • Sudden changes in website behaviour or performance shortly after marketing tags load.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Malware distribution via trusted pages: attackers rely on visitors trusting your legitimate website.
  • Credential harvesting: spoofed “re-login” flows for Brevo or related accounts (email, DNS, website admin, payment platforms) aimed at taking over marketing and admin access.
  • Secondary compromise: once an attacker gets access to a marketing platform, they can send convincing phishing from your domain to your contacts.

What to do today

  • Confirm whether you use Brevo scripts on your public website(s) (including landing pages run by agencies, microsites, and donation/event pages).
  • Temporarily remove or disable non-essential third-party tags (especially marketing/analytics widgets) until your web team confirms they’re clean and necessary.
  • Warn staff (marketing, comms, reception, IT, finance): be cautious of Brevo-themed emails/DMs asking for logins, DNS changes, “urgent verification”, or downloads.
  • Review account security for Brevo and your web/DNS/CDN providers: ensure MFA is enabled and remove stale users/access tokens where possible.
  • If you suspect impact: take a screenshot, note the time/URL, and ask your web support/MSP to investigate quickly before more visitors are exposed.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you confirm whether our sites embed Brevo JavaScript (directly or via Google Tag Manager/another tag manager)?
  • What monitoring do we have for unexpected third-party script changes on our website, and can we add it?
  • Do we have a quick process to disable third-party tags safely (and roll back) if a supplier is compromised?
  • Can you help us check for lookalike Brevo phishing in our mail filtering/quarantine and share guidance with staff?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Nothing specific to patch from today’s main item. The priority is supplier risk management: checking embedded third-party scripts and tightening access controls (MFA, least privilege) for marketing, DNS, and web tooling.

One action today

Have your web/MSP team confirm today whether any of your websites embed Brevo scripts (directly or via a tag manager) and temporarily disable non-essential third-party tags until verified safe.
