SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief: supplier-incident scams and ‘trusted update’ confusion

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Tuesday 11 August 2026, 18:11 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier incident scams, fake invoice/payment changes, and ‘update/download’ impersonation

What to look out for today

Expect an uptick in phishing and payment-diversion attempts that reference a real-world supplier incident. Wesco has confirmed it is investigating a cybersecurity incident, which criminals may use as cover for “urgent” messages about invoices, account changes, delivery issues, or document re-sharing.

Separately, Mozilla has updated the GPG signing key used for Firefox/Thunderbird releases after accidental exposure. That kind of news can also be abused by attackers to push fake “reinstall / update” emails or lookalike downloads.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Invoice fraud risk: criminals often use genuine supplier incidents as a believable reason to request new bank details, resend invoices, or “confirm” payment status.
  • Operational disruption: if you rely on distributors/logistics partners, a supplier incident can trigger delays, order changes, and last-minute requests—perfect cover for scams.
  • Trust signals get messy: when there’s legitimate news about signing keys or “security updates”, staff may be more likely to click an email link or bypass normal checks.

Warning signs

  • Email or call claiming a supplier has had an incident and you must change bank details or pay a replacement invoice.
  • Pressure tactics: “today only”, “accounts team unavailable”, “avoid service interruption”.
  • Requests to open “secure portal” links, shared files, or password-protected attachments to “verify” an order.
  • Messages about browser/email client “security issues” urging you to download a “new signed installer” from an emailed link.
  • Small changes to email addresses (extra characters, different domains) or replies coming from a different contact than usual.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Supplier-incident pretexting: using the real Wesco news to make fake payment-change requests sound credible.
  • Thread hijacking: replying inside existing email chains with altered bank details or new “payment instructions”.
  • Fake update/download lures: using “signing key” or “security update” language to push malicious installers or credential-harvesting pages.
  • Remote access pressure: asking staff to install a “support tool” to “fix access” or “recover orders”.

What to do today

  • Reinforce payment-change controls: any new bank details must be verified using a trusted number from your address book/contract (not the email).
  • Freeze and verify anomalies: if an invoice, remittance, or purchase order looks different, stop and verify with the supplier via known-good channels.
  • Remind staff: do not install software or updates from emailed links. Use official app stores or your IT-managed update process.
  • Check email security basics: ensure MFA is on for email, and review rules/forwarding on finance mailboxes for anything unexpected.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have extra monitoring on finance mailboxes for suspicious inbox rules, forwarding, or unusual login locations?
  • How are we protecting against invoice/payment-change phishing (email filtering, impersonation protection, DMARC alignment where possible)?
  • If staff receive “update/download” emails, what’s our approved software update route and how do we communicate it?
  • Do we have a quick process to lock accounts and investigate if a finance user clicks a link or enters credentials?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Cisco has warned about high-severity ClamAV-related issues that can crash scanning in some Cisco Secure Endpoint Connector setups (denial-of-service against the scanning process). If you use Cisco Secure Endpoint, ask your IT provider to confirm whether you’re affected and what mitigations or updates are being applied—this is mainly about reduced protection/visibility rather than immediate data theft.

One action today

Send a same-day note to finance and admin staff: ‘No bank detail changes or “replacement invoices” are actioned unless verified by a call to a known number—never from email details.’
