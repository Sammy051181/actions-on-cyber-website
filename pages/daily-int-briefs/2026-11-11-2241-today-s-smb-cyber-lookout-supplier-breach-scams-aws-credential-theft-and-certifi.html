Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: supplier breach scams, AWS credential theft, and certificate trust issues

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Wednesday 11 November 2026, 22:41 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier incident scams + cloud credential theft + certificate trust disruption

What to look out for today

  • Supplier breach ripple effects: Maersk has confirmed a data breach tied to a ransomware demand. Expect impersonation scams aimed at customers, partners and suppliers (fake payment changes, fake “invoice resend” emails, fake shipment updates).
  • Cloud account takeover attempts: Reports show attackers using “agentic” pentesting tooling (AntiFox) in campaigns that steal AWS credentials and pivot inside environments.
  • Certificate/website trust disruptions: Certificate authority eMudhra has revoked additional intermediate CA certificates after a private key handling incident. This can lead to browser warnings or failed connections for services that relied on affected certificate chains.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs are often targeted after a big-name incident because criminals know smaller firms have fewer controls and are more likely to approve urgent requests. If you rely on shipping/logistics providers, cloud platforms (especially AWS), or SaaS tools, today’s stories translate into real-world risks: fraudulent payments, account takeover, and business disruption (systems or portals failing due to certificate trust issues).

Warning signs

  • Emails or calls claiming to be from a logistics/shipping partner asking you to change bank details, pay a “late” invoice, or reconfirm account credentials.
  • Unexpected “shipment document” attachments, “invoice correction” PDFs, or links to log in to a portal you don’t usually use.
  • In AWS (or any cloud): new users/keys created, unusual logins, sudden permission changes, or unexplained new resources/services appearing.
  • Customers or staff reporting browser certificate warnings when accessing your website, client portal, VPN, or line-of-business app.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Brand impersonation: Using Maersk-themed messages (or pretending to be a forwarder/agent) to push payment diversion, harvest credentials, or deliver malware.
  • Credential theft to lateral movement: Stealing cloud credentials (e.g. AWS keys) to access production data, email, backups, and billing—then extort or disrupt operations.
  • Trust-chain confusion: Taking advantage of certificate warnings and “urgent fix” panic with fake IT support calls (“We need to install a new security certificate now”).

What to do today

  • Finance: Re-brief staff: no bank detail changes or urgent payments based on email alone. Use a known phone number (from your records, not the email) for call-backs.
  • Ops/logistics teams: Treat unexpected shipment links/attachments as suspicious. Verify directly via your usual portal/bookmark, not email links.
  • Cloud/SaaS owners: Check who has admin access; remove stale accounts; confirm MFA is enforced for admins; review recent sign-in/activity logs for anomalies.
  • Website/app owners: Ask your IT/provider to confirm your public-facing services are not affected by recent CA revocations and that certificate renewal monitoring is in place.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have alerting for unusual cloud activity (new access keys, new admin users, sign-ins from new locations, unusual data access)?
  • Is MFA enforced for all admin accounts (cloud, email, finance systems) and are any exceptions documented?
  • Do we routinely check and remove unused credentials (old AWS keys, ex-staff accounts, service accounts)?
  • How do we monitor certificate expiry and revocation for our domains, VPNs, and externally hosted apps?
  • If a supplier incident triggers phishing against us, what’s our internal reporting route and response SLA?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

There are reports of threat actors targeting internet-facing servers (including analytics and backup-related platforms). If you host business systems on-prem or in the cloud, ensure your provider is monitoring for suspicious access attempts and keeping externally exposed services under active maintenance—especially anything that touches reporting/analytics, messaging, backups, or remote access.

One action today

Send a same-day internal note to finance and ops teams: “No payment/bank-detail changes from email; verify supplier requests using our saved phone numbers and portals only.”
