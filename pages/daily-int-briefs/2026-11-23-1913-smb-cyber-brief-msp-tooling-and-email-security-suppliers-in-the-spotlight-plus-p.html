SMB Cyber Brief: MSP tooling and email security suppliers in the spotlight (plus payment fraud warnings)

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Monday 23 November 2026, 19:13 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier/MSP security incidents and payment fraud

What to look out for today

Today’s themes are about supplier and MSP dependency risk and the knock-on effect on smaller organisations:

  • Remote support / MSP tooling risk: reports of attackers exploiting a ScreenConnect issue to compromise MSP technician machines and MSP servers.
  • Email security supplier risk: N-able has discussed a breach investigation involving its hosted email filtering services (Mail Assure / SpamExperts) and a serious issue identified during that work.
  • Router / remote management risk: GL.iNet says attackers exploited a flaw in its GoodCloud remote management service to push malware onto some customer routers and KVM devices.
  • Payment fraud pressure: CERT-In warning that AI-assisted attacks have contributed to unauthorised transactions at banks/fintechs and tech service providers (useful context for reinforcing payment controls even if you’re not in that sector).

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs often rely on outsourced IT providers, remote support tools, email filtering, and cloud-managed routers. If any of these suppliers are impacted, criminals may:

  • Use trusted remote tools to access your PCs and servers.
  • Intercept or manipulate email (inbound filtering, quarantines, allow-lists) to make phishing and invoice fraud more likely.
  • Gain a foothold through a router or remote-access device and then move towards ransomware or data theft.
  • Push “urgent payment” or “bank details changed” requests at higher volume and with more convincing language.

Warning signs

  • Your IT provider asks for emergency access changes, or says they are “rebuilding” remote tooling unexpectedly.
  • Unusual remote support pop-ups/sessions, especially outside normal support hours.
  • Changes to email security behaviour: missing emails, unexpected quarantines, new allow/deny rules you didn’t request.
  • Network instability: router reboots, new admin accounts, or remote access enabled that wasn’t previously used.
  • Finance team sees a spike in: urgent payment requests, supplier bank-detail changes, or “CEO/partner is in a meeting—pay now” messages.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • MSP compromise: attackers target MSP remote support to reach many downstream clients quickly, often aiming for business disruption or ransomware.
  • Email control: by abusing email filtering/admin capabilities, criminals can make phishing more effective (or hide their traces by diverting emails).
  • Edge device foothold: router/KVM compromise can provide persistent access that’s harder for a small business to notice.
  • Payment fraud: criminals combine stolen context (invoices, threads, supplier names) with persuasive, AI-written messages to push unauthorised payments.

What to do today

  • Confirm with your IT/MSP whether you use ScreenConnect, N-able Mail Assure/SpamExperts, or GL.iNet GoodCloud-managed devices anywhere in the estate (including at satellite sites).
  • Reinforce payment controls today: require call-back verification (using known numbers, not email) for any new payee or bank change, and for urgent same-day payments.
  • Check admin access: ensure MFA is enabled for remote support, email admin portals, and any router/cloud management portals.
  • Watch for abnormal remote activity: ask for a quick review of remote session logs and admin logins for the last 7–14 days.
  • Brief staff (10 minutes): “If it involves money, access, or MFA codes, slow down and verify.”

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we use ScreenConnect anywhere? If yes, what’s been done to reduce risk (updates applied, configuration hardening, access restrictions), and how are technician endpoints protected?
  • Do we use N-able Mail Assure/SpamExperts (directly or via you)? What is our exposure, and have any admin/API credentials been rotated as a precaution?
  • Do we have any GL.iNet routers or GoodCloud-managed devices? If yes, can you confirm they are clean and review device management access logs?
  • What monitoring would alert us if remote tooling or email filtering rules were changed unexpectedly?
  • If your tools are disrupted, what is the fallback support process (and how do we verify it’s really you contacting us)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Where you (or your IT provider) run remote support platforms or managed email security, today is a good day to confirm updates and mitigation steps have been applied promptly and that privileged access (admin/API) is tightly controlled and monitored—especially for MSP tooling and hosted email filtering services.

One action today

Send a same-day note to your IT/MSP to confirm whether you use ScreenConnect, N-able Mail Assure/SpamExperts, or GL.iNet GoodCloud—and ask them to evidence what monitoring and access controls are in place (including MFA and recent log reviews).
