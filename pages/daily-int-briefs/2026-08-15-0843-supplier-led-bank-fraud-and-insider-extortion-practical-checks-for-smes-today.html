Supplier-led bank fraud and insider extortion: practical checks for SMEs today

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Saturday 15 August 2026, 08:43 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier incident scams, payment fraud and insider data theft

What to look out for today

Two themes SMEs should watch closely today:

  • Supplier/service-provider weakness leading to bank fraud: criminals allegedly exploited a flaw at a service provider to steal money from bank customers. This is a reminder that “your bank” isn’t the only dependency in the chain.
  • Insider/contractor data theft and extortion: a contractor was jailed after stealing data and extorting their employer. Many SMEs rely heavily on contractors and outsourced IT—this risk is not limited to big firms.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Payment disruption hits hardest: if funds are withdrawn or payments are diverted, cashflow and payroll can be immediately affected.
  • You inherit supplier risk: an issue in a provider (payments, payroll, CRM, IT support, telecoms) can become your incident with little warning.
  • Smaller teams often have “all-access” contractors: broad permissions and shared admin accounts make data theft, sabotage and extortion easier.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected bank alerts, new payees, or small “test” payments you don’t recognise.
  • Staff reporting “odd” MFA prompts or approvals they didn’t initiate.
  • Supplier emails claiming an urgent issue and asking you to “reconfirm” bank details, approve a payment, or sign in via a link.
  • Contractor/offboarded user accounts still active; unusual downloads/export activity; access outside normal hours.
  • Threatening messages referencing internal data, invoices, HR files or customer lists.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Supplier-led payment fraud: attackers abuse a weakness at a provider (or its integrations) to enable unauthorised withdrawals/transactions, or to make customers easier to socially engineer during confusion.
  • “Incident pretexting”: criminals may impersonate your bank/provider support team and push urgent actions (approve a transfer, reset credentials, change payee details).
  • Insider leverage: someone with legitimate access (employee or contractor) copies sensitive data and uses it to pressure the business for money, or to cause disruption.

What to do today

  • Reconfirm your payment-change process: no bank-detail changes from email alone; require a call-back using a known-good number (not one in the email).
  • Turn on or tighten bank controls: daily transaction alerts, lower payment limits, and dual approval for new payees and high-value transfers (where your bank supports it).
  • Do a quick access check: list contractors/suppliers with admin access (IT, finance systems, payroll, email) and confirm they still need it.
  • Backups and exports: ensure you can export your key SaaS data (finance, CRM, HR) and that backups are tested—extortion often targets “business-critical” data, not just PCs.
  • Staff briefing (5 minutes): remind staff that unexpected MFA prompts and “urgent supplier incidents” are common fraud tactics; when in doubt, stop and verify.

Ask your IT provider

  • Which third parties currently have privileged access to our Microsoft 365/Google, finance, payroll, and backups? Can we see a list?
  • Do we have separate named admin accounts (no shared logins) and strong MFA for admins?
  • What monitoring/alerting is in place for mass downloads, mailbox rules, new admin creation, and unusual sign-ins?
  • What is our offboarding process for contractors—how quickly is access removed, and who signs off?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Several industrial/medical device advisories were published this week, but most SMEs won’t use the specific products. If you operate building management/energy systems or specialist equipment managed by third parties, ask your maintainer what devices are in scope and how they monitor, update and recover them after a disruption.

One action today

Run a same-day review of who can change payees/bank details (in banking and finance systems) and enforce dual approval plus call-back verification for any new or changed payment instructions.
