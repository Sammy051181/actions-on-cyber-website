Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber look-out: AI-assisted impersonation, ‘watermark’ confusion, and a quick Windows patch check

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Saturday 15 August 2026, 13:57 UK time
Today’s look-out: AI impersonation and trust signals (plus routine patch verification)

What to look out for today

Two themes to brief staff on today:

  • AI-assisted impersonation (emails, messages, documents) designed to look ‘official’ and urgent.
  • Confusion around AI “watermarks” and “watermark removers” being used as a pretext for scams, policy-bypasses, or risky tool downloads.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs are often targeted with believable, low-effort impersonation that aims to trigger one risky action: paying an invoice, changing bank details, sharing a file, or approving an account change. If staff believe AI-generated content can be reliably “detected” or “undetected”, it can create a false sense of security and reduce healthy verification habits.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected requests to change payment details, “reconfirm” bank accounts, or pay a “reissued” invoice.
  • Messages that insist you must act today and discourage calling to confirm (“I’m in a meeting / travelling / use email only”).
  • A colleague/supplier email that feels plausible but has slightly off wording, unusual tone, or new sign-off details.
  • Anyone recommending you use a new tool/service to “remove watermarks” or “prove it’s not AI” (especially if it requires uploading business documents).
  • Links to “AI verification portals” or “secure review pages” that you’ve never used before.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Impersonation at scale: AI-written emails to finance, payroll, or front desk that mimic real suppliers or executives.
  • Trust-signal scams: claiming content is “watermarked/verified” (or that they can “remove” a watermark) to push you into trusting a document or downloading a tool.
  • Data exposure: encouraging staff to paste sensitive text (contracts, customer details, HR content) into third-party “detectors” or “removers”.

What to do today

  • Reinforce a simple rule: payment changes and urgent requests must be verified using a known phone number or known contact method (not the one in the email).
  • Remind staff not to upload internal documents into random “AI detection / watermark removal” websites.
  • Set an internal escalation path: if someone is unsure, forward the message to a named mailbox/person (IT provider or office manager) for a quick check.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have protections in place for email impersonation and lookalike domains, and how are suspicious messages quarantined/reported?
  • Can we add (or confirm) a payment-change verification process that’s easy for finance/admin to follow?
  • What’s our guidance on staff using third-party AI tools with business data (what’s allowed, what’s not)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Microsoft has issued a fix for a Windows zero-day issue (“LegacyHive”). This isn’t a call for panic, but it is a good prompt to confirm your Windows updates are being applied promptly across laptops and desktops (including any rarely used devices).

One action today

Send a 2-line reminder to finance/admin: “No bank detail changes or urgent payments without calling a known number to verify — and don’t upload business documents to ‘AI watermark remover/detector’ sites.”
