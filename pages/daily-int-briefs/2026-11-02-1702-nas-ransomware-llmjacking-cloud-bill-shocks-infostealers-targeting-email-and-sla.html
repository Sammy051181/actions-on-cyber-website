Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

NAS ransomware + “LLMJacking” cloud bill shocks + infostealers targeting email and Slack

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Monday 02 November 2026, 17:02 UK time
Today’s look-out: Business disruption and unexpected costs via compromised accounts, NAS ransomware, and supplier/cloud dependency

What to look out for today

  • Ransomware targeting NAS devices (especially Synology) – increased reports of attacks that can encrypt shared files and backups stored on the NAS.
  • Stolen logins leading to fast, expensive cloud/AI usage (“LLMJacking”) – attackers hijack access to AI/model services and run up large bills quickly.
  • Infostealer malware stealing browser passwords, email and collaboration data – including credentials/cookies in Chromium browsers, Outlook data and Slack workspace information.
  • Operational disruption risk – attackers have remotely changed refrigeration temperature settings in European supermarkets, highlighting risks to connected building/retail/IoT systems.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

These issues don’t require a “big enterprise” footprint. A single compromised user account, an exposed NAS, or a poorly controlled third-party system can cause:

  • Immediate downtime (loss of shared files, invoices, case files, lesson materials, or booking systems).
  • Rapid financial impact (unexpected cloud/AI charges in hours, not weeks).
  • Knock-on compromise (stolen cookies and passwords can lead to access to email, payroll, customer data, and supplier portals).
  • Real-world disruption if you rely on connected facilities/retail systems (temperature monitoring, building management, alarm systems, etc.).

Warning signs

  • NAS shares suddenly inaccessible, lots of files renamed, or ransom notes appearing on shared drives.
  • Unusual admin logins to cloud consoles or AI/automation tools, especially outside normal hours.
  • Unexpected spikes in cloud spend, new API keys, or new “projects/workspaces” you don’t recognise.
  • Users reporting repeated sign-in prompts, MFA fatigue, or accounts being logged out of email/Slack unexpectedly.
  • Supplier/managed service alerts about IoT/building systems behaving oddly (e.g., temperature setpoints changing).

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Infostealers often arrive via booby-trapped downloads, fake software updates, or malicious email attachments/links, then steal saved passwords and session cookies to bypass normal logins.
  • NAS ransomware commonly follows exposed remote access, weak credentials, or compromised admin accounts—then encrypts shared data that many staff rely on.
  • LLMJacking can start with a stolen password, leaked API key, or compromised admin account, then quickly consumes expensive AI/model resources to monetise your account.
  • Connected systems (retail/IoT/building) can be targeted through remote management tools, vendor access, reused credentials, or poorly segmented networks.

What to do today

  • Check NAS exposure and access: confirm remote access is intentional, admin accounts are minimal, and MFA is enabled where available.
  • Backups sanity-check: ensure you have at least one backup that’s not continuously connected/writable from normal user accounts (so ransomware can’t encrypt it too).
  • Set cloud spend guardrails: enable budget alerts and spend limits where possible; review API keys and disable any not in active use.
  • Reset riskier credentials: prioritise email, Microsoft 365/Google Workspace, finance, payroll, and admin accounts—especially if passwords may be saved in browsers.
  • Reinforce staff reporting: ask staff to report unexpected sign-in prompts, MFA spam, or being logged out of key tools immediately.
  • Review any connected facilities systems: know who can remotely change settings, and ensure you can rapidly revoke supplier access if needed.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have a clear inventory of NAS devices, how they’re accessed remotely, and who has admin rights?
  • What is our NAS recovery plan (how long to restore, and from what backup)? Have we tested it recently?
  • Do we have cloud budget alerts and anomaly detection set up to catch sudden AI/model usage spikes?
  • Are we monitoring for impossible travel/new device logins and unusual creation of API keys/tokens?
  • How are Slack/Microsoft 365/Google Workspace sessions protected (MFA, conditional access, session controls)?
  • If we use building/retail/IoT systems, how is the network segmented and how is vendor remote access controlled and logged?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Vendors are warning of active attacks against internet-facing systems and appliances today. If you run remote access gateways or NAS devices, treat vendor security advisories as time-sensitive: confirm your IT provider has a same-day process to review exposure and apply updates (or implement compensating controls) without breaking business access.

One action today

Turn on (or verify) cloud budget/spend alerts today for your key accounts (Microsoft/AWS/Google/AI tools) so sudden “LLMJacking” usage triggers an immediate notification to finance and IT.
