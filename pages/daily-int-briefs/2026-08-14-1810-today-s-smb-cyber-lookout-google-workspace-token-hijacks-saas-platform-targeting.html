Today’s SMB cyber lookout: Google Workspace token hijacks, SaaS platform targeting, and macOS remote access abuse

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Friday 14 August 2026, 18:10 UK time
Today’s look-out: Cloud account takeover and remote access abuse (with knock-on supplier/SaaS disruption risk)

What to look out for today

Three themes SMEs should pay attention to today:

  • Google Workspace compromise that doesn’t start with phishing – attackers can use stolen OAuth tokens to access Gmail, Drive and connected apps.
  • SaaS / platform risk – a critical issue in SAP Commerce Cloud is being actively targeted, which may impact organisations that run (or are supplied by) online shops on this platform.
  • macOS remote access misuse – active exploitation tied to macOS Screen Sharing is being used to deploy a Monero (cryptocurrency) miner, which can slow devices and disrupt work.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Workspace is your business backbone: if an attacker gets into email and files, they can impersonate staff, intercept invoices, and access client data.
  • You may be affected via suppliers: even if you don’t use SAP Commerce Cloud, your retail, booking, or eCommerce supplier might—disruption or data exposure can ripple to you and your customers.
  • Mac performance and costs: crypto-miners can cause slow laptops, overheating, battery drain, and increased support time—often noticed first by end users.

Warning signs

  • Google Workspace: unexpected consent prompts, new “authorised” apps you don’t recognise, unusual email forwarding/share settings, unexplained file sharing, sign-ins from unusual locations/devices.
  • eCommerce suppliers: sudden site instability, unusual admin activity, unexpected maintenance windows, or supplier notifications that feel vague but urgent.
  • macOS: fans running constantly, sudden slowness, high CPU usage, Screen Sharing/remote access enabled when it shouldn’t be, user reports of “my Mac is boiling” or battery draining fast.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Token-based takeovers: instead of stealing a password, attackers aim for OAuth tokens that let them act as the user in Gmail/Drive and connected SaaS tools.
  • Platform targeting: attackers focus on widely used cloud commerce platforms because one weakness can impact many organisations and their customers.
  • Remote access to deploy miners: once on a Mac, a miner may be installed to monetise access—often quietly—while the business pays the performance/support cost.

What to do today

  • Google Workspace quick check: review third-party app access/authorised apps and remove anything not required; confirm MFA is enforced for all users (including admins).
  • Supplier/SaaS check: if you run eCommerce or rely on a web shop provider, ask whether they use SAP Commerce Cloud and what monitoring/mitigations they’ve put in place.
  • macOS hygiene: confirm Screen Sharing/remote management is only enabled where explicitly needed; investigate any reports of sustained slowness or overheating.
  • Staff reminder: treat unexpected “app access” prompts and urgent admin/security emails as suspicious—report first, don’t click through.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you provide a list of Google Workspace OAuth / third-party app access granted in our tenant and flag anything risky or unnecessary?
  • Do we have alerting for suspicious Workspace activity (new OAuth grants, unusual sign-ins, mass file sharing, new forwarding rules)?
  • Are any of our business systems (or key suppliers) dependent on SAP Commerce Cloud, and what is our contingency plan if a supplier’s storefront is disrupted?
  • On Macs, are Screen Sharing / remote management settings centrally controlled, and can you quickly detect abnormal CPU usage consistent with mining?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

There are reports of a recently patched SAP Commerce Cloud issue being actively targeted, and separate reports of active exploitation tied to macOS Screen Sharing. For SMEs, the key is confirming your providers (and outsourced IT) are monitoring these items and applying relevant vendor updates quickly—especially for internet-facing commerce systems and managed Macs.

One action today

Review and remove unnecessary third‑party/OAuth app access in Google Workspace today, and require staff to report unexpected consent prompts immediately.
