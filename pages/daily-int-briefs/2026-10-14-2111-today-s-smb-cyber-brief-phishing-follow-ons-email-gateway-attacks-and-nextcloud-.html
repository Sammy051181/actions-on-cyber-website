Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB Cyber Brief: phishing follow‑ons, email gateway attacks, and Nextcloud server risk

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Wednesday 14 October 2026, 21:11 UK time
Today’s look-out: Phishing and email service compromise leading to account takeover, invoice fraud, and business disruption

What to look out for today

Three practical themes to brief staff and check with suppliers/IT today:

  • Phishing surge and “follow-on” scams: a major phishing ecosystem (linked to over 1 billion scam messages) has been disrupted. This often triggers copycat campaigns and clean-up themed scams (e.g., “your account was compromised, verify now”).
  • Email security gateway compromise risk: Cisco has warned of active attacks against its Secure Email Gateway. If your organisation (or your MSP) runs this product, email can become a route to compromise.
  • Nextcloud server backdooring reports: attackers have reportedly planted web shells on some Nextcloud servers via an optional Collabora “Built-in CODE” app flaw. Any internet-facing self-hosted collaboration/file platform should be treated as high priority.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Phishing drives payment fraud (invoice redirection, “change of bank details”, fake overdue bills) and account takeover (Microsoft 365/Google/email logins).
  • Email infrastructure issues are disruptive: if an email gateway is compromised, attackers can potentially intercept, alter, or use email flows to spread malware and steal credentials.
  • Collaboration/file platforms are business-critical: compromise can mean data theft, ransomware staging, or operational downtime—especially for charities, schools, and professional services handling sensitive documents.

Warning signs

  • Emails urging urgent action: “password expires today”, “unusual sign-in”, “quarantine release”, “voice message/fax”, “DocuSign/SharePoint/OneDrive link”.
  • Unexpected requests to re-confirm bank details, change payment instructions, or “re-send” an invoice to a new address.
  • Sudden increase in blocked/quarantined emails or staff reporting missing emails (could be misconfiguration or malicious rules).
  • Nextcloud users seeing unusual logins, new admin accounts, unfamiliar apps enabled, or unexplained server performance spikes.
  • Customers/suppliers receiving strange emails “from you” shortly after a legitimate thread.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Re-packaged phishing kits: even if one set is disrupted, criminals often pivot to new infrastructure and reuse the same lures, targeting finance teams and shared mailboxes.
  • Compromise the email ‘front door’: attacking email gateways can give criminals a foothold that bypasses user awareness training.
  • Server-side compromise of collaboration tools: if a Nextcloud server is exposed to the internet, attackers may attempt to plant backdoors for later data theft or ransomware deployment.
  • Trust abuse: criminals may reference the news (“Microsoft takedown”, “security update”, “your mailbox is affected”) to make phishing feel legitimate.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-sentence staff heads-up: do not click “verify/release/quarantine” links from unexpected emails; report suspicious messages to IT/MSP.
  • Finance control: require a call-back (using a known number) for any payment detail changes—even if it’s part of an existing email thread.
  • Email account hygiene: review shared mailboxes and finance inboxes for suspicious forwarding rules and mailbox delegates.
  • Nextcloud check (if you host it): confirm whether the Collabora Built-in CODE app is in use and ask for a review of logs and admin accounts.
  • Prepare for disruption: ensure you can operate for a day without email (alternative contact method; access to key documents; urgent supplier/customer phone list).

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we (or you, on our behalf) use Cisco Secure Email Gateway? If yes, what immediate risk-reduction steps have been taken and how will we know if we were targeted?
  • Are you monitoring for suspicious mailbox rules, new OAuth/app consents, and impossible travel sign-ins on our email tenant?
  • Do we run Nextcloud anywhere? If yes, is the Collabora “Built-in CODE” app enabled, and have you checked for indicators of backdoors (unexpected files, new admin users, unusual outbound traffic)?
  • If our email filtering layer fails or is compromised, what is our fallback plan for business communications?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you use Cisco Secure Email Gateway or self-host Nextcloud (especially with Collabora’s Built-in CODE app), treat this as a priority operational check with your IT provider today. The key SME takeaway is to confirm whether you are affected and what mitigations/updates and monitoring have been applied, rather than relying on user awareness alone.

One action today

Message staff and finance today: ‘Do not act on email links asking you to verify/release/quarantine/confirm password or change payment details; report it and use a known phone number for any bank detail changes.’
