SMB Cyber Briefing: firewall/VPN credential harvesting, fake “Cloudflare checks”, and ransomware targeting backups

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Wednesday 07 October 2026, 15:29 UK time
Today’s look-out: Credential theft and web-injected malware leading to account takeover and ransomware disruption

What to look out for today

Three practical watch-outs for SMEs today:

  • Firewall/VPN credential harvesting (Fortinet devices): law enforcement is warning an active campaign is collecting credentials linked to internet-facing FortiGate firewalls/SSL VPNs.
  • Fake “Cloudflare check” pages on hacked websites: compromised sites are showing convincing verification prompts that can lead to information-stealing malware.
  • Ransomware targeting backups: criminals are increasingly trying to delete, encrypt or sabotage backups to remove your recovery options.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • One set of stolen VPN credentials can be enough for criminals to access email, files and finance systems—often without immediately tripping alarms.
  • Info-stealers are designed to grab saved passwords, browser sessions and cookies, which can lead to rapid account takeovers (Microsoft 365/Google, accounting, payroll, CRM).
  • If backups are reachable from normal admin accounts, ransomware impact becomes “business-stopping” (longer downtime, higher pressure to pay, harder recovery).

Warning signs

  • Unexpected VPN login prompts for staff who don’t normally use VPN, or users reporting “my password stopped working”.
  • Unfamiliar logins to Microsoft 365/Google/admin portals, especially from unusual locations or at odd hours.
  • Staff report websites asking them to complete a “Cloudflare / security check” that looks unusual, asks to run something, or behaves differently from a normal CAPTCHA.
  • Backups suddenly show failed jobs, deleted restore points, changed retention settings, or unexpected admin activity on the backup console.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Reuse leaked passwords against VPN/firewall portals and then move into file servers, email and line-of-business apps.
  • Use web-injected fake checks to trick staff into actions that lead to an info-stealer infection, then use stolen sessions to bypass MFA where possible.
  • Once inside, attack backup infrastructure first (backup servers, consoles, connected storage) before deploying ransomware—reducing your ability to restore.

What to do today

  • Confirm MFA is enforced on VPN, Microsoft 365/Google, payroll, accounting, and any remote admin tools.
  • Reset and harden: if you use Fortinet SSL VPN/FortiGate, review remote access accounts, remove old users, and rotate passwords (especially any shared/admin credentials).
  • Backups: ensure you have at least one isolated/immutable copy and that you can complete a restore test (a file restore is better than nothing).
  • Staff reminder (2 minutes): if a site shows an odd “Cloudflare check” prompt, stop and report it—don’t “click through” or run anything.
  • Monitoring quick check: review sign-in logs for suspicious access and set alerts for new admin users and MFA changes.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have a list of all internet-exposed services (VPN, firewalls, remote access portals) and are they monitored for suspicious logins?
  • For our firewall/VPN, do we enforce MFA for every user (including admins), and do we block/alert on repeated failed logins?
  • Are our backups immutable or offline, and who has admin access to the backup console?
  • When was our last restore test, and how long would it take to restore our key systems (file share, email, finance, line-of-business app)?
  • If an info-stealer hits a staff laptop, what is our credential reset and session revoke process for Microsoft 365/Google and key SaaS tools?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If your business uses Fortinet FortiGate/SSL VPN, treat this as a prompt to review your vendor guidance and your managed service provider’s hardening/maintenance plan. The key SME focus today is credential hygiene and remote access controls (MFA, password rotation, disabling legacy/unused accounts), rather than chasing technical details.

One action today

Run a same-day remote access and backup check: confirm MFA on VPN and core SaaS, rotate any shared/admin remote access passwords, and verify you have an immutable/offline backup with a successful recent restore test.
