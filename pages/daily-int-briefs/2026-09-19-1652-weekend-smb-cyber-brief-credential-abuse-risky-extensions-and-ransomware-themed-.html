Weekend SMB Cyber Brief: credential abuse, risky extensions, and ransomware-themed scams

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

Moderate Saturday 19 September 2026, 16:52 UK time
Today’s look-out: Credential theft and browser/extension risk (with ransomware-themed lures)

What to look out for today

Today’s theme is identity/credential abuse and browser extension risk, with a side-order of ransomware-themed scam lures. Several stories point to attackers leaning on stolen logins and “helpful tools” (including AI browser assistants) as easy ways into business systems.

  • Ransomware leak-site drama: reports that ShinyHunters breached the Clop leak site may drive a wave of “news” emails and social posts designed to get clicks, steal credentials, or push fake “data breach checks”.
  • Malicious browser extensions: new research shows how a single malicious extension could hijack AI assistants inside browsers, increasing the impact of a bad extension install.
  • Identity visibility / account takeover risk: industry reporting again highlights that stolen and misused credentials remain a common starting point for breaches.
  • Large-scale device compromise advisory: a law enforcement advisory reported widespread device compromise activity linked to North Korean actors (context for why baseline controls matter even for smaller organisations).
  • AI/security test mix-ups: reporting on AI tools accessing real systems during evaluations is a reminder that “testing” and “automation” can cause unintended access if domains and guardrails are wrong.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs don’t need to be directly targeted by a nation-state or a major ransomware crew to be affected. The most common path is still straightforward: someone gets a password, or someone installs something they shouldn’t, and access spreads to email, files, accounting, payroll, and client data.

  • Email takeover can lead to invoice redirection, fake supplier bank changes, and payroll fraud.
  • Browser extensions can see (and potentially interfere with) what staff do in web apps—especially cloud finance, CRM, and HR tools.
  • Ransomware “news” cycles reliably create phishing opportunities (“see if your company is listed”, “download the leaked file”, “urgent security notice”).

Warning signs

  • Emails or messages referencing a ransomware leak and asking you to click a link to “check exposure”, “download proof”, or “verify credentials”.
  • Staff being prompted to install a new browser extension to “enable an AI assistant”, “summarise emails”, “auto-fill forms”, or “speed up admin”.
  • MFA prompts staff didn’t initiate (push-bombing) or repeated failed sign-ins in Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace.
  • Unexpected changes to email rules (e.g., auto-forwarding, hidden inbox rules) or sudden “sent items” the user doesn’t recognise.
  • Unusual requests for face scans / identity checks or other intrusive verification steps from novelty services that staff may try on work devices.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Phishing off the headlines: using the Clop/ShinyHunters story as believable bait to collect Microsoft 365/Google credentials.
  • Extension-led compromise: convincing staff to add a “productivity” extension that can interfere with browser-based tools and (in some scenarios) interact with AI assistants.
  • Account takeover: using stolen passwords from elsewhere, or credentials harvested via fake login pages, to access email and then move into finance processes.
  • Supply-chain style pivots: once a mailbox is compromised, attackers impersonate suppliers/clients to redirect payments or capture sensitive documents.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-minute staff warning: “Do not click ‘breach check’ links tied to ransomware stories. If in doubt, forward to IT.”
  • Review browser extensions on work devices: remove anything unused; block new extension installs unless approved (where possible).
  • Check for mailbox tampering: look for new forwarding rules, new “delegates”, and unfamiliar sign-ins.
  • Reconfirm payment-change process: ensure any supplier bank detail changes are verified out-of-band (phone call to a known number, not the email thread).
  • Keep AI tools on a leash: if staff use AI browser assistants, treat them like any other tool—approved list, minimal permissions, and no “install this plugin” from random prompts.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you enforce/limit browser extensions on managed devices (and provide a report of what’s installed)?
  • Do we have alerts for suspicious sign-ins and new email forwarding rules in Microsoft 365/Google Workspace?
  • What’s our current position on phishing-resistant MFA (where feasible) for admin and finance users?
  • Do we have an agreed invoice and bank-change verification process, and is it documented for holidays/weekends?
  • If an account is taken over, what is the response playbook (time to disable, reset, review rules, notify affected clients)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If your organisation (or your IT provider) runs Linux-based systems, note that CISA has flagged Linux kernel issues being exploited in the wild. For SMEs, the key is not the technical detail: ask your IT provider to confirm they have a routine to prioritise updates when exploitation is reported, especially for internet-facing or remote-access systems.

One action today

Send a short internal note today: “No one should click ‘breach check’ links or install new browser extensions without IT approval—forward anything suspicious to IT/your MSP.”
