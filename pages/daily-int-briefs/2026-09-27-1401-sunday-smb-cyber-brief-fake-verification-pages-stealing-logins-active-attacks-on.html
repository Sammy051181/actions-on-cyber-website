Sunday SMB Cyber Brief: fake verification pages stealing logins + active attacks on business systems

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Sunday 27 September 2026, 14:01 UK time
Today’s look-out: Credential theft via fake “verification/CAPTCHA” pages and renewed exploitation of internet-facing business platforms

What to look out for today

1) Fake “verification”/CAPTCHA pages used to trick staff into running steps that lead to password and browser-session theft. Reports describe ClickFix-style lures where a site shows a fake Cloudflare-style check and prompts users to complete actions that ultimately install an information-stealing malware focused on browser credentials.

2) Renewed exploitation against internet-facing business platforms. Multiple reports flag active attacks against enterprise web systems (including Oracle PeopleSoft) and warnings that some widely used platforms are being exploited in the wild.

3) Citrix NetScaler remote access: unpatched zero-days reported under active exploitation. If your organisation (or IT provider) uses NetScaler for VPN/remote access/app delivery, treat this as an urgent exposure-management issue.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Credential theft is a fast path to fraud. If an attacker steals browser passwords/session cookies, they may access email, Microsoft 365/Google, bookkeeping, payroll, CRMs and banking portals without needing to "hack" anything else.
  • SMEs often inherit risk via suppliers. Hosted HR/payroll portals, MSP remote tools, web agencies and managed hosting can expose you if they run vulnerable internet-facing systems.
  • Disruption cascades quickly. Even if you’re not running PeopleSoft/Citrix yourself, your suppliers might—leading to invoice fraud, account takeover, or service outages that hit you.

Warning signs

  • A website unexpectedly shows a “verify you are human” page that asks you to copy/paste something, run unusual steps, or enable extra permissions.
  • Staff report being repeatedly prompted to re-authenticate to email/Teams/SharePoint/Google Workspace, or MFA prompts they did not initiate.
  • New “inbox rules”, strange auto-forwarding, or unexpected OAuth/app consents in Microsoft 365/Google.
  • Unexplained logins from new locations/devices, or multiple failed logins followed by a success.
  • Suppliers warning of “maintenance”, “security incidents”, or asking you to “re-verify” access via a link.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • ClickFix-style social engineering: attackers compromise websites or place malicious content and then use fake verification/CAPTCHA workflows to get a user to perform steps that lead to malware install and credential theft.
  • Targeting exposed web apps: attackers scan for internet-facing business systems and use bypass techniques to get around common perimeter filtering, then plant persistence and extract data.
  • Remote access appliance compromise: if edge devices (like remote access gateways) are compromised, attackers can pivot into internal systems, harvest credentials, and deploy ransomware or conduct data theft.

What to do today

  • Staff message (5 minutes): “Do not follow any website instructions that involve copying/pasting commands, running scripts, or ‘verifying’ via unusual steps. If in doubt, close the tab and report it.”
  • Protect browsers and email accounts: ensure MFA is enforced on email, finance, payroll and admin accounts; review any newly-approved apps/integrations.
  • Check for account takeover indicators: review recent sign-in logs for admin and finance users; look for new forwarding rules and unusual OAuth consents.
  • Confirm what’s internet-facing: ask your IT provider to list any public-facing portals/appliances (remote access, web apps, CMS, customer portals) and confirm monitoring is active.
  • Prepare for supplier ripple effects: remind finance teams to validate bank detail changes using a known phone number (not one in an email).

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we (or any of our suppliers you manage) use Citrix NetScaler for remote access or application delivery? If yes, what immediate risk-reduction steps are in place while fixes are pending?
  • Are any of our systems running Oracle PeopleSoft, or are we reliant on a hosted PeopleSoft provider? What monitoring is in place for suspicious web activity and unexpected admin actions?
  • Have you checked our Microsoft 365/Google tenant for new inbox rules, forwarding, suspicious logins, or risky OAuth app consents in the last 24–48 hours?
  • What’s our process if a user reports a “verification/CAPTCHA” page asking them to run steps—who do they tell, and what gets checked?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

Several reports highlight active exploitation affecting widely deployed platforms and edge devices, including unpatched Citrix NetScaler zero-days and renewed attacks against Oracle PeopleSoft, plus warnings of exploited flaws in products such as SharePoint/WSO2/Adobe Commerce. Rather than focusing on versions, the key SME action is to confirm with your IT provider what public-facing systems you have, what’s monitored, and what emergency mitigations or temporary exposure reductions are in place.

One action today

Send a same-day staff note: do not complete any ‘verification/CAPTCHA’ steps that involve copying/pasting or running anything—close the page and report it to IT.
