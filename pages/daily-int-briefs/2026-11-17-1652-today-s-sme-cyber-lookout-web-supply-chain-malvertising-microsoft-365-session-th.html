Today’s SME cyber lookout: web supply-chain malvertising + Microsoft 365 session theft

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Tuesday 17 November 2026, 16:52 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier incident scams, web supply-chain risk, and Microsoft 365 account takeover phishing

What to look out for today

Three practical watch-outs for SMEs today:

  • Website supply-chain malware: reports of a suspected supply-chain incident involving Optimizely leading to malicious JavaScript being served on well-known sites (including major news and SaaS brands). This is a reminder that “trusted” websites can temporarily deliver harmful scripts.
  • Microsoft 365 token/session theft phishing: newer phishing kits don’t just steal passwords — they aim to steal the session so attackers can get into email even if MFA is enabled.
  • Supplier/SaaS ripple effects: a cloud-environment breach at Barracuda and a breach affecting Uber drivers tied to a third-party AI infrastructure provider show how incidents in suppliers can create knock-on risk for customers and users.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Day-to-day browsing risk: staff visiting mainstream websites for work (news, research, tools) can be exposed if a supplier’s script is compromised.
  • Email compromise is still the fastest route to fraud: if Microsoft 365 sessions are hijacked, attackers can read invoices, change payment details, create mailbox rules, and convincingly impersonate staff.
  • Third-party breaches become your problem: even if your own systems aren’t “hacked”, stolen data or supplier access keys can increase phishing, impersonation and account takeover attempts against your team.

Warning signs

  • Staff report being redirected from a normal website to an unexpected page, or seeing unusual pop-ups/“verify you’re human” prompts.
  • Microsoft 365 alerts: impossible travel, unfamiliar device sign-ins, new OAuth/app consents you don’t recognise, or unexpected MFA prompts.
  • New inbox rules forwarding email externally, sudden “read” status on messages, or missing emails around invoices/bank details.
  • Increased targeted phishing referencing real suppliers, real staff names/roles, or “recent security incidents” as a pretext.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Supply-chain browsing attacks: attackers compromise a commonly used web component and use it to inject malicious scripts that attempt to steal payment/crypto data or push further infection.
  • Session replay: phishing pages capture Microsoft 365 tokens so attackers can access mail without repeatedly prompting for MFA.
  • Incident-themed social engineering: scammers may use news of breaches (or fake breach notifications) to pressure staff into “resetting”, approving sign-ins, or paying an “urgent invoice change”.

What to do today

  • Send a short staff note: be cautious even on familiar websites today; don’t enter payment or card details after unexpected prompts; report odd redirects immediately.
  • Focus on Microsoft 365 account protection: review sign-in logs for suspicious activity; check for new inbox forwarding rules and unfamiliar app consents.
  • Reinforce payment-change controls: require call-back verification to a known number for any new bank details or “urgent” payment changes.
  • Supplier awareness: list your critical web/marketing and security suppliers (e.g., website CMS/optimisation tools, email security, MSP tools) and confirm how you’d be notified of an incident.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you quickly check for suspicious Microsoft 365 sign-ins, token-like session anomalies, and mailbox forwarding rules across the business?
  • Do we have conditional access controls appropriate to our size (e.g., blocking legacy authentication, requiring compliant devices for admin access)?
  • How do we monitor third-party script changes on our own website (especially marketing/optimisation tags) and how fast can we disable them?
  • What’s our process if a key supplier (email security, website platform, MSP tool, payroll/HR platform) suffers a breach—who contacts us, and what do we do first?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

No specific patch action is the key takeaway from today’s items. The immediate risk is account/session theft and supplier-side compromise, so prioritise monitoring (sign-ins, mailbox rules, app consents) and tightening authentication and payment-change processes.

One action today

Review Microsoft 365 for suspicious sign-ins and new mailbox forwarding rules today (and remove anything you don’t recognise).
