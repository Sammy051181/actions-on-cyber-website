Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

ASOS incident scams + fake AI “ad portals” targeting ad accounts and MFA

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Tuesday 06 October 2026, 21:58 UK time
Today’s look-out: Impersonation & credential-theft scams (brand incident lures + ad account takeovers)

What to look out for today

  • ASOS incident-related scams: emails/texts/push-style messages claiming your ASOS account needs “verification”, a “refund”, or “security reset”.
  • Fake AI advertising portals: lookalike sites pretending to be ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and similar “AI ad tools”, designed to steal ad platform logins and MFA codes.
  • Marketing account takeover attempts: attacks focused on people who manage paid ads (Google/Meta and agency logins), business pages, or billing for campaigns.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Ad account compromise can be expensive fast: attackers can run unauthorised adverts, change payment details, or lock you out of your own campaigns.
  • Brand incidents create believable lures: when a well-known retailer reports an issue, criminals quickly copy the news to trick staff and customers into handing over passwords or payment details.
  • MFA isn’t a silver bullet if you hand over codes: some phishing flows are built to capture one-time codes in real time.

Warning signs

  • Messages urging urgency: “account will be closed”, “refund pending”, “security alert – act now”.
  • Links to “ad verification”, “business account connection”, “campaign optimisation” portals you weren’t expecting.
  • A login page appearing inside what looks like a browser window, but behaviour feels odd (can’t select the real address bar, can’t open new tabs normally).
  • Unexpected MFA prompts or repeated one-time code requests.
  • New admin users, new payment methods, or sudden campaign changes you didn’t make.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Incident lures: criminals reference the ASOS news to make “account reset” or “compensation” messages feel legitimate and time-sensitive.
  • Credential + MFA harvesting: fake AI “ad tool” sites persuade ad managers to sign in, then capture passwords and one-time codes.
  • Ad account takeover: once inside, attackers can add themselves as admins, change billing details, and launch ads (often to promote more scams).

What to do today

  • Brief staff in 5 minutes: if it mentions ASOS, refunds, account security, “AI ad optimisation”, or asks for a login/MFA code—treat it as suspicious and verify via a known, saved bookmark.
  • Lock down ad/admin access: reduce who has admin rights on ad accounts; remove old agency/freelancer access you no longer need.
  • Review account security: check recent logins, admin lists, and billing/payment methods on ad platforms and key social accounts.
  • Set an internal rule: nobody shares one-time passcodes (ever) or approves unexpected sign-in prompts.
  • Customer-facing teams: be ready for callers saying they received “ASOS support” messages—advise them not to click links and to use the official app/site directly.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can we enable stronger sign-in controls for marketing/admin accounts (e.g. conditional access, restricting logins to known devices/locations where appropriate)?
  • Do we have alerting for suspicious sign-ins and newly-added admin users on Microsoft 365/Google accounts used for marketing and billing?
  • What is our process to quickly regain control if an ad account or social account is taken over (contacts, evidence needed, timelines)?
  • Can we run a quick access review to confirm who currently has admin rights to ad platforms and key SaaS tools?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

No specific patch action from today’s items for most SMEs. The immediate risk is credential theft and impersonation, so prioritise access reviews, sign-in monitoring and staff awareness rather than rushing technical changes.

One action today

Do a same-day access review of your ad accounts (Google/Meta/agency logins): confirm admins, remove anyone not needed, and check billing/payment methods for unauthorised changes.
