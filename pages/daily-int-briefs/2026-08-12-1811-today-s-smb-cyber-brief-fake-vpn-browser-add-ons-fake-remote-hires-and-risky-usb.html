Today’s SMB Cyber Brief: Fake VPN browser add‑ons, fake remote hires, and risky USB drop attacks

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Wednesday 12 August 2026, 18:11 UK time
Today’s look-out: Account takeover and endpoint compromise via “helpful” tools (VPN extensions), hiring abuse, and physical/USB intrusion

What to look out for today

  • Browser “free VPN” extensions that route your web traffic through third‑party proxies (potentially exposing logins and sensitive browsing).
  • Fake remote worker / contractor onboarding where a criminal gets a real company account and access under a false identity.
  • USB / device plug‑in attacks that trigger Windows to install risky vendor software and elevate access on a PC.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs often rely on a small number of admin staff, outsourced IT, and cloud apps for day‑to‑day work. These stories share a theme: attackers don’t need to “hack the firewall” if they can (a) trick staff into installing something, (b) get hired into your environment, or (c) touch a device briefly. Any of these can lead to email compromise, payment diversion attempts, data exposure, or ransomware disruption.

Warning signs

  • Staff installing (or requesting) free VPN/proxy browser extensions to access content or “increase privacy”.
  • New starters pushing for urgent access before checks are complete (“I can’t start work without the account today”).
  • Shipping a laptop to a new hire and then being asked to change delivery details last minute or use a different recipient name.
  • Unexpected prompts on PCs about new hardware detected or software/drivers being installed after a device was plugged in.
  • USB sticks or “lost” devices appearing in reception, car parks, meeting rooms, or posted to the office.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Malicious VPN/proxy extensions can intercept or reroute browsing, increasing the chance of credential theft and session hijack, especially for webmail, payroll, and finance systems.
  • Fake remote hires can obtain legitimate credentials, get added to Teams/SharePoint, access customer data, and quietly set up forwarding rules or persistence for later fraud.
  • USB/device attacks can be used to gain high privileges on a Windows machine, then move on to file shares and cloud tokens—often without needing to know passwords.

What to do today

  • Set (or ask IT to set) a browser extension policy: only approved extensions, block “VPN/proxy” categories unless specifically needed, and remove anything unapproved.
  • Send a short staff note: “Do not install free VPN/proxy extensions. Request approved options via IT.”
  • Tighten onboarding: no account access until identity checks are complete; least-privilege on day one; time-limited access for contractors.
  • Harden device handling: discourage unknown USB use; keep spare USB ports blocked/controlled where practical; ensure reception/mailroom know to treat unsolicited devices as suspicious.
  • Review key account protections: MFA everywhere possible, especially email, Microsoft 365/Google Workspace admin, payroll, banking portals, and remote access tools.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you report and block risky browser extensions (especially VPN/proxy) across managed devices?
  • Do we have device control in place (USB restrictions) and an alert when new drivers/software install unexpectedly?
  • What is our new-starter security workflow (identity verification steps, least privilege, conditional access, and early monitoring)?
  • Can you provide a monthly list of “newly installed” software/extensions across endpoints for review?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If your business uses SAP Commerce Cloud (including partners hosting it for you), confirm your supplier has addressed a newly reported high-severity issue and can evidence their mitigation/patch status. For most SMEs this will be a supplier assurance check rather than an internal patching task.

One action today

Message staff today: “Do not install any free VPN/proxy browser extensions or plug in unknown USB devices—raise requests via IT.”
