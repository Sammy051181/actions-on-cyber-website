Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: supplier outages, breach follow-on scams, and ageing firewalls

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Friday 20 November 2026, 16:07 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier incident scams and service disruption (MSP/email filtering) plus internet-edge risk

What to look out for today

1) Supplier outage risk for email security: N-able reports a security breach that led to a major outage affecting Mail Assure and SpamExperts Hosted Cloud services.

2) “Breach follow-on” impersonation scams: A third-party vendor breach reportedly exposed customer information linked to major payment/financial brands. Even if you aren’t a direct customer, criminals often use widely reported breaches to make phishing more believable.

3) Internet-edge exposure from ageing kit: SonicWall is warning that Gen 6 firewalls will not receive security patches, including for critical issues. Unsupported perimeter devices are a common route into SMEs.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Email disruption = business disruption: If your filtering/quarantine service is down or unstable, more spam and phishing may reach staff, and legitimate mail can be delayed.
  • Supplier incidents fuel convincing scams: Attackers reuse public news to craft emails like “service incident update”, “account revalidation”, “invoice re-send”, or “security check”.
  • Unsupported firewalls increase ransomware risk: If a perimeter device can’t be patched, the safest assumption is it will eventually be targeted.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected spikes in spam/phishing reaching inboxes, or staff reporting “quarantine digest” emails that look different.
  • Emails claiming to be from N-able/SpamExperts/Mail Assure (or your IT provider) asking you to log in, reset passwords, re-authorise, or change mail settings.
  • Customers/suppliers receiving odd “payment brand” themed messages (Visa/Mastercard/Chase-themed lures) asking for verification, refunds, chargebacks, or updated details.
  • Firewall support/renewal emails pressuring urgent action, especially if the sender wants you to install something or share remote access.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Incident-themed phishing: “We detected suspicious activity during the outage—sign in to confirm.”
  • Helpdesk/social engineering: Calling the office pretending to be IT/MSP or a vendor, using the outage/breach as justification to get password resets or MFA bypasses.
  • Perimeter targeting: Scanning for older, unsupported internet-facing firewalls and attempting compromise as a foothold into the network.
  • Credential harvesting: Reusing leaked or guessed passwords—especially where teams share credentials via spreadsheets or informal methods.

What to do today

  • Brief staff (2 minutes): “Expect more spam today. Don’t trust ‘service incident’ links. Report anything asking you to re-login or change settings.”
  • Raise monitoring for email: Ask your IT provider to watch mail flow, quarantine performance, and inbound spam volumes; agree what to do if filtering degrades.
  • Lock down payment changes: Remind finance: no bank detail changes from email alone; always call back using a known number (not one in the email).
  • Check your firewall generation/support status: If you’re on SonicWall Gen 6, treat this as a replacement planning trigger, not a “later” task.

Ask your IT provider

  • Are we using N-able Mail Assure or SpamExperts Hosted Cloud? If yes, what’s our fallback plan if service is degraded (and how will staff be told)?
  • Did we see any abnormal inbound email patterns or admin logins during the reported outage window?
  • What’s our current firewall make/model and support status? If end-of-support, what’s the replacement timeline and temporary risk reduction (e.g., exposure minimisation)?
  • Do we have enforced MFA on email/admin portals, and can we confirm conditional access / geo or risk-based controls where available?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

This isn’t a patch-today bulletin, but it is a good moment to confirm you’re not relying on unsupported perimeter devices. If any firewall or edge appliance is out of support, prioritise a replacement plan—patches can’t help when vendors stop issuing them.

One action today

Send a short internal note today: ‘We’re expecting more supplier-incident themed phishing—don’t click “mail quarantine/incident” links; report to IT, and verify any payment change by phone using a known number.’
