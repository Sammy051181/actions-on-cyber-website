Today’s SMB Cyber Brief: Gmail/Outlook email spying via fake extensions + 1Password supplier breach fallout

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Thursday 26 November 2026, 17:42 UK time
Today’s look-out: Credential theft and email access via fake browser extensions, social ads and brand-impersonation lures

What to look out for today

Watch for attempts to steal Google Workspace/Microsoft 365 logins and gain access to email content using:

  • Fake Chrome extensions that pretend to be legitimate work tools (e.g. a “Google Sheets” helper) but are designed to read or steal Gmail data.
  • Malware delivered via social media adverts (reported via Meta ads) that then forces a browser extension onto a device to spy on Gmail/Outlook.
  • Phishing invitations offering “paid expert interviews” or webinars, impersonating well-known brands, to capture Google/Okta credentials.
  • Supplier ripple effects from a reported breach at 1Password following an employee GitHub account compromise.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

Email is still the quickest route to business disruption: if an attacker gets into a mailbox, they can read invoices, reset passwords for other systems, impersonate staff, and target customers and suppliers. Browser extensions and “sign in to view” prompts can bypass normal caution because they look like everyday work tools.

If you use 1Password (or any password manager), a supplier incident can trigger follow-on scams and increased account-takeover attempts using previously stolen details or trust in the brand.

Warning signs

  • Staff report being prompted to install a Chrome extension to view a sheet, document, interview invite, or “security update”.
  • Unexpected messages about “browser management”, “your browser is now managed”, or new policies appearing in the browser.
  • Login prompts that feel “normal” but arrive via an email/webinar/interview invitation and ask for Google/Okta credentials.
  • Unusual mailbox behaviour: unexplained forwarding rules, new delegates, sent items the user didn’t send, or MFA prompts the user didn’t initiate.
  • Staff receive increased emails referencing 1Password (password resets, “security alerts”, “verify your vault”) they weren’t expecting.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Extension-led data access: a malicious extension can request broad permissions and then read email content or session data.
  • Account takeover: stolen Google/Okta/Microsoft credentials are used to access email, cloud files and admin portals; attackers then set forwarding rules to stay in the loop.
  • Payment diversion: once inside email, attackers watch for invoices and insert “bank details changed” messages at the right time.
  • Supplier-incident impersonation: criminals reuse a real breach story (e.g. 1Password) to make their phishing seem more believable.

What to do today

  • Message staff today: “Don’t install new browser extensions from links in emails/ads. If you think you need one for work, request it through IT.”
  • Check browsers for risky changes: look for newly installed extensions and any signs devices/browsers became ‘managed’ unexpectedly.
  • Harden email security basics: enforce MFA, review mailbox forwarding rules, and alert on new sign-ins from unusual locations.
  • Prepare for 1Password-themed scams: remind staff to access 1Password only via the usual app/browser route (not email links) and to report suspicious “security alert” emails.

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you block or restrict browser extensions (allow-list) on company devices, especially for Chrome/Edge?
  • Do we have monitoring for new mailbox forwarding rules, suspicious OAuth/app authorisations, and unusual sign-in patterns?
  • Are we enforcing MFA everywhere (Google Workspace/Microsoft 365/admin portals) and do we have a plan for MFA push-bombing resistance?
  • If we use 1Password, what’s our current stance: any required resets/rotations, and how do we verify we’re not responding to spoofed comms?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If you run internet-facing network/security appliances (e.g. firewalls/remote access/voice systems), ask your IT provider to confirm they’re tracking vendor advisories and CISA’s “known exploited” list—these issues are often used for rapid break-ins at the perimeter, which can lead to ransomware or email compromise in smaller organisations too.

One action today

Send a same-day staff note: do not install any new browser extensions from email links or adverts; request new extensions via IT and report any unexpected Google/Microsoft login prompts immediately.
