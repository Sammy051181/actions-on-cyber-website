Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SMB cyber lookout: Microsoft 365 session theft phishing, fake “ChatGPT” ClickFix lures, and remote-access tool risk

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Wednesday 30 September 2026, 14:41 UK time
Today’s look-out: Account takeover and remote-access footholds via phishing and ‘AI tool’ lures

What to look out for today

Expect a mix of (1) convincing Microsoft 365 login/phishing lures aimed at senior staff and finance-adjacent roles, and (2) staff being pulled to fake “AI / ChatGPT” sites via sponsored search results that try to trick them into running steps on their computer (“ClickFix”). Once attackers get a foothold, they may deploy remote monitoring/management (RMM) or remote access tooling to keep access and move quickly.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Microsoft 365 session theft can bypass password changes if attackers capture an active session, leading to mailbox access, invoice fraud, payroll diversion attempts, and data theft.
  • Remote-access tooling turns “just one click” into persistent access—often long enough to tamper with payments, harvest contacts, and spread phishing internally.
  • Fake AI tools are a realistic staff risk: people search for productivity tools, click the top sponsored result, and follow instructions without realising they’re enabling malware.

Warning signs

  • Emails prompting an urgent Microsoft 365 login, “verify your account”, “view secure document”, or “unusual sign-in” alert—especially addressed to directors/partners/senior managers.
  • Reports from staff that they were asked to copy/paste instructions, “fix” something in their browser, or run steps to access an AI/chat tool (classic ClickFix-style behaviour).
  • Unexpected new device sign-ins, unfamiliar session activity, or new mailbox rules/forwarding behaviour.
  • New remote access/RMM software appearing on endpoints without a logged IT change request.
  • Clients/suppliers receiving strange replies from your mailbox threads (conversation hijacking).

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Session theft + mailbox access: attackers read invoices, change bank details, set up forwarding, and target your suppliers/customers with believable payment-change requests.
  • Remote tooling after a phish: attackers attempt to establish persistent access, reduce reliance on stolen passwords, and expand to finance endpoints.
  • Sponsored-search “AI” lures: users land on lookalike sites and are socially engineered into taking actions that result in malware or remote access tools being installed.

What to do today

  • Send a 2-minute staff note: “Don’t trust sponsored ‘ChatGPT/AI’ ads; only use approved links/bookmarks. If a site asks you to run steps to ‘fix’ access, stop and call IT.”
  • Prioritise Microsoft 365 session protection: ensure MFA is enforced for all users (especially leadership, finance, and shared mailboxes) and review sign-in alerts.
  • Check for mailbox tampering: look for new forwarding rules, hidden inbox rules, and unexpected delegated access on key accounts.
  • Harden remote access: confirm only approved remote tools are installed and monitored; remove/disable anything not explicitly required.
  • Prep finance controls: remind staff that bank detail changes must be verified out-of-band (known phone number, not email).

Ask your IT provider

  • Can you show us recent sign-in anomalies for Microsoft 365 (impossible travel, new devices, unusual locations) for directors/finance/shared mailboxes?
  • Do we have monitoring to detect new mailbox forwarding rules and suspicious inbox rules?
  • What remote access/RMM tools are approved in our environment, and do you alert on new installations?
  • Do you block or warn on malicious/suspicious sponsored domains via web filtering/DNS protection?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

If your business uses TeamViewer (directly or via an outsourced IT provider), treat today as a prompt to confirm you’re on the latest supported client/host versions and that TeamViewer is only installed where it’s genuinely needed. Remote access products are high-value targets because they can become a direct doorway into your systems.

One action today

Send a short internal warning today: “Avoid sponsored ‘ChatGPT/AI’ ads; only use approved links. If any page asks you to run ‘fix’ steps to sign in or view a file, stop and report it.”
