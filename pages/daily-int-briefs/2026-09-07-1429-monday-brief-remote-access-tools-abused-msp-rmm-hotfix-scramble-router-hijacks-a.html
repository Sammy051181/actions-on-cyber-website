Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Monday brief: Remote access tools abused, MSP/RMM hotfix scramble, router hijacks, and breach-driven scams

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Monday 07 September 2026, 14:29 UK time
Today’s look-out: Remote access & supplier-platform abuse leading to malware, account takeover and disruption

What to look out for today

  • Remote support tool abuse: attackers are abusing remote support journeys and ScreenConnect environments to spread malware to newly connected machines.
  • MSP/RMM dependency risk: N-able N-central is issuing repeated hotfixes and reports indicate attacks are ongoing/possible. If your IT provider uses it, this is a business continuity and access-risk issue.
  • Router takeovers: MikroTik routers with internet-exposed management (e.g. SSH) are being hijacked in active exploitation, which can lead to traffic interception, redirection, or follow-on compromise.
  • Data breach ripple effects: a third-party logistics breach affecting Trezor customers and a large breach at the Mathspace learning platform increase the chances of convincing phishing, fake support calls, and “update your details” scams.
  • Session-cookie/account takeover risk: malware that steals session cookies can bypass normal sign-in prompts for services like Google accounts, increasing the impact of a single infected device.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs often rely on remote support, an outsourced IT provider/MSP, and a small number of core internet services (email, accounting, payroll, file sharing). When attackers abuse remote access tools or compromise network edge devices (routers), the result is often rapid: password resets, email compromise, invoice fraud, and downtime. Separately, large supplier breaches commonly trigger highly targeted phishing against staff and customers.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected prompts to install “support”, “remote access”, or “security update” software (especially after a call or pop-up claiming urgent help is needed).
  • New or unfamiliar ScreenConnect/remote support sessions, or devices showing remote control when nobody requested it.
  • Staff reporting browser sessions that “look logged in already” or security alerts about unusual sign-ins/forwarding rules.
  • Internet issues that feel like misrouting: strange certificate warnings, being redirected to odd websites, or multiple users seeing login pages change.
  • Phishing emails referencing real supplier names (shipping/logistics providers, education platforms) asking to “confirm address”, “pay a fee”, or “verify account”.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Tech-support scams → remote tool install: a convincing call or pop-up persuades a user to allow remote access, then the attacker pivots to other machines and accounts.
  • Abuse of remote access platforms: if ScreenConnect instances or clients are targeted, attackers may spread malware to additional hosts that connect.
  • MSP tool compromise: if an RMM platform used by an IT provider is exposed/impacted, attackers may gain broad access across many customer networks.
  • Router hijack: taking control of a router can enable interception, redirection, or setting up onward access to internal systems.
  • Cookie theft: malware on a PC can steal session cookies, allowing access to cloud services without needing to re-enter passwords or complete MFA in the normal way.

What to do today

  • Re-brief staff (2 minutes): “No unsolicited support calls. No installing remote tools from a link. If in doubt, hang up and call our known IT number.”
  • Check remote access hygiene: confirm who is allowed to run remote support tools and ensure staff can’t approve remote control without a clear internal request.
  • Ask your IT provider if you’re exposed: specifically whether ScreenConnect and/or N-able N-central are used anywhere in your environment and what mitigations are in place.
  • Router sanity check: confirm your router management interfaces are not exposed to the internet and that administration is restricted to known locations/devices.
  • Raise the bar on email fraud: for any change of bank details or urgent payment request, use call-back to a known number and a second-person approval.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we use ScreenConnect anywhere (directly or via a supplier)? If yes, what controls prevent unauthorised sessions and how are suspicious connections detected?
  • Do you use N-able N-central to manage our systems? If yes, what is your current hotfix/mitigation status and how are you monitoring for misuse?
  • Are any router management services (SSH/web admin) exposed to the internet? If not sure, can you provide evidence (not just “should be fine”)?
  • What alerts do we have for new email forwarding rules, unusual login locations, or repeated failed sign-ins?
  • What is our playbook if a remote access tool is abused (immediate isolation steps, password resets, customer comms)?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

ConnectWise has issued temporary mitigations for a new ScreenConnect issue awaiting a patch, and N-able has released additional N-central hotfixes in quick succession; if your IT provider runs these tools, ask them to confirm mitigations/hotfixes are applied and that monitoring is in place for suspicious remote access.

One action today

Send a short staff note today: no one should install or approve remote support tools/sessions unless they initiated the request via your known IT contact method.
