Daily SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief

Today’s SME cyber lookout: tampered downloads, rogue apps, and active web/perimeter attacks

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Friday 27 November 2026, 17:26 UK time
Today’s look-out: Supplier and software supply-chain risk (trojanised installers/updates) plus active compromise of web and edge systems

What to look out for today

  • Trojanised “official” software downloads and updates (Q-Dir and DesktopOK installers reportedly tampered with on the vendor’s own website, including via the update process).
  • Rogue / abused app delivery where a Microsoft Store app was used as part of a chain to deliver a remote access trojan (RAT).
  • Active attacks on common business web and perimeter systems, including Drupal website takeovers and targeted exploitation of internet-exposed firewall management interfaces.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

SMEs often rely on “trusted” sources (vendor sites, app stores, auto-updaters) and a small number of key systems (website CMS, firewall/VPN). When those trust points are abused, a single employee install or a single exposed admin interface can lead to business-wide disruption: stolen credentials, remote control of devices, data access, and knock-on fraud (invoice/payment diversion) even without ransomware.

Warning signs

  • Staff reporting unexpected security prompts, new admin pop-ups, or tools asking for elevated permissions “to update”.
  • AV/EDR alerts after installing or updating everyday utilities (especially file managers, desktop tools, “snipping”/productivity utilities).
  • Unusual remote-access behaviour: new remote-control software appearing, unknown services starting, or devices contacting unfamiliar domains.
  • Website symptoms: unexpected redirects, new admin accounts, altered pages, or customers reporting strange behaviour.
  • Firewall/VPN symptoms: configuration changes you didn’t make, new admin users, or management access enabled/exposed unexpectedly.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Supply-chain tampering: attackers modify installers/updates so the victim installs the malware themselves, bypassing many “don’t click links” habits.
  • Trojan delivery via trusted branding: users are more likely to run software that looks signed/legitimate or comes via an app ecosystem, then the malware provides remote control.
  • Internet-exposed admin interfaces: attackers scan for reachable management portals (websites, firewalls) and attempt takeover, leading to persistence and data access.

What to do today

  • Send a short staff note: only install software your business has approved; avoid “handy utilities” found via search results; report any update prompt that looks unusual.
  • Review endpoints for recent installs (last 7 days): flag new “desktop utilities”, “snipping tools”, remote admin tools, or anything installed outside your normal process.
  • Check your website and admin accounts: confirm CMS admin users are expected, MFA is enabled, and the site hasn’t been modified.
  • Confirm firewall/VPN management access is not exposed to the internet and that MFA/logging is enabled for administration.
  • Be extra cautious with payment change requests over the next few days (a common follow-on after device or email access): verify changes out-of-band.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we have an approved software list and a controlled way for staff to request/install tools?
  • Can you audit recent software installations across our devices and flag anything unusual?
  • Is our EDR/antivirus configured to alert on suspicious installers, persistence, and remote admin tools?
  • Is our firewall/VPN management interface restricted (not internet-exposed), protected by MFA, and monitored?
  • For our website/CMS, do we have monitoring and quick restore (known-good backups) if a takeover happens?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

There are reports of active attacks against Drupal sites and against certain firewall platforms where management interfaces are exposed. If you run your own website or manage your own firewall/VPN, treat this as a prompt to urgently confirm you’re on supported versions, administration is locked down, and your IT provider is actively monitoring vendor advisories and applying fixes promptly.

One action today

Send a same-day staff message: “Do not install or update non-approved utilities; if you see an unexpected update/install prompt, stop and report it to IT immediately.”
