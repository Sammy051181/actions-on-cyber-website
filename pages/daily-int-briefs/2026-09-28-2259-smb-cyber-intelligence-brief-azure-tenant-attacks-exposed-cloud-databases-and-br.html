SMB Cyber Intelligence Brief: Azure tenant attacks, exposed cloud databases, and breach-driven phishing

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Monday 28 September 2026, 22:59 UK time
Today’s look-out: Cloud account takeover & data exposure (Azure / SaaS misconfig) plus breach-themed phishing

What to look out for today

Today’s key theme for smaller organisations is cloud account takeover and data exposure, especially where you rely on Microsoft Azure, cloud-hosted apps, or managed service providers.

  • Azure tenant attacks with destructive impact: reporting describes ransomware operators targeting Azure tenants, stealing credentials and destroying core cloud components.
  • Publicly exposed cloud databases: researchers found large numbers of misconfigured Supabase databases exposing personal data, passwords, or authentication tokens.
  • Breach-and-incident news driving scams: confirmed data breach at a car-sharing service and a ransomware disruption at a major transport operator are the sort of headlines criminals reuse in phishing (“urgent security update”, “account verification”, “refund”, “missed payment”).
  • Supply-chain / third-party security tooling risk: a major crypto exchange said an attacker got in via a flaw in a third-party security product and then used high-level credentials to issue fraudulent actions.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

  • Cloud is your office now: if your identity platform or cloud tenant is compromised, it can stop email, files, line-of-business systems, and remote working.
  • Destruction hurts more than encryption: attacks that delete resources or break cloud foundations can create longer outages than “classic” ransomware.
  • Misconfiguration is a common SME failure mode: exposed databases and overly-permissive access are often accidental and can go unnoticed until someone misuses the data.
  • Incidents create cover for fraud: criminals time payment scams and credential theft around widely reported breaches and disruptions.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected MFA prompts, password reset emails, or “your account was accessed” alerts.
  • New or changed admin accounts, unfamiliar “break-glass” users, or role changes you can’t explain.
  • Sudden loss of access to cloud resources, missing subscriptions/resources, or unusual configuration changes.
  • Staff receiving emails/texts about urgent security checks, “download this update”, “verify your Microsoft account”, or “new payment details”.
  • Customer/supplier complaints about spam from your domain, or unusual sign-in locations for staff.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Credential-led cloud takeover: steal or trick users into handing over credentials, then move into cloud admin functions.
  • Abuse of tokens and secrets: if a database or app store exposes authentication tokens/secrets, attackers may log in without needing a password.
  • Destructive disruption: once inside a tenant, attackers may disable security controls, remove resources, or break recovery paths before demanding payment.
  • Third-party dependency weakness: compromise via a supplier product or integration, then use high-privilege credentials to perform fraudulent actions.
  • Incident-themed phishing: “we were affected by X breach—confirm your details”, “new bank account due to disruption”, “refund/chargeback required”.

What to do today

  • Reconfirm who has admin access to Microsoft 365/Azure and remove anything not required.
  • Turn on and review sign-in alerts (especially for admin accounts): look for impossible travel, unusual locations, and repeated failed logins.
  • Check cloud backups and recovery: ensure you can restore key systems and that recovery access is protected (separate admin, strong MFA).
  • Run a quick “exposed data” sanity check on any customer-facing apps/portals: verify databases aren’t publicly readable and that secrets/tokens aren’t stored in the open.
  • Brief staff in two minutes: no payment detail changes by email alone; verify via a known phone number; treat “security verification” links as suspicious.

Ask your IT provider

  • “If our Microsoft/Azure tenant was hit, what would you check first, and how quickly would you know something was being deleted or disabled?”
  • “Do we have separate admin accounts and strong MFA for them, and are admin actions logged and monitored?”
  • “Are our cloud backups protected from tampering (separate credentials, immutable options where available), and when did we last test a restore?”
  • “Do we have an inventory of third-party integrations (security tools, connectors, single sign-on apps) and who reviews their permissions?”
  • “For our web apps/databases, what’s our process to prevent misconfigurations exposing data (reviews, automated checks, alerts)?”

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

No specific patch action is the main takeaway from today’s items. The priority is identity protection, least-privilege admin access, and monitoring for unusual cloud changes, because credential misuse and misconfiguration are recurring routes into SME environments.

One action today

Review and reduce Microsoft/Azure admin access today (confirm every admin is required, uses strong MFA, and has sign-in alerts enabled).
