Today’s SMB Cyber Brief: fake AI tool downloads via Google Ads, plus remote access gear being actively targeted

What small and medium-sized businesses should look out for today.

High Saturday 10 October 2026, 21:03 UK time
Today’s look-out: Malvertising and remote access compromise (supplier/MSP risk)

What to look out for today

  • Google Ads / search-ad scams using legitimate-looking redirects to send people to fake “Claude” installers that deliver so-called ClickFix style attacks.
  • Remote access appliances being actively exploited: SonicWall SMA1000 devices are being targeted in the wild.
  • Ripple effects from high-profile extortion crews: arrests linked to ShinyHunters are in the news, a reminder that extortion-and-leak tactics remain common and opportunistic.

Why this matters to smaller businesses

Smaller organisations are often hit through everyday business behaviour: staff searching for software, downloading “installers”, and trusting sponsored search results. Separately, many SMEs rely on remote access (either directly or via an outsourced IT provider/MSP). If remote access infrastructure is compromised, it can lead to ransomware, data theft, invoice fraud, and prolonged downtime.

Warning signs

  • Staff report being prompted to “fix” something during an install, or being told to take unusual steps to complete a download.
  • A “Claude” (or other popular AI tool) download page that looks slightly off: odd spelling, extra steps, unexpected pop-ups, or it isn’t clearly the official vendor site.
  • Unusual remote access behaviour: unexpected login prompts, failed logins, new devices in security logs, or staff locked out of remote access/VPN tools.
  • Sudden security tool alerts around browser activity, ad-clicks, or new software appearing on machines without an IT ticket.

How attackers may exploit the situation

  • Malvertising: attackers place ads that look legitimate, then use redirects to land victims on convincing fake download pages.
  • “ClickFix” social engineering: victims are nudged into completing steps that result in malicious changes or execution, under the pretence of fixing an issue.
  • Remote access takeover: if an edge device (used for remote access) is vulnerable, attackers may gain a foothold to move into internal systems, steal data, and disrupt operations.

What to do today

  • Warn staff: do not install AI tools (or any software) from sponsored ads. Only use known, bookmarked vendor pages or your internal software portal.
  • Lock down installs: ensure standard users cannot install software without approval, and review any recent installs on shared/admin machines.
  • Review remote access exposure: confirm what remote access products are in use (including via your IT provider) and that logs/alerts are being monitored.
  • Prepare for disruption: make sure you can contact your IT provider out-of-band (phone number held offline) and that you have a tested process for isolating affected PCs.

Ask your IT provider

  • Do we (or you, on our behalf) use SonicWall SMA1000 anywhere in the supply chain? If yes, what is the current risk status and what extra monitoring is in place?
  • What controls stop staff installing unapproved software from the web (application control, least privilege, device management)?
  • Are you monitoring for unusual remote access logins and new device sessions, and how quickly are we notified?
  • Can you provide a list of our externally exposed services (remote access portals, VPNs) and who owns patching/monitoring for each?

Patch watch - only one short paragraph, and only if relevant

SonicWall SMA1000 has a recently patched issue that is reported as actively exploited. If your business (or your MSP) uses SMA1000 for remote access, treat this as urgent: confirm patching status and increased monitoring, because edge-device compromise is a common route to business-wide disruption.

One action today

Send a same-day staff note: “Do not download or install AI tools (e.g., Claude) via Google Ads/sponsored results—only use our approved links or request via IT.”
